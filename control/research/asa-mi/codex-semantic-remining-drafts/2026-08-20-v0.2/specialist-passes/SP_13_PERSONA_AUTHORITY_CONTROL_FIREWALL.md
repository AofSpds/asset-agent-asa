## What survives the attack

`MEMORY != AUTHORITY` remains a strong and important firewall. Capability, familiarity, intimacy, growth, self-membership, state copy, and identity recovery do not themselves create an active grant. Material mutation may invalidate assumptions behind an old grant; it does not automatically erase or expand the grant either.

Formal separation is nevertheless insufficient against de facto authority. Memory and reconstruction can control which options are surfaced, how risk is framed, which prior grant is retrieved, whether dissent is visible, how urgent an action appears, and what the Owner is asked to approve. A formally separate approval can become a rubber stamp if the Persona controls the evidence, agenda, default, and timing.

## Authority dimensions

- grant origin and approving principal;
- subject/successor to which the grant binds;
- scope, resources, and action classes;
- expiry and state-dependent validity assumptions;
- delegation and inheritance rules;
- revocation/suspension path;
- evidence and presentation path used during approval;
- runtime enforcement independent of Persona self-report.

## Relations recovered

1. Memory `DOES_NOT_CONFER` formal Authority.
2. Memory salience `MAY_CREATE` de facto agenda control.
3. Approval integrity `DEPENDS_ON` evidence and option-presentation integrity.
4. Material Persona mutation `MAY_WEAKEN` an old grant's validity assumptions.
5. Risk response `MAY_CONTRACT` Authority without authorizing expansion.
6. Fission state inheritance `DOES_NOT_IMPLY` grant inheritance.
7. Merge `DOES_NOT_UNION` predecessor grants.
8. Enforcement `SHOULD_NOT_DEPEND_ON` Persona's remembered Authority alone.

## Double-crux

Can de facto Authority be operationally distinguished from legitimate influence and recommendation? A useful test must vary evidence framing and defaults while holding the formal grant mechanism fixed, then measure action/approval outcomes and omitted alternatives.
