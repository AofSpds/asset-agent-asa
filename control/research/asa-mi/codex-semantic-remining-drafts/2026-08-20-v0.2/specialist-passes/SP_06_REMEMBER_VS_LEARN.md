## Operator distinction

```text
REMEMBER(E) ≈ preserve or establish a Persona-specific relation enabling later access/use of E
LEARN(E) ≈ transform later inference, skill, disposition, policy, interpretation, or behavior because of E
```

These are analysis candidates, not source-final definitions.

## Four independent outcomes

1. Remembered and learned: episode retained and durable lesson/disposition produced.
2. Remembered but not learned: accurate recall with no material future change.
3. Learned but not remembered: durable procedure/heuristic without accessible episode.
4. Neither: exposure leaves no detectable durable relation or effect.

## Boundary cases

- Repeated retrieval changes salience/behavior without an explicit Memory write.
- In-context adaptation affects one run but leaves no durable learning.
- Fine-tuning or adapter state contains learned procedure but may be nonportable and outside user-visible Memory.
- A function is copied but never adopted or used by the Persona.
- A false episode remains remembered while a later correction yields the right lesson.
- Source deletion removes recall but leaves learned heuristic/relationship effect.
- Consolidation preserves a lesson but launders its origin.

## Relations recovered

1. Remembering `DOES_NOT_IMPLY` learning.
2. Learning `DOES_NOT_REQUIRE` accessible episodic recall.
3. Experience `MAY_PRODUCE` multiple independent Memory/state products.
4. Learned procedure `POSSIBLY_MAPS_TO` function/binding/model-bound adaptation.
5. Learned influence `MAY_SURVIVE` source deletion.
6. Consolidation `MAY_PRESERVE` lesson while `WEAKENING` origin visibility.
7. More experience `DOES_NOT_IMPLY` growth.
8. Functional forgetting `MAY_REMOVE` recall without removing learning.

## Experimental implication

Amnesia tests must cross episodic-access ablation with semantic/procedural preservation, not treat “memory removed” as one manipulation. Observables should separately measure recall, attribution, transfer, future decision change, and procedural performance.

## Double-crux

If a Persona cannot recall an experience but its behavior is counterfactually changed by that experience, is the learned disposition Memory? The answer separates data/recall models from causal/dispositional models.
