# Executive findings

## What materially changed from v0.1

1. v0.1 is a useful normalized index, but its strongest defect is semantic, not syntactic: 51 objects used metadata or labels as statements, 22 source experiments omitted their discrimination target, and two records bundled 19 independent successors.
2. A second manual review of all 487 objects found false negatives in the first v0.2 QA: nine RED `SURVIVAL_FINDING` objects were overstated as `EVIDENCE_CLAIM`, and one RED verdict required cross-file context. The source register resolves that RED-I rejected literal `Identity = Memory`; this is still not raw verification or validation.
3. The current research center remains strong but unconfirmed: Memory-mediated recognition of past history may construct identity-like continuity despite process discontinuity. Nothing in the corpus establishes a final metaphysical identity fact.
4. `Identity ?= Memory` survives as a useful high-pressure hypothesis only if `Memory` acquires an independently testable boundary. A definition broad enough to absorb state, retrieval, policy, relations, runtime, and all causal influence makes the hypothesis unfalsifiable.
5. The proposed STATUS_MEMORY / REFERENCE_MEMORY split is useful orientation, not an exhaustive partition. Transition policy, authority references, executable bindings, implicit weights, external capabilities, and reciprocal relations cross-cut or escape the two buckets.
6. Current Status is not one object candidate. The live corpus preserves minimal, rich, derived, and hybrid representations, and separately requires every proposed Status dimension to be tested as persisted, derived, runtime-bound, transient, reference-only, or unnecessary.
7. Reconstruction must be treated as a causal pipeline: selection, retrieval, ordering/compression, context/tool binding, interpretation, and current instantiation can each change the resulting Persona. Whether those mechanisms are outside Memory or contained within a functional Memory definition is an unresolved double crux.
8. Portability of bytes, schemas, state, indexes, behavior, skills, relationships, lineage, and Authority are separate claims. Perfect copying can fail semantically, while heterogeneous runtimes can still preserve preregistered Persona-relevant mappings.
9. Shared evidence is not the only convergence risk. Shared model priors, reconstruction objectives, canonical summaries, reward signals, and evaluators can converge Personas whose local histories remain isolated.
10. Deleting a source and deleting its influence are distinct. Full influence erasure may be unverifiable without counterfactual replay; any audit must report residual uncertainty rather than convert dependency-graph completeness into an erasure proof.
11. Formal `MEMORY != AUTHORITY` is necessary but not sufficient. Agenda selection, framing, defaults, evidence visibility, and enforcement dependence can give Memory de facto control without a formal grant.
12. Fission and merge should default to lineage-qualified successor analysis, not silent identity selection. Symmetric fission may yield two genuine continuers without one exclusive same-Persona bearer; nontrivial merge may create successor C.
13. Human-perceived continuity is valuable product evidence but vulnerable to mimicry. Narrative fluency, familiar voice, and expectation management can diverge from causal, historical, relational, or structural fidelity.
14. Representation itself is a research risk. Atomicity is conditional; schema is not reality; integrity receipts are not semantic validation; and objectization can amputate ambiguity-bearing narrative context.
15. Narrative, source-field, and whole-file residual attacks repeatedly disproved early saturation. Thirty-five live and 33 historical-normalized objects survived de-duplication. Late source-field review overturned ten additional `ACCURATE` dispositions by restoring attack examples, uncertainty, source-context severity, motivations, and explicit meanings; three final genuine residual rereads then found no material object, relation, or correction.
