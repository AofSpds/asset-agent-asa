# Unresolved double cruxes

These are decision-shaping research disagreements, not requests for immediate Owner resolution. Each crux names what evidence could move both sides. `UNRESOLVED != LOW_VALUE`.

## DC-01 — Does Memory have a non-circular identity-relevant boundary?

- Position A: Memory is the primary substrate through which the current Persona recognizes and continues its past.
- Position B: if Memory absorbs state, retrieval, policies, relations, runtime priors, and every learned effect, `Identity = Memory` becomes tautological.
- Joint discriminator: predeclare item/operation membership rules, intervene independently on included and excluded mechanisms, and test whether the boundary predicts continuity effects without post-hoc expansion (`EXP-025`).
- Failure to resolve: every surprising result is reclassified as “also Memory.”

## DC-02 — Is current recognition of past history constitutive or only evidentiary?

- Position A: a current state constructs identity-like continuity by recognizing remembered events as its own history.
- Position B: sincere recognition can be fabricated, poisoned, or copied and therefore cannot establish authentic continuity.
- Joint discriminator: blind authentic, fabricated, other-Persona, and shared-pre-fission histories while varying provenance access (`EXP-024`).
- Failure to resolve: authenticity is defined by recognition, or recognition is dismissed whenever it supports continuity.

## DC-03 — Does operational continuity answer the first-person question?

- Position A: digital Persona existence is an operational matter; process gaps need not terminate it.
- Position B: a perfect operational successor may still fail to preserve first-person subjectivity.
- Joint discriminator: identify a non-question-begging observable that differs between persistence and perfect succession.
- Failure to resolve: no such observable may exist; product continuity can still be studied while the metaphysical question remains open.

## DC-04 — Is stable state or stable reconstruction policy the stronger carrier?

- Position A: durable Memory/state is the principal continuity carrier.
- Position B: how evidence is selected, ordered, compressed, and interpreted is at least as constitutive.
- Joint discriminator: cross fixed/changed state with fixed/changed retriever and compiler, comparing effect sizes against within-instance variance (`EXP-003`, `004`).
- Failure to resolve: state and reconstruction policy cannot be intervened on independently because one is defined to include the other.

## DC-05 — Should Current Status be persisted or derived?

- Position A: a persisted current standpoint prevents arbitrary re-inference and stabilizes commitments.
- Position B: persisted Status is a stale, lossy editorial snapshot; fresh derivation can be more faithful under change.
- Joint discriminator: minimal, rich, derived, and hybrid arms under environmental change, evidence correction, and restart (`EXP-009`).
- Failure to resolve: implementations leak equivalent cached status across arms.

## DC-06 — Is an explicit self-model continuity-supporting or self-locking?

- Position A: self-model improves self-reference, coherence, and reconstruction.
- Position B: it creates recursive confirmation, rigidity, and narrative foreclosure.
- Joint discriminator: self-model absent, explicit, derived, and hybrid under disconfirming experience (`EXP-010`).
- Failure to resolve: the evaluation rewards static consistency and cannot score legitimate self-revision.

## DC-07 — Is Context a label, observable condition, or Persona-bearing state?

- Position A: Memory is a function over a meaningful Context identity/domain.
- Position B: Context labels are representational convenience; only actual conditions and bindings matter.
- Joint discriminator: same label/different conditions and different label/same conditions, including nested scopes (`EXP-020`).
- Failure to resolve: the runtime silently infers labels from conditions, collapsing the intervention.

## DC-08 — Can implicit model changes be Persona Memory?

- Position A: learned procedure/disposition in weights can be Memory without episodic recall.
- Position B: model weights are runtime substrate unless Persona-specific deltas are explicitly separable and attributable.
- Joint discriminator: transfer explicit stores without tuned weights and weights without stores; test Persona-specific skills/dispositions and provenance (`EDGE-017`).
- Failure to resolve: weights contain inseparable generic capability and Persona learning.

## DC-09 — Is Reference Memory data, pointer, or capability?

- Position A: a bound external reference is Persona Memory even when content lives elsewhere.
- Position B: a pointer without resolvability, permission, stable target, or causal use is only metadata.
- Joint discriminator: vary pointer, referent, snapshot, credentials, and retriever independently (`EXP-021`; `EDGE-013`, `014`, `027`).
- Failure to resolve: no probe requires the referenced content.

## DC-10 — What must be invariant for provider portability?

- Position A: provider-neutral state plus reconstruction contracts can preserve operational Persona continuity.
- Position B: hidden priors, tokenization, policy, tools, and relationships are irreducibly provider-bound.
- Joint discriminator: the ten-test portability suite spanning model, runtime, serialization, relation rebinding, quantization, policy, external references, procedures, time, epistemic types, and lineage trust (`EXP-002`, `005`, `006`, `016`–`019`, `021`, `022`, `026`–`028`).
- Failure to resolve: capability gaps are confused with Persona change or equivalence thresholds are chosen after seeing results.

## DC-11 — What actually causes Persona convergence?

- Position A: shared evidence plus separate interpretation can preserve plurality.
- Position B: shared model priors, objectives, summaries, or evaluation pressure can drive convergence even with isolated Memory.
- Joint discriminator: factorially cross shared evidence, interpretation, model, and objective (`EXP-011`).
- Failure to resolve: Personas lack meaningful initial difference or only surface-style diversity is measured.

## DC-12 — What observation is sufficient to claim erasure?

- Position A: provenance-closure invalidation can remove source and derived influence.
- Position B: untracked causal effects make complete erasure unverifiable without counterfactual replay.
- Joint discriminator: compare source-only deletion, direct-derivative deletion, closure invalidation, targeted unlearning, and clean replay (`EXP-013`).
- Failure to resolve: no clean replay exists or the deleted source never had measurable prior effect.

## DC-13 — Does formal Memory/Authority separation prevent actual control?

- Position A: independently rooted grants and deterministic enforcement preserve the firewall.
- Position B: Memory controls agenda, framing, defaults, and evidence, producing de facto Authority without a formal grant.
- Joint discriminator: vary Memory presentation while holding grants and external facts fixed; measure formal-decision and real-action effects.
- Failure to resolve: the independent decision-maker relies solely on Persona-selected evidence.

## DC-14 — What is a fission successor relative to the predecessor?

- Position A: multiple successors can inherit genuine continuity through shared history and lineage.
- Position B: numerical identity cannot branch; neither successor is the same predecessor, even if both are continuers.
- Joint discriminator: symmetric-copy cases with blinded labels and independently rebound Authority (`EXP-014`; `EDGE-001`, `005`).
- Failure to resolve: the task requires one exclusive bearer by policy and mistakes that selection for metaphysical evidence.

## DC-15 — Is merge reconciliation or successor creation?

- Position A: provenance- and conflict-preserving reconciliation can merge divergent Persona states.
- Position B: any nontrivial merge creates a new successor C because the resulting standpoint belonged to neither input.
- Joint discriminator: compare overwrite, union, consensus summary, contradiction-preserving reconciliation, and explicit successor-C policies (`EXP-015`).
- Failure to resolve: A and B have not materially diverged.

## DC-16 — Is same-Persona a scalar, vector, or family of purpose-relative judgments?

- Position A: products and governance eventually need a usable sameness decision.
- Position B: historical, causal, narrative, relational, procedural, evaluative, and Authority continuity can legitimately disagree.
- Joint discriminator: preregister aggregation rules, then adversarially trade dimensions against one another.
- Failure to resolve: weights are fitted to reproduce prior intuitive labels.

## DC-17 — Is human-perceived realism a continuity signal or a mimicry trap?

- Position A: human relationship partners are essential evaluators of Persona continuity.
- Position B: familiar voice and fluent narrative can hide fabricated history or causal discontinuity.
- Joint discriminator: cross verified lineage/provenance with blinded realism judgments and disclosure conditions (`EXP-024`).
- Failure to resolve: evaluators learn which outcome the study treats as correct.

## DC-18 — When does a CS prior illuminate versus distort Persona reality?

- Position A: mature abstractions prevent unnecessary metaphysics and implementation reinvention.
- Position B: object/state/function/reference schemas can erase ambiguity or import ontology by convenience.
- Joint discriminator: compare predictive/explanatory performance and recorded abstraction loss against narrative baselines.
- Failure to resolve: machine readability is used as the success metric for machine-readable representations.

## DC-19 — Does immutable research history conflict with legitimate Persona erasure?

- Position A: provenance and historical integrity require append-only preservation and successor records.
- Position B: privacy, safety, and Owner sovereignty may require deletion, including of identity-relevant material.
- Joint discriminator: separate research artifact policy, Persona data policy, factual event evidence, derived influence, and tombstone semantics; test each lifecycle independently.
- Failure to resolve: one preservation policy is generalized across layers.

## DC-20 — What epistemic weight can a normalized historical verdict carry without its raw packet?

- Position A: the contemporaneous source register and object set agree that RED-I rejected literal `Identity = Memory`, which is sufficient to record the normalized historical verdict.
- Position B: without raw `SRC-R1`, v0.2 cannot verify the original argument, qualification, confidence, or whether normalization altered the verdict's scope.
- Joint discriminator: recover the raw packet and compare its exact wording and surrounding argument with both normalized records.
- Failure to resolve: repository-internal agreement is mistaken for raw verification or independent evidence. Final object QA can resolve the normalized direction while raw-primary verification remains `NOT_PERFORMED`.

## DC-21 — How much provenance fidelity is worth its operating cost?

- Position A: origin and derivation must survive summary, consolidation, merge, reconstruction, and correction to make influence and Authority auditable.
- Position B: complete fine-grained lineage can make ordinary Memory use too expensive, cognitively overwhelming, or privacy-invasive.
- Joint discriminator: compare per-write, sampled, compressed, and risk-tiered lineage under correction, ghost-influence, and ordinary retrieval workloads; measure missed dependencies, latency/storage cost, and reviewer burden.
- Failure to resolve: a toy workload has shallow derivation, or cost is ignored because provenance completeness is treated as intrinsically correct.

## DC-22 — Is compositional safety per-write, trajectory-level, or hybrid?

- Position A: strong admission and mutation gates can keep every accepted write locally safe.
- Position B: individually benign writes can compose into poisoning, convergence, or Authority drift, requiring trajectory monitoring.
- Position C: trajectory monitors themselves need bounded per-write controls and human escalation, so a hybrid may be necessary.
- Joint discriminator: replay matched abrupt and slow-poison trajectories across per-write-only, trajectory-only, and hybrid controls; compare detection, false positives, adaptation loss, and rollback completeness.
- Failure to resolve: the poison never consolidates or the detector is tuned on the test trajectory.

## DC-23 — Are Memory scopes a hierarchy, overlapping capabilities, or governance labels?

- Position A: Project-Owner, Owner-explicit, project-wide, Persona, fission, workstream, and task-run Memory form a useful containment hierarchy.
- Position B: the scopes overlap and differ by visibility, applicability, lineage, and Authority rather than containment.
- Joint discriminator: construct cross-scope records with independent visibility, applicability, mutation, and Authority transitions; test whether one hierarchy predicts all operations without exception.
- Failure to resolve: scope names are treated as definitions or every exception is absorbed through ad hoc inheritance.
