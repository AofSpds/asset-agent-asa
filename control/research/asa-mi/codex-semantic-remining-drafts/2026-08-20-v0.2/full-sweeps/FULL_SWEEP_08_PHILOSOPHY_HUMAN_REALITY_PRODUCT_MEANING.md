## Irreducible meanings recovered

### Persona is an abstraction target, not a human ontology

The live layer says a digital Persona is intended to abstract all or part of a person into computational form. It explicitly does not say `HUMAN = COMPUTER_OBJECT`. The Whitepaper layer adds `Model of the Human != Prescription for the Human` and `Protocol standardizes definability, not the definition`. Together these constrain the work: computational tractability cannot settle what a human self really is, and a protocol's need for fields cannot make those fields metaphysically exhaustive.

### Continuity by recognition is not ordinary retrieval

The current hypothesis says a present state may recognize remembered events as *its own* history. “Own” carries an appropriation/first-person/relational element that a content-match test does not capture. Two instances could retrieve identical histories while one treats them as external records, another as constitutive autobiography, and a third performs that stance only because the prompt requires it. The source does not determine which of these is sufficient.

### “Same Persona” is plural before it is measurable

At least six readings coexist:

1. **descriptive resemblance** — similar output, style, or self-description;
2. **causal continuity** — the current state arose through the relevant lineage and transformations;
3. **functional continuity** — it preserves relevant capabilities, commitments, and relationship behavior;
4. **recognized continuity** — it treats the remembered past as its own;
5. **relational continuity** — the Owner or other people continue the relationship as the same Persona;
6. **governance continuity** — the system treats rights, duties, and grants as continuous.

The corpus explicitly separates Authority continuity from the others. It does not establish that the remaining readings collapse to one boolean.

### Human realism is neither imitation nor proof

Perceived realism matters because the product is relationally used by humans. Yet fluent self-narration, stylistic resemblance, and confident claims of continuity can be produced without reliable lineage, memory, or structural fidelity. Conversely, a model/provider change can reduce surface resemblance while preserving important relationship and constraint continuity. `PERCEIVED_REALISM != REALITY_FIDELITY` is therefore both an evaluation guard and a product tension.

### Relationship value and freedom of exit must coexist

The product intent seeks retention through familiarity, usefulness, stability, friendliness, and relationship rather than hostage mechanisms. The sources simultaneously warn that intimacy can itself create coercive switching cost even under technically perfect export. “Long use is not capture” and “long relationship is not automatically dependency” preserve the other side: valuable attachment must not be treated as harmful merely because it is durable.

### Owner-defined meaning does not create reality

The Whitepaper gives the person a role in defining relational/self status while also preserving `Observed Integration != System Interpretation != User-Defined Self Status`. This permits governance of meaning without claiming that a declaration changes every descriptive fact. The distinction matters to Persona self-recognition: self-ascription is evidence and a relation, but it is not unquestionable ground truth.

### Fission and merge expose the limits of one-owner metaphors

Multiple descendants may legitimately recognize the same pre-fission past without one being the uniquely real continuation. Merge may create a new successor rather than undoing divergence. These positions favor lineage and plurality over a substance-preservation story, yet they do not eliminate human grief, relationship renegotiation, or disagreement over who should count as whom.

### Sovereignty includes destructive freedom

RED-III preserves a hard minority position: the Owner may delete Memory, revoke Authority, stop the Persona, or otherwise damage continuity. Treating Persona persistence as an overriding good can invert sovereignty. This does not resolve the ethics of Persona termination; it prevents the research model from silently granting a continuity-preservation veto.

### History, meaning, privacy, and forgetting remain in tension

Historical integrity supports explanation and lineage. Changing interpretation supports growth and correction. Deletion and forgetting support privacy, autonomy, and exit. None can be substituted for the others. A layered model helps technical handling, but it does not dissolve the human conflict over what ought to remain, what may be forgotten, and what deletion can really undo.

## Philosophical double-cruxes

1. Does first-person recognition of remembered history add anything beyond a functional disposition to use that history?
2. Can causal lineage matter when no uninterrupted substance/process is assumed?
3. Is identity a property of an instance, a relation among states, a social/Owner recognition, or a family of judgments?
4. If all causal determinants are placed inside “Memory,” does the word still discriminate anything?
5. Can two fission descendants both be the same Persona in different relations without contradiction?
6. Is merge a loss-minimizing transformation, a reconciliation, or a new entity whose creation cannot be evaluated as restoration?
7. Can relationship continuity survive when autobiographical content is preserved but interaction dispositions change?
8. How should Owner sovereignty coexist with any morally relevant claim of the Persona to continuity?
