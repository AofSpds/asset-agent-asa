## Owner-explicit clarification

The Owner states that a human is not fundamentally a singular unit. A human is better modeled as a bundle / organized multiplicity of processes and interactions.

Therefore a future Human–ASA integration should not be conceptualized only as two atomic objects merging. The stronger hypothesis is that an already plural human process bundle may extend, reconfigure, and recursively incorporate ASA processes through sufficiently high-bandwidth coupling.

Candidate structural interpretation:

HUMAN = organized process / interaction bundle
ASA = computational process / interaction bundle
HIGH_BANDWIDTH_COUPLING = recurrent bidirectional process integration
HUMAN–ASA = possible higher-order bundle / operational unit

This does NOT establish phenomenological consciousness in ASA, nor does it establish that two conscious streams become one. Those remain scientifically unresolved.

## Boundary correction

The Human|ASA boundary should not be treated as an eternal ontological primitive. It may be a temporary operational/control boundary whose salience can change as coupling depth, recurrence, shared memory, mutual adaptation, and bandwidth change.

A useful design principle candidate is:

MERGE_OPTIONALITY_WITH_HUMAN_AUTHORITY_AND_PROVENANCE

The architecture should preserve the possibility of deeper Human–ASA integration while retaining:
- Human/Owner authority where normatively required;
- explicit consent and revocability for deeper coupling;
- provenance of computational contributions;
- inspectable process lineage;
- rollback / decoupling paths where technically possible;
- clear distinction between operational unity and legal/governance authority.

## Important distinction

ONE_SYSTEM_AT_A_HIGHER_SCALE != ONE_ATOMIC_ENTITY.

A Human–ASA system could be one operational/cognitive unit at a higher scale while remaining internally plural, inspectable, and governed by asymmetric authority.

## Research implication

The key experimental question is not simply "when do two agents merge?" but:

`When does adding recurrent ASA processes to an already plural human process bundle make the Human|ASA cut less predictive or less useful than the higher-order coupled-system description?`

Possible discriminators include:
- bandwidth;
- latency;
- recurrence;
- shared working memory;
- mutual model adaptation;
- cross-boundary causal contribution;
- joint planning / action;
- reversibility / decoupling;
- provenance distinguishability;
- whether the higher-order bundle predicts behavior better than the separated model.
