## Clarification

Owner states that some mappings may be called/classified as protocols, but Protocol is not necessarily a privileged ontological layer.

Mappings can be composed. When composable mappings are abstracted as a composite, the composite may itself be treated as one mapping.

Conceptually:

m1 : A => B
m2 : B => C

if composition is supported/compatible, then:

m2 ∘ m1 : A => C

and the composite may be treated as a single mapping at a higher abstraction level.

However, composition is not guaranteed. In multi-mapping / generalized mapping cases, compatibility constraints may make some compositions impossible.

The fact that some mapping or mapping bundle is called a Protocol depends on the kind/role of mapping under the relevant semantics/context; Protocol should not be assumed to be an immutable meta-layer.

## Emerging research implication

This clarification suggests a stronger candidate basis than `Mapping alone`:

MAPPING / TRANSFORMATION + COMPOSITION + COMPOSABILITY / COMPATIBILITY + ABSTRACTION

Object-like, Protocol-like, Persona-like, and higher-order structures may potentially materialize from repeated composition/bundling/abstraction rather than being fixed primitives.

A key unresolved question is whether composability/compatibility itself is intrinsic, contextual, protocol-dependent, scale-dependent, or dynamically mutable. If composability is fixed in an external type/meta-rule layer, hidden rigidity may simply migrate there.
