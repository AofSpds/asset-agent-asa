Owner expects that their existing worldview has internal consistency and therefore ASA-MI may be able to surface:

- ideas the Owner had previously thought but is not currently recalling;
- elements strongly implied by the currently reconstructed worldview;

Consistency is NOT proof.

1. OWNER_RECALLED / OWNER_EXPLICIT — Owner directly states or recalls the idea.
2. ASA_INFERRED_FROM_PATTERN — ASA infers a candidate because it is strongly consistent with multiple Owner-stated premises.
3. ASA_NEW_PROPOSAL — ASA introduces a new candidate or abstraction that was not established as Owner-held.

Candidates from categories 2 and 3 require Owner review and/or adversarial research before currentization.

## 2. Why MEMBER / COMPOSITION Is the Natural Next Axis

Current strong candidates already include:

- P0 Genesis/Liveness premise;
- relation-first modeling;
- relational constitution;
- instance-as-relational-view;
- materialized instance as a discrete object;
- succession rather than continuity;
- boundary as relation / rule / materialized result candidate;
- merge / absorption / fission;
- primitives are not denied.

If an INSTANCE is materialized from a relational configuration and may be described as a composition of members, the next unavoidable questions are:

- What counts as a MEMBER?
- Is membership itself a RELATION?
- Are members stored or derived?
- Can the same member participate in multiple instances?
- Can one instance be a member of another?
- Are relations themselves members or a separate first-class part of composition?
- How do merge/fission operations transform composition?

## 3. Critical Layer-Separation Problem — Hypothesis Member vs Instance Member

A major candidate distinction surfaced.

The project currently discusses both:

- currentized/tagged HYPOTHESES used to construct a model; and
- MEMBERS that constitute a materialized INSTANCE.

These may not be the same thing.

Potential distinction:

### HYPOTHESIS_MEMBER / MODEL_MEMBER
A selected current hypothesis used in the model/materialization recipe.

### INSTANCE_MEMBER
A concrete primitive/process/reference/instance/etc. included in the materialized object.

Why this matters:

If hypotheses about reality are stored as if they were literal members of reality, the epistemic/model layer and modeled-world layer may collapse into one another.

This distinction is a major Codex adversarial-review target and is NOT yet adopted as a canonical schema.

## 4. Strong Candidate — MEMBERSHIP AS RELATION

Strongly implied by the Owner's relation-first worldview:

> Membership may be better modeled as a relation than as intrinsic containment inside an immutable object.

Instead of treating membership only as:

`X in A`

research forms such as:

`MEMBERSHIP_RELATION(A, X, context, role, ...)`

Potential consequences:

- membership can vary by time/context;
- the same member can participate in multiple instances;
- membership can have types/roles;
- membership can be disputed/partial/contextual;
- boundary can be derived from or related to membership relations.

This is a strong inferred currentization candidate, not yet Owner-adopted.

---

## 5. Strong Candidate — COMPOSITION OVER ESSENCE

Potential working statement:

> What a concrete instance is may be explained more by the composition and relational organization of participating members/processes than by an immutable identity substance.

This is highly consistent with:

- relational constitution;
- self as connected-process bundle;
- discrete instances;
- succession;
- merge/fission;
- P0 non-finalization/liveness.

However, this remains a hypothesis and must be compared against simpler entity-first implementations.

## 6. Bare Set Is Probably Insufficient

If the same member set can support different relational topology, a bare set cannot fully represent an instance.

For example:

`Members = {A, B, C}`

may produce very different instances under different relations.

Therefore a candidate instance representation may require both membership and relational configuration, conceptually:

`INSTANCE ~= (MEMBERS, RELATIONS, ...)`

This is only a modeling sketch.

The current research must not prematurely commit to set, graph, hypergraph, category, ECS, relational algebra, or another formalism.

---

## 7. Overlapping Membership Candidate

A member may participate in multiple instances simultaneously.

Examples include a person in multiple communities or a process contributing to more than one higher-level operational unit.

Potential hypothesis:

> Overlapping composition may be normal rather than exceptional; strict tree containment may be too restrictive for reality modeling.

This candidate is strongly consistent with the current relation-first worldview but requires Owner review and technical evaluation.

---

## 8. Recursive Composition Candidate

An INSTANCE may itself become a MEMBER of another materialized INSTANCE.

Potential pattern:

`primitive/process -> instance -> higher-order instance -> community/system ...`

This could permit scale-dependent modeling without asserting that any one level is the final ontology.

Research questions:

- Does recursive membership create cycles?
- Does materialization require bounded depth?
- Can a higher-order instance and lower-order instance overlap?
- What becomes the operational reference unit?

---

## 9. MEMBER ROLE May Matter More Than Bare Membership

The same member may participate in different instances under different roles.

Therefore:

`same member` does not imply `same contribution`.

Potential `MEMBER_ROLE` examples are intentionally not fixed yet.

The important candidate is:

> Composition may require role-sensitive membership rather than a flat list of members.

This connects to function representation: a member's contribution might be evaluated through a role/context-specific mapping.

---

## 10. Dynamic / Conditional Participation Candidate

A member may exist in the broader relational substrate but participate in a particular materialized instance only under certain conditions.

Possible distinction:

- available/latent relation or member;
- active participation in the current materialization.

This could avoid requiring every relation in the world to be included in every instance.

It also provides a possible implementation mechanism for limiting relational explosion.

---

## 11. Merge / Absorption / Fission as Recomposition

The current worldview supports treating merge and fission not as identity exceptions but as composition transformations that create new instances.

Candidate patterns:

- MERGE: A + B -> C
- ABSORB: A + B -> A' (asymmetric successor semantics may be represented, while A' remains a new instance)
- FISSION: A -> B + C
- DETACH: A -> A' + B
- RECOMBINATION: A + B -> C + D
- BRANCH/CLONE: one predecessor produces multiple initially similar successors

Important:

These relations do not automatically transfer authority, ownership, obligations, or all memories. Those are separate relations/rules.

---

## 12. Membership != Ownership != Identity != Authority

This separation is especially important for Persona/Self research.

Being a member of an instance does not automatically mean:

- the instance owns the member;
- the member owns the instance;
- the member is identical with the whole;
- authority is inherited;
- legal responsibility transfers;
- self-membership grants execution rights.

This is a strong safety and modeling distinction and should remain visible during INIT design.

## 13. Primitive Does Not Mean Metaphysical Atom

Owner explicitly does not deny atomic primitives or elements that the current model does not divide further.

Important distinction:

> An implementation primitive is the current modeling floor, not necessarily a claim about the ultimate structure of reality.

Therefore recursive relational composition can coexist with primitives without contradiction.

Primitives themselves remain revisable under P0 at the model level.

## 14. No Unique Canonical Decomposition Candidate

A strong inferred candidate:

> The same reality may admit multiple useful decompositions into members depending on scale, purpose, context, or current abstraction hypotheses.

If true:

- one permanent member list is unlikely to be canonical;
- materialized instances are purpose/context-sensitive representations;
- boundary and membership become linked to materialization rules;
- observer/perspective becomes the next major research axis.

This is not yet an Owner-adopted hypothesis and is a high-value adversarial target.

## 15. MEMBER / BOUNDARY / INSTANCE Co-Constitution Candidate

A strong candidate emerged from the circularity already observed in Boundary research.

Potential statement:

> INSTANCE, MEMBERSHIP, BOUNDARY, and RELATION may not be linearly determined one after another. They may be mutually dependent aspects of one relational configuration/materialization problem.

This would explain why simple one-way function representation may be insufficient.

Possible technical alternatives to compare:

- fixed-point systems;
- constraint solving;
- iterative materialization;
- graph/hypergraph closure;
- event-sourced projection;
- entity-component systems;
- hybrid approaches.

This is a strong research candidate, not an INIT commitment.

## 16. Composition Recipe vs Materialized Composition

Inspired by the DB View / Materialized View analogy, another useful distinction is:

### COMPOSITION / MATERIALIZATION RECIPE
The current hypotheses, rules, functions, boundary criteria, relation filters, scope, time/environment and other conditions used to derive an instance.

### MATERIALIZED INSTANCE
The exact resulting object at a bounded point in time/context.

Potential benefits:

- exact replay/provenance;
- separate model change from instance history;
- compare alternate recipes over the same base substrate;
- preserve old materializations while current hypotheses evolve.

This is likely valuable for INIT experimentation.

## 17. Implementation-Cost / Anti-Stagnation Direction

The project must not solve the final ontology of membership before INIT.

Potential low-cost INIT path:

1. maintain an explicit bounded base substrate;
2. maintain selected relations/references;
3. use an explicit composition/materialization recipe;
4. materialize a concrete instance;
5. persist exact member/relation inputs and recipe refs;
6. freeze that exact materialization as historical evidence;
7. when inputs or hypotheses materially change, create a new instance rather than rewriting the old one;
8. connect instances by explicit succession / merge / fission relations.

Possible cost controls:

- bounded scope/depth;
- dependency manifests;
- cached materializations;
- event-triggered rematerialization;
- incremental view maintenance;
- avoid live whole-world recomputation.

Whether this is actually cheaper than entity-first/event-sourced alternatives must be benchmarked.

## 18. Primary Adversarial Question

The strongest practical counterargument remains:

> Why not use a stable technical entity/reference plus event sourcing and mutable projections? Does relational composition actually provide capabilities that justify its complexity?

INIT selection should depend on implementation suitability and comparative evidence, not philosophical elegance.

## 19. Next Major Axis

The MEMBER / COMPOSITION discussion naturally opens the next research axis:

`OBSERVER / PERSPECTIVE / PURPOSE`

If there is no unique canonical decomposition, then the same underlying reality may produce different valid materialized instances under different modeling questions/scales/purposes.

This must be studied carefully because it risks collapsing reality into arbitrary observer-relative construction.
