## 3. Next Legacy Harvest — Extended Mind

High-value ancestor candidate:

`EXTENDED MIND / EXTENDED COGNITION`

Clark & Chalmers (1998) introduced the influential extended-mind / active-externalism thesis: under some conditions, external structures can participate constitutively in cognitive processes rather than merely supplying inputs to an entirely internal mind.

Relevance to ASA-MI:

- directly challenges skull/skin as a necessary cognitive-system boundary;
- gives strong prior art for treating external cognitive artifacts as parts of an extended cognitive process;
- provides legacy vocabulary for discussing cognitive extension without requiring AI itself to become the sovereign subject;
- opens a direct question of what coupling/integration conditions qualify an external resource as constitutive rather than merely instrumental.

Important caution:

`EXTENDED COGNITION != EXTENDED SOVEREIGNTY`

The fact that a cognitive process is extended does not determine who should hold final authority/governance over that process.

## 4. Distributed Cognition

High-value ancestor candidate:

`DISTRIBUTED COGNITION`

Hutchins-style distributed cognition studies cognition at the level of sociotechnical systems consisting of multiple humans, artifacts, representations, and interactions. The unit of analysis need not be fixed in advance at the individual skull/body boundary.

Relevance to ASA-MI:

- supports multi-scale cognitive system materialization;
- supports treating a human+AI+artifact+social configuration as a cognitive system for some purposes;
- strongly aligns with relational constitution and scale-relative materialization;
- provides an existing research tradition for following information transformation/propagation across components instead of presupposing a single isolated thinker.

Potentially important ASA-MI resonance:

> the analysis boundary can be chosen so that important explanatory relations are not excluded merely because they cross an assumed organism boundary.

This is highly compatible with the current reluctance to treat `BOUNDARY` as a fixed metaphysical shell.

## 5. Cognitive Offloading

High-value empirical legacy candidate:

`COGNITIVE OFFLOADING`

Research distinguishes using external tools to reduce internal cognitive demand from purely internal cognition. External reminders, notes, smartphones, calendars, GPS, and similar resources can improve task performance but can also create dependence and reduce unaided recall under some conditions.

Relevant empirical pattern:

- offloading can strongly improve immediate performance;
- loss of access to an offloaded resource can produce large performance costs;
- offloading strategy is itself metacognitively regulated and can be biased;
- therefore external cognitive extension creates both capability gain and dependency/governance risk.

This maps directly onto ASA-MI's objective:

`EXPANSION BENEFIT` must be separated from `SOVEREIGNTY / DEPENDENCY RISK`.

## 6. Critical Distinction — Extended Cognitive System vs Locus of Agency

Recent philosophy of cognitive enhancement explicitly asks whether cognition can be extended while autonomous agency remains specifically human.

This creates an especially useful research split for ASA-MI:

`COGNITIVE CONSTITUTION MAY BE EXTENDED`

but

`FINAL GOVERNANCE / NORMATIVE AGENCY NEED NOT BE DISTRIBUTED IDENTICALLY`.

This is not adopted as truth. It is a high-value legacy dispute because ASA-MI's whitepaper objective similarly seeks deep integration without automatically transferring final human governance authority to the whole human-artifact system.

## 7. Very High-Value Candidate — Extension Topology != Governance Topology

ASA-MI new synthesis candidate, derived from existing legacy but not claimed as a known established theorem:

> The topology of cognitive constitution/participation may differ from the topology of governance/authority.

Example:

- memory retrieval can be distributed across human + AI + external artifacts;
- reasoning can use multiple AI personas and external knowledge stores;
- execution may be delegated to autonomous tools;
- yet veto, replacement, revocation, final preference update, identity-governance, and authority assignment can remain asymmetrically anchored on the human side.

Working expression:

`COGNITIVE MEMBERSHIP != GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY`

and

`EXTENSION TOPOLOGY != AUTHORITY TOPOLOGY`.

This closely echoes the prior ASA-MI distinction:

`MEMBERSHIP != OWNERSHIP != IDENTITY != AUTHORITY`.

This is a high-value candidate for Codex adversarial research.
