## 1. Owner-explicit clarification

OWNER_EXPLICIT:

- The preference for low rigidity and relational modeling is not only an engineering/design convenience.
- The Owner currently believes reality itself operates in a highly relational, interaction-dependent, variable manner.
- Therefore the desired model is a world-model hypothesis: the architecture should attempt to mirror this presumed structure of reality, not merely optimize software extensibility.
- Prematurely fixing object identity, relation types, interaction rules, or protocol semantics is undesirable not only because it reduces flexibility, but because it may misrepresent how reality actually unfolds.
- Diversity and flexibility therefore function both as engineering requirements and as epistemic/ontological worldview commitments.

## 3. Research consequence

Evaluation must distinguish:

A. `ENGINEERING FLEXIBILITY`
- easy extension;
- low coupling;
- reconfiguration;
- composability.

B. `WORLD-MODEL FIDELITY HYPOTHESIS`
- whether the representation naturally treats relations/interactions/events as constitutive rather than merely external operations over fixed objects;
- whether objects can be contextually constituted or changed through interaction;
- whether multiple locally valid structures can coexist;
- whether identity/status/meaning need not be globally fixed in advance.

A candidate can score highly on A while remaining weak on B.

## 4. Reo implication

Reo's low-rigidity, first-class interaction architecture is highly relevant, but Reo alone remains a partial precedent because its components are commonly treated as black boxes and its purpose is coordination rather than a rich ontology/world model.

The important next question is not only:

`Can Reo-like coordination make the system flexible?`

but:

`Can a similarly low-rigidity interaction-first formalism represent a world in which relation, event, interaction, object state, identity, and meaning may themselves be dynamically constituted?`

## 5. Open research questions

Remain OPEN:

- whether Relation is fundamental;
- whether Event/Process is fundamental;
- whether Object is emergent/materialized from interaction history;
- whether Protocol is distinct from Relation/Event or is a higher-order interaction structure;
- what invariants reality/model requires despite high variability;
- where flexibility becomes underconstraint;
- when model mutation is required because the current representation no longer matches observed interaction structure.

Do not close these by inference.
