## 1. Current hypotheses are operative INIT basis, not weak optional suggestions

Owner clarified that many items called `current hypotheses` would, in a conventional project, function much like requirements or design principles.

The research language `hypothesis` was chosen to preserve revisability and falsifiability, not to make the items optional to ignore.

Operational interpretation:

- Current hypotheses are the present best working basis for INIT.
- They should be deeply understood and addressed by model researchers.
- They may be preserved, derived, reformulated, challenged, or superseded.
- Except for P0 in the current cohort, there is no principle that is categorically unbreakable.
- Replacement is justified only when a materially better alternative exists and the gain is large enough to justify semantic/design/implementation switching cost and lost value.
- `REPLACEABLE != OPTIONAL`.
- `REVISABLE != SILENTLY_IGNORABLE`.

Candidate response classes discussed:
- PRESERVE
- DERIVE
- REFORMULATE
- CHALLENGE
- SUPERSEDE

`SILENT_IGNORE` is not a valid research contribution when the basis was part of the supplied context.

## 2. P0 and breakability

Owner clarified the intended debate boundary:

- P0 remains the constitutive premise for the current research cohort.
- P0 aside, the accumulated worldview may be broken if a challenger has sufficiently strong reasons and a better purpose-aligned alternative.
- The research should not protect current hypotheses merely because substantial effort was invested in them.
- At the same time, a challenger must first understand what the current hypothesis was trying to solve before claiming superiority.

Working principle:

`UNDERSTAND BEFORE BREAKING`.

A strong challenger may conclude:
- keep the current hypothesis,
- narrow it,
- split it,
- replace it,
- or tell the Owner explicitly that it should be changed.

## 3. Why the Owner used the term "adversarial challenger / 대항군"

The adversarial challenger is not merely a critic, evaluator, or red-team fault finder.

It is an independent research position that:

1. deeply reconstructs the current ASA-MI worldview,
2. understands the goals and the reasons behind current hypotheses,
3. forms its own model/theoretical position,
4. debates the current worldview from inside that shared understanding,
5. may expose missing spaces or unresolved assumptions,
6. may propose a materially stronger alternative,
7. must state what would be gained, lost, and changed,
8. may actively advocate: "this alternative better serves your purpose; change the current hypothesis."

The desired challenger is therefore capable of both destruction and enrichment.

Good outcomes include:
- BREAK: expose a weak current hypothesis and replace it,
- GAP DISCOVERY: find an important missing dimension implied by the Owner's purpose,
- REFORMULATION: sharpen or decompose an existing idea,
- DEFENSE: after serious attack, conclude the current basis is still best,
- COUNTERMODEL: provide an executable/modelable alternative rather than criticism only.

## 4. World Model competition is an instrument, not the final objective

Owner clarified that the model competition / tournament idea was created to find a richer theory set for the worldview.

Primary objective is not:

`MANY MODELS -> SCORE -> ONE WINNER`.

More faithful objective:

`CURRENT WORLDVIEW`
-> `INDEPENDENT THEORY / WORLD-MODEL CHALLENGERS`
-> `DEBATE / PRESSURE / IMPLEMENTABILITY`
-> `BREAKS / GAPS / REFORMULATIONS / NEW THEORIES`
-> `THEORY SET / THEORY ECOLOGY`
-> `WORLDVIEW CURRENTIZATION`
-> `BETTER INIT BASIS`.

A model may lose the tournament yet contribute a highly valuable theory or reveal a missing problem.

A winning model may have lower worldview contribution if it only restates the current worldview elegantly.

Therefore future evaluation should separate at least:

- MODEL FITNESS
- WORLDVIEW CONTRIBUTION

Worldview contribution should preserve:
- strengthening contribution,
- destructive contribution,
- missing-space discovery,
- reformulation,
- new theory,
- new question,
- independent rediscovery,
- unresolved conflict.

## 5. Theory Ecology

Owner's clarified research aim is to find a set of theories that enrich the worldview.

Theories do not need to collapse into one unified doctrine.
Competing or mutually incompatible theories may remain in the Theory Ecology when evidence does not justify convergence.

A useful theory record should eventually preserve fields such as:
- THEORY_ID
- ORIGIN / LINEAGE
- INDEPENDENT_REDISCOVERY
- SUPPORTING_MODELS
- OPPOSING_MODELS
- PURPOSE_BENEFIT
- LOSS / COST
- CONFLICTS
- OPEN_QUESTIONS
- CURRENT_WEIGHT / CURRENT_JUDGMENT

Repeated rediscovery is evidence to inspect, not automatic truth.

## 6. Worldview comprehension must precede competition

Owner expected candidate researchers to have a deep understanding of the worldview before generating models.

A mere list of current concepts is insufficient.

A future `WORLDVIEW COMPREHENSION ACT` should require a challenger to reconstruct:

- what the project is trying to build,
- why a World Model is being researched before Persona implementation,
- why the current hypotheses arose,
- which hypotheses are in tension,
- which are high-weight but unconfirmed,
- which concepts were weakened or currentized from legacy forms,
- what remains OPEN,
- what practical Persona INIT benefit is expected,
- how human familiarity, continuity, sovereignty, relation, memory, succession, and other current themes connect to the project purpose.

Comprehension should be checked before allowing the challenger to claim that a current hypothesis is inferior.

## 7. Debate / advocacy structure

Future challengers should not optimize for agreement with the Owner.
They should optimize for the Owner's purpose.

A proposed dialectic structure:

1. CURRENT WORLDVIEW COMPREHENSION
2. DISAGREEMENT / ATTACK MAP
3. MISSING SPACE
4. COUNTERMODEL / COUNTERTHEORY
5. PURPOSE ADVANTAGE
6. PRESERVATION / DESTRUCTION MAP
7. LOST VALUE
8. SWITCHING / REPLACEMENT COST
9. ASA COUNTERATTACK
10. CHALLENGER REBUTTAL
11. FINAL ADVOCACY OR RETRACTION

A challenger may strongly say "change this" only after accounting for what the current theory was accomplishing and what is lost in replacement.

A valid outcome is also: "I attacked it, but the current basis remains better; keep it."

## 8. Basic qualification still matters

Owner confirmed that challengers/models still need minimum basic competence.

Worldview agreement is not a qualification criterion.
Basic competence should instead concern substantive World Model capability, for example:

- explicit world semantics,
- explicit change semantics,
- visible primitive/derived/undefined commitments,
- native semantic consequences rather than answer-encoding,
- bounded microprobe / executable contact,
- falsifiability / abandonment condition,
- visible assumption surface,
- ability to survive "tooling/runtime/ledger only" exclusion attacks.

A model that is philosophically interesting but cannot make meaningful computational contact may remain theory input, but should not automatically qualify as a strong World Model candidate.

## 9. Human shell origin of the research

Owner clarified a foundational motivation:

The research began from the idea that a kind of `human shell` is needed for an AI Persona.

This does not mean literal biological human imitation or a permanent human-object ontology.
It means a sufficiently rich conceptual/operational substrate in which a long-lived AI Persona can be represented in ways relevant to human experience and interaction, including themes such as memory, change, continuity, relation, history, interpretation, succession, and human legibility.

The shell is not merely UI or character styling.
It is closer to the philosophical and computational substrate needed to support a persistent, changing, relational Persona in a human-compatible way.

## 10. Philosophy as reality abstraction

Owner stated that philosophy should be able to abstract reality, at least reality as humans can meaningfully understand and reason about it.

Current research interpretation:

- Philosophy is not decorative rationale added after engineering.
- Philosophy, mathematics, science, and computer science provide competing abstraction systems for reality.
- Computer implementation always selects abstractions implicitly even when no explicit philosophy is discussed.
- ASA-MI should therefore examine those abstraction choices consciously, challenge them, and seek a better computable abstraction for Persona INIT.

Important distinction:

`HUMAN-COMPATIBLE ABSTRACTION != HUMAN ONTOLOGY IMITATION`.

The internal representation need not reproduce ordinary human intuition exactly, but the result should remain useful, understandable, governable, and natural enough for the human-facing purpose.

## 11. Why World Models are useful as pressure instruments

World Model construction forces philosophical propositions into concrete consequences.

Examples of useful pressure questions:
- If relation is constitutive, exactly what changes when relations change?
- If identity is reconstructed, what is inherited across restart/fission/succession?
- If event boundaries depend on scale/perspective, how does the model operationalize that?
- If continuity is human-familiar projection rather than deep structural identity, how are the two mapped?
- If a theory claims non-globality, what concrete fixture distinguishes it from a global model?

This pressure can reveal:
- missing conditions,
- hidden ontology,
- collapsed distinctions,
- over-broad claims,
- implementation impossibility,
- better alternative theories.

Therefore World Models are a mechanism for improving the worldview itself.
