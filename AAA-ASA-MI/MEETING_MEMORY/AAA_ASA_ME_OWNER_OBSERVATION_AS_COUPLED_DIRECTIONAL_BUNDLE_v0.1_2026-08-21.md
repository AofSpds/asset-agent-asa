## Clarification

Owner notes that human cognition, living on a time axis and focusing on causality, tends to read interactions as one-way directional sequences. That may be a projection rather than the full structure.

Observation need not be modeled only as a one-way mapping from observed source to observer target. If both sides can change, a more general representation may be a coupled bundle containing mappings in different directions, potentially with multiple mappings whose roles depend on the interaction form.

Candidate interpretation:
- one-way arrow = a view/projection of a richer interaction bundle;
- source/target roles may be local to a mapping or observation slice, not permanent ontological roles;
- observation may be represented by paired or multi-directional mappings, potentially abstractable as one higher-order interaction;
- both observed and observer may change;
- time-order and causal direction remain important operational views, but the model should not force every interaction into a single immutable directional sequence.

## Scientific guardrail

Quantum-mechanical measurement should not be used as proof of this worldview. Interpretations differ on whether observables possess definite values prior to measurement; the useful point here is the formal possibility of modeling measurement as an interaction/joint transformation rather than a passive readout.

## Research implication

SPEC_CANDIDATE:
The World Model should permit an interaction to be represented as a bundle of directionally distinct mappings, including bidirectional or multi-party transformations, and permit a one-way source→target arrow to be treated as a context/time/observation-specific projection when appropriate.

Open question: Is directional decomposition fundamental, or can the higher-order interaction/bundle be primary with directed mappings derived as views?
