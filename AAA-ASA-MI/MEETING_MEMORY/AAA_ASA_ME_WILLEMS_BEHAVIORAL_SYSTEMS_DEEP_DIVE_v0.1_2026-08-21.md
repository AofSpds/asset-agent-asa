## 2. Willems behavioral systems theory — primary structural idea

Jan C. Willems' behavioral approach separates a system from its representations. A dynamical system is represented as a triple (T, W, B), where T is a time set, W is a signal space, and B is the set of trajectories considered possible by the model. Equations, state-space realizations, or input/output maps are representations of B rather than the system itself.

This is important for AAA because it weakens commitment to an internal object essence. Two different mathematical implementations may represent the same behavior.

## 3. No privileged input/output split

The behavioral approach does not require an a priori unique partition of variables into inputs and outputs. This is a substantial contrast to arrow-based input/output modeling and is one reason Willems developed the framework for open and interconnected systems.

Potential AAA harvest:
- interaction participants do not need to be assigned permanently to agent/input/output roles;
- directional roles may be contextual/materialized rather than primitive.

## 4. Interconnection as compatibility / variable sharing

For systems with compatible variables, full interconnection can be expressed by requiring trajectories to satisfy both behaviors; mathematically this is behavior intersection in the full-interconnection case. Willems also formalized interconnection through shared variables.

Interpretation for AAA:
- a relation need not be merely a tag attached to A and B;
- interconnection changes the joint space of possible behavior;
- once systems are coupled, only trajectories compatible with all relevant constraints remain possible.

This is a stronger relational notion than `A.relation = B` because coupling modifies what the combined system can actually do.

## 5. Tearing, zooming, and linking

Willems' interconnected-system modeling procedure:
- tearing: view a system as an interconnection of smaller subsystems;
- zooming: model those subsystems;
- linking: model the interconnections.

The resulting model may contain many latent variables, while a chosen manifest-variable assignment determines what is externally presented. A model of an interconnected system can itself be reused as a module at a higher level.

Potential AAA harvest:
- system boundary is partly a modeling/observation choice;
- the same underlying interconnected structure can be viewed at multiple scales;
- a subsystem at one scale can become a single module at a higher scale;
- this strongly resonates with the Owner view that a human is a set of interacting elements and a community is another higher-level system.

## 6. Critical discovery: interaction can itself be modeled as a module

Willems' 2008 interconnected-modeling exposition states that the methodology keeps primitive interconnections standardized/simple, while complex features are represented by modules. Multi-terminal connectors, mechanical joints, hooks, and hinges are modeled as modules rather than as bare connections.

This is unusually relevant to the Owner's earlier thought experiment that an interaction/rule-set might itself be treated object-like.

AAA interpretation:
- if an interaction mechanism is complex, model that interaction mechanism as a system/behavior in its own right;
- therefore `interaction-as-system` is supported as a modeling pattern;
- recursive hierarchy is possible: systems interact through interaction-systems, and the resulting whole can become a higher-level system.

This is NOT equivalent to saying Willems endorses an interaction-only ontology.

## 7. Toy mapping to the Owner's human/community example

Non-biological conceptual illustration only:
- subsystem behaviors describe ranges of possible trajectories of constituent processes;
- coupling constraints describe how those processes can coexist/interact;
- the higher-level `human` behavior is the set of trajectories satisfying the coupled constraints;
- if critical couplings disappear, the constituent matter does not vanish, but the behavior compatible with the higher-level living-human system may cease to exist;
- at a higher social scale, humans can in turn participate in coupled behaviors constituting a community.

This maps more naturally to `whole = organized interaction pattern` than to `whole = primitive object with intrinsic essence`.

## 8. Trinity fit assessment

### 제법무아 fit: STRONG_PARTIAL
- no privileged internal representation is required;
- system identity is centered on behavior rather than an intrinsic implementation;
- however, B itself is still treated as a defined mathematical object/set.

### 오온개공 fit: STRONG_STRUCTURAL_ANALOGY
- tearing/zooming/linking permits wholes to be decomposed into interacting subsystems;
- manifest vs latent variables and hierarchical module reuse weaken the idea of a unique absolute boundary;
- not a Buddhist ontological claim.

### 연기설 fit: HIGH_STRUCTURAL_RESONANCE
- interconnection/variable sharing changes the possible joint behavior;
- a larger system is constituted by compatibility constraints among interconnected components;
- but classical behavioral theory does not by itself assert that all participant identities are constituted by interaction.

## 9. Main mismatch with AAA

The Owner wants interaction flexibility to be central.

Willems' modeling philosophy often keeps interconnection primitives standardized/simple and moves complex semantics into modules. This is potentially opposite to an AAA architecture if AAA demands that the interaction relation itself be indefinitely flexible and generative.

However, the mismatch is softened by the fact that complex connectors/interactions can themselves be modeled as modules/behaviors.

Thus the strongest harvest is not `use Willems unchanged`, but:

`SYSTEM = BEHAVIOR / ALLOWED TRAJECTORY SET`
+
`INTERCONNECTION = SHARED-VARIABLE COMPATIBILITY`
+
`COMPLEX INTERACTION MAY ITSELF BE A SYSTEM`
+
`WHOLE SYSTEM MAY BE RE-MATERIALIZED AS A MODULE AT A HIGHER SCALE`.

## 10. Current successor hypothesis exposed

Candidate hypothesis, not selected:

`Interaction-Behavior Recursive System`

- primitive commitment to object identity is minimized;
- a system is provisionally identified by a behavior/constraint structure;
- interaction constrains and changes joint possibility space;
- complex interactions may themselves be systems;
- recursively coupled systems can materialize as higher-level systems;
- boundaries and object labels are scale/context/perspective-dependent projections;
- a future extension must allow interaction semantics/rules themselves to evolve or be replaced without hiding semantic mutation.

## 11. Critical tests

1. Can interaction modules be composed without simply recreating a rigid ontology in module types?
2. Can participant behavior/rules themselves change because of interaction, not merely be intersected/restricted?
3. Can new previously unseen interaction forms be represented without enumerating all connector classes in advance?
4. Can system boundaries be derived/materialized rather than fixed by the model author?
5. Can recursive interaction-systems remain falsifiable and computationally tractable?
6. Can semantic plurality/non-convergence be preserved rather than forcing a single behavior equivalence?
