## Core prior-art finding

Enactivist and participatory sense-making research treats cognition and social meaning as constituted through ongoing interaction rather than solely by pre-existing internal representations. De Jaegher & Di Paolo (2007) argue that interaction processes can take on a form of autonomy and that meaning is generated and transformed in the interplay between interaction dynamics and participating individuals. Dynamical-systems work models agent and environment as coupled systems whose behavior is jointly determined by their interaction. Perceptual-crossing simulations and experiments show cases where successful social behavior depends constitutively on mutual coupling rather than isolated individual mechanisms.

## Fit to current AAA-ASA-ME worldview hypothesis

Strong conceptual fit:
- interaction is not merely transport between already-complete objects;
- relation/coupling may partly constitute behavior and meaning;
- participants and interaction can stand in circular causal relations;
- local and global interaction dynamics may co-determine outcomes;
- world/agent state should not be assumed fully fixed prior to interaction;
- different trajectories may emerge from different histories of coupling.

## Important difference from Reo

Reo makes interaction first-class and formally composable but often leaves component internals as black boxes.
Enactivist work goes further ontologically: interaction may be constitutive of the participants' subsequent state, meaning, and social cognition.
However, enactivist research is weaker as a ready-to-use software protocol formalism. It relies heavily on dynamical-systems descriptions and experimental paradigms rather than a mature general-purpose protocol/object architecture.

## Mathematical / computational bridge

Relevant bridge lines:
- coupled dynamical-systems models of agent-environment interaction (Beer 1995);
- evolutionary robotics and perceptual-crossing models where interaction dynamics produce behavior;
- Enactive Markov Decision Processes / sensorimotor schemes;
- transition-system based enactivist mathematical models (Weinstein, Sakcak, LaValle 2022);
- active-inference / generalized-synchronization approaches to social interaction.
