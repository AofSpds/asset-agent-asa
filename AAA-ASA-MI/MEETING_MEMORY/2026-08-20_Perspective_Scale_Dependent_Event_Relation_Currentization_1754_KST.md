## 4. Same Phenomenon, Different Materialization

Candidate examples for adversarial testing:

- a handshake can be materialized as a short Event at human/social scale, or as a sustained contact Relation at millisecond/physical scale;
- a twenty-year employment Relation can be materialized as one historical Event at another scale/purpose;
- a conversation may be one Event at meeting scale, thousands of Events at utterance scale, and a Process at interaction scale;
- a stable Relation may be a compressed materialization over a long Event history;
- a change in Relation configuration may itself be materialized as an Event.

The hypothesis does NOT assert that every Event can always be losslessly rewritten as every Relation or vice versa.

## 5. Key Research Question — Where Does the Distinction Live?

A central question for Codex adversarial research:

> Is the Event/Relation distinction intrinsic to reality, intrinsic only in some domains, or introduced by the modeling perspective/scale/purpose used to materialize reality?

Sub-questions:

- Are there phenomena that are irreducibly Event-like regardless of scale/perspective?
- Are there phenomena that are irreducibly Relation-like?
- When both representations are possible, what information is lost under conversion?
- Can one unified substrate support multiple exact projections without conflating them?
- Can classification itself be a function/result of the current materialization context?
- What happens when two perspectives materialize incompatible Event/Relation interpretations of the same underlying evidence?

---

## 6. Strong Anti-Vacuity Constraint

A broad encompassing model risks becoming unfalsifiable if it says that anything can be anything depending on perspective.

Therefore a useful model must NOT merely erase distinctions.

It must explain:

1. what remains invariant across perspectives, if anything;
2. what changes with scale/scope/purpose;
3. why a given projection is preferred for a given task;
4. what information is lost or preserved between projections;
5. when two projections are compatible, incomparable, or contradictory;
6. what operational cost each projection introduces.

`PERSPECTIVE DEPENDENCE != ARBITRARY CLASSIFICATION`.
