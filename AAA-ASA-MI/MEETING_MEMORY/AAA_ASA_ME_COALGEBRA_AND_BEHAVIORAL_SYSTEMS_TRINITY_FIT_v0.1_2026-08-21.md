## Coalgebra
Relevant prior art:
- J.J.M.M. Rutten, Universal coalgebra: a theory of systems, TCS 249 (2000).
- Bart Jacobs, Introduction to Coalgebra: Towards Mathematics of States and Observation.

Key structural point:
A coalgebra models a state-based system through observable behavior and transitions rather than primarily through constructive internal decomposition. Bisimulation supplies behavioral equivalence.

Fit:
- strong fit to Owner's black-box/function intuition: internal implementation need not define behavioral identity;
- useful for persistent state, observation, transition, behavioral equivalence;
- weak/partial fit to the stronger Owner hypothesis that interaction/coupling itself is ontologically prior.

Critical gap:
Coalgebra generally starts with a state carrier X and a structure map X -> F(X). Therefore it does not by itself eliminate system/state objects or make interaction itself primitive. It can be a strong behavioral semantics layer without being the final interaction-first ontology.

## Behavioral systems theory (Willems) — newly elevated prior-art candidate
Relevant prior art:
- Jan C. Willems' behavioral approach to systems theory.
- A system is separated from a particular representation and described by a set of admissible trajectories/behaviors.
- Input/output status is not primitive; it can be a modeling choice.
- Interconnection is central in the behavioral framework; later work treats control through interconnection.

Why this may fit Owner unusually well:
- system != one privileged internal representation;
- behavior/allowed trajectories are primary;
- input/output causal orientation need not be pre-imposed;
- interconnected systems are handled compositionally;
- closer to `system as set of allowable mappings/trajectories under constraints` than an object-first ontology.

Critical gap:
- still formalizes a system using a chosen time axis, signal space, and behavior set;
- does not automatically imply Buddhist dependent origination or objectlessness;
- interaction is important but not necessarily the sole primitive;
- many classical results focus on linear dynamical/control systems, so transfer to general world/persona modeling is nontrivial.
