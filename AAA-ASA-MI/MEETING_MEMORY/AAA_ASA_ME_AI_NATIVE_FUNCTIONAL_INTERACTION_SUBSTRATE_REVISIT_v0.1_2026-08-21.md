## Owner explicit signal
- Owner previously considered that an AI might find a function/process/interaction-centric substrate easier to manipulate than a human, but abandoned the idea in part because functional programming was cognitively difficult for the Owner to work with directly.
- Owner now revisits that assumption because ASA can mediate between a machine-native substrate and human-friendly views.

## Current research interpretation
- Human difficulty with functional/process-centric representations is an ergonomics constraint, not evidence that an object-first ontology is more faithful to reality.
- Candidate architecture remains OPEN:
  - canonical substrate: function/process/morphism/interaction/composition/history;
  - ASA: translation, explanation, provenance, replay, manipulation, validation support;
  - human-facing views: object/class/API/relation/event/narrative as generated materializations.
- Do not claim that LLMs intrinsically "prefer" functional representations. The valid hypothesis is narrower: AI-mediated tooling may reduce the human ergonomic cost of a process/interaction-native representation enough to make it testable.

## Discriminating test
Represent the same small world in:
1. HUMAN-FIRST OBJECT MODEL;
2. AI-NATIVE INTERACTION/PROCESS MODEL.
Then compare machine manipulation fidelity, explainability, semantic mutation handling, replay, provenance, and quality of human-facing projections.

## Risk
Do not allow AI-mediated complexity to become opaque. A machine-native substrate still requires deterministic lineage, inspectable rule changes, replay, explicit unsupported states, and reversible/traceable projections where feasible.
