## Research question
Whether a Mapping / Transformation / Morphism / Interaction-first primitive family is an appropriate computational basis for both:
1. the current three philosophical/computational principles (제법무아, 오온개공, 연기설), and
2. Persona lifecycle requirements including change, differentiation, merge, re-separation, continuity, memory, boundary change, and mechanism change.

## Current answer
NOT_PROVEN. The mapping-first family is a strong candidate because it may allow object, identity, boundary, and system views to be derived rather than assumed. But fitness must be demonstrated rather than inferred from philosophical resonance.

## Trinity fit tests
### 제법무아
Pass condition: no immutable self/object essence is required as a primitive; object-like identity can be recovered from stable interaction/composition/history when useful.
Failure: hidden carrier/object/node/entity identity is required for semantics.

### 오온개공
Pass condition: a whole can be decomposed into interacting processes/components and can itself participate as a component at another scale; no absolute decomposition level is required.
Failure: model fixes one privileged object granularity or cannot express recomposition across scales.

### 연기설
Pass condition: outcomes, relations, boundaries, and potentially the mapping/mechanism itself can change through conditions and interaction/history.
Failure: interactions only move state under fixed rules, with rigidity merely displaced to an immutable meta-rule.

## Persona lifecycle fit tests
The model should naturally represent:
- INIT without assuming permanent atomic identity
- STATE CHANGE
- PARAMETER CHANGE
- RELATION/TOPOLOGY CHANGE
- INTERFACE/BOUNDARY CHANGE
- MECHANISM CHANGE (f_t -> f_{t+1})
- DIFFERENTIATION / branching into successors
- CO-ADAPTATION
- PARTIAL or FULL MERGE into a higher-order bundle
- RE-SEPARATION after merge
- continuity without requiring immutable substance
- provenance/history sufficient to explain lineage at relevant scales

## Key discriminating question
Do these Persona operations emerge naturally from the primitive semantics, or are they bolted on through special-case object operations such as clone(), merge(), parent_id, and mutable attributes?

## Required comparison
At minimum compare:
A. OBJECT-FIRST CONTROL
B. MAPPING/TRANSFORMATION-FIRST CANDIDATE
C. at least one independently discovered third-family candidate.

## Decision rule
Do not select Mapping as primitive because it aligns aesthetically with Owner worldview. Select only if it provides lower semantic distortion, fewer hidden assumptions, better support for dynamic mechanism/boundary/identity, and executable handling of Persona lifecycle stress cases.
