## OWNER_EXPLICIT

- The model was intentionally kept as non-rigid as possible in order to preserve relationality.
- Relationality is not merely an ontological preference; it is a design reason for avoiding premature hard-coding of objects, identity, protocols, or interaction paths.
- Excess rigidity can destroy the very flexibility needed for relations to vary by context, interaction, event, perspective, and protocol.

## CURRENT RESEARCH INTERPRETATION

MODEL_INFERRED / NOT CANONICAL:

The Owner's direction is better represented as:

`RELATIONAL EXPRESSIVITY -> MINIMIZE PREMATURE STRUCTURAL COMMITMENT`

rather than:

`RELATIONALITY -> FIX ONE RELATION ONTOLOGY`.

Therefore a candidate model should be judged not only by whether it represents relations, but by whether its representational commitments leave room for:

- heterogeneous relation types;
- interaction-dependent relation emergence;
- event-driven relation change;
- multiple protocol/interaction semantics;
- local/contextual materialization;
- explicit unsupported/out-of-scope states;
- bounded reconfiguration or successor mutation where required.

This is a rationale for flexibility, not a permission for unconstrained semantics.

## REO RELEVANCE

The Reo family is currently high-interest because it treats interaction/coordination as a first-class structure and avoids embedding every interaction rule inside object implementations. However, Reo's black-box treatment of component internals remains a major gap for AAA-ASA-MI world/persona modeling.

The current high-interest research question is whether a richer world/object model can coexist with a Reo-like low-rigidity interaction layer without reintroducing hidden rigidity or losing semantic meaning.
