## Core clarification

Owner questions whether `A <-> B` is ontologically primitive at all.

Reason: time is directional in lived causal experience, and the participant at a later time is not literally the same instantaneous state as the participant at the earlier time.

A more faithful representation candidate is a time-extended causal history:

`A_t0 -> B_t1 -> A_t2 -> B_t3 -> ...`

or, more generally, asynchronously interleaved future-directed event mappings in opposite participant directions.

Thus:
- every concrete influence remains future-directed in time;
- `A -> B` and `B -> A` are not two arrows between the same static A and B;
- the source and target are time-local event/state slices of changing process bundles;
- reciprocity/bidirectionality may be a coarse-grained abstraction over a history of counter-directional causal mappings;
- `same participant` across time may be a succession relation, not strict identity.

Candidate principle:

`RECIPROCITY_IS_A_PROPERTY_OF_CAUSAL_HISTORY_NOT_A_PRIMITIVE_SIMULTANEOUS_EDGE`

Candidate formal view:

`event/process slice -> causally reachable future event/process slice`

with opposite-direction mapping bundles coupling across time.
