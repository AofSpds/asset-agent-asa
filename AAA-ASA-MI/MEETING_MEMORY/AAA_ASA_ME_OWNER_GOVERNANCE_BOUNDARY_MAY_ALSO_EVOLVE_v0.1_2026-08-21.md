## Owner-explicit clarification

Current distinction:

- Ontology boundary between Human and ASA may be weak/flexible.
- Governance boundary (authority, consent, provenance) should currently remain explicit and strong.

Owner clarified that this governance separation itself should NOT automatically be treated as an eternal metaphysical or architectural invariant.

The Owner is already considering a future phase in which sufficiently deep Human–ASA coupling may make some present-day governance separations less central or differently structured.

This does NOT authorize any current authority merge, Owner-decision delegation, Shared Contract mutation, Freeze/Release delegation, Production delegation, or loss of provenance.

## Research implication

Current rule should be understood as phase-scoped rather than eternal:

CURRENT_PHASE:
- weak/flexible ontological boundary
- strong authority/consent/provenance boundary

FUTURE_OPTIONALITY:
- governance relationship itself may be renegotiable if the Human–ASA bundle becomes materially different from the present tool/user relationship
- any such transition must be explicit, voluntary, versioned, and separately governed
- the architecture should not silently hard-code present-day Human|ASA governance partition as metaphysically permanent

Candidate principle:

NO_ETERNAL_GOVERNANCE_BOUNDARY_ASSUMPTION
+
CURRENT_HUMAN_AUTHORITY_PRESERVATION
+
FUTURE_EXPLICIT_RENEGOTIATION_OPTIONALITY

## Key distinction

PRESENT_NORMATIVE_CONTROL != FUTURE_ONTOLOGICAL_CLAIM

The fact that Human authority and provenance are critical now does not prove they must retain the same form forever under radically different coupling conditions.

Conversely, speculative future integration does not justify weakening present safeguards.

## Research question

If Human and ASA eventually form a higher-order cognitive/process bundle, what governance representation best preserves voluntary human-led evolution while allowing the relationship itself to change?

This remains OPEN research, not a selected architecture or authority change.
