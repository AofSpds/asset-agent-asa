# 1. Thesis and design posture

N08 models an Object and a Protocol as separate, first-class, typed entities joined by a proof-carrying contract. An Object is not a disguised record of whichever Protocol currently observes it, and a Protocol is not a tag, enum, method table, or ambient interpretation baked into the Object. Each has identity, state, lineage, and an independently addressable interface. Their contact is explicit, contextual, eventful, and bidirectional.

The stable substrate is intentionally small. It supplies identity, namespaced types, values, relations, events, contexts, claims, evidence, judgments, authority, and lineage. Protocols extend this substrate through declared schemas and typed contract surfaces; they do not silently rewrite the substrate. This allows a previously unseen Protocol to be received and reasoned about without pretending that every such Protocol can be understood, proven, composed, or executed.

Proof-carrying Protocol contracts form a context-indexed refinement poset with partial compatibility, meet, and join operations. It is a **partial lattice**, not a universal lattice: comparison is meaningful only when the typed mappings, role polarity, context, proof system, and observational boundary required by the comparison are declared. There is no privileged `AnyProtocol`, no global top that faithfully abstracts every behavior, no global bottom that safely implements every contract, and no rule that manufactures a join for incompatible meanings.

The proposal makes uncertainty and plurality structural. `UNKNOWN`, `CONFLICT`, `NOT_PROVEN`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, and `MULTIPLE_VALID` are terminally reportable results, not temporary strings to be coerced to booleans. Multiple Protocols and multiple Personas may remain simultaneously active over one Object. Switching is an evidenced state migration, never a relabeling trick. Semantic changes create successor identities and lineage rather than rewriting history.

This document is a frozen, non-normative candidate. Its definitions are precise enough to implement and falsify, but they are not a claim of validation, consensus, completeness, or universal applicability.

# 2. Scope, commitments, and non-goals

## 2.1 In scope

N08 commits to:

1. an explicit Object Model independent of any particular Protocol;
2. a first-class Protocol Model in which Protocol references and Protocol variables can be stored, passed, constrained, bound, compared, and switched at runtime;
3. proof-carrying assume/guarantee contracts over typed event traces;
4. context-indexed refinement and compatibility judgments with partial lattice operations;
5. bidirectional Object–Protocol contact mediated by proposals, decisions, events, and receipts;
6. first-class relations and relation-responsive execution;
7. honest admission of heterogeneous and previously unseen Protocol descriptions;
8. contextual, provenance-bearing views that do not overwrite their Objects;
9. simultaneous plurality, including multi-Persona and multi-Protocol cases;
10. an explicit boundary among `READ`, `VIEW`, `RUNTIME_MUTATION`, and `SEMANTIC_MUTATION`;
11. immutable semantic identities, successor construction, and queryable lineage;
12. executable interfaces, conformance traces, invariants, failure behavior, and falsifiers.

## 2.2 Non-goals

N08 does not attempt to:

- prove that all Protocols are mutually comparable or composable;
- infer an unseen Protocol's intended meaning solely from its syntax or observed examples;
- collapse context-relative answers into one context-free truth;
- force contradictory evidence into a single winning value;
- merge Personas or Protocol states merely because they refer to the same Object;
- promise lossless switching where no verified state/trace mapping exists;
- accept opaque executable code as a substitute for a contract or proof;
- grant a Protocol authority merely because it has been parsed or admitted;
- treat proof-checker acceptance as metaphysical truth;
- make liveness claims in the absence of a declared scheduler and resource model;
- erase predecessors after mutation, migration, revocation, or supersession.

The word **supported** is local throughout this proposal. It means supported by a named substrate version, checker set, capability set, context, and host policy. It never means universally valid.

# 3. Stable typed substrate

Let `K_v` be a versioned kernel substrate. All contract terms must either use a kernel type or reference a namespaced extension schema whose encoding is carried with the contract. Kernel evolution is additive where possible. Reinterpreting an existing kernel field is a semantic mutation of the substrate and therefore requires a new `K_v`, an explicit bridge, and proof obligations.

The substrate contains the following irreducible sorts:

```text
EntityId       opaque, globally collision-resistant identity
SemanticId     immutable identity of a meaning-bearing entity version
StateRevision  monotone revision within one SemanticId
TypeRef        (namespace, name, semantic_version, schema_digest)
Value          typed value or preserved opaque bytes with TypeRef and digest
ContextRef     immutable reference to a contextual fact set and policy horizon
Claim          typed proposition scoped to ContextRef
EvidenceRef    immutable pointer plus digest, producer, method, and validity scope
Judgment<T>    evidenced result algebra defined in section 11
AuthorityRef   scoped authorization and delegation record
TimeRef        logical/causal time; wall time is optional metadata
LineageEdge    typed predecessor/successor relation defined in section 15
```

The stable transport forms are:

```text
ObjectRef   = (object_root_id, semantic_id, state_revision?)
ProtocolRef = (protocol_family_id, semantic_id)
PersonaRef  = (object_root_id, persona_semantic_id)
RelationRef = (relation_id, semantic_id, state_revision?)
EventRef    = (event_id, event_digest)
```

Absence is never overloaded. A field may be `ABSENT` because the schema permits absence; a value may be `UNKNOWN` because relevant evidence is missing; a claim may be `NOT_PROVEN`; and a query may be `OUT_OF_SCOPE`. These conditions have different encodings.

Extension values are carried as:

```text
ExtValue {
  type: TypeRef,
  canonical_bytes: Bytes,
  content_digest: Digest,
  optional_schema: SchemaRef,
  optional_decoder: CapabilityRef
}
```

A host that lacks the decoder may preserve, relay, hash, authorize, and compare the opaque value for byte identity. It may not claim semantic equality, validation, or executability. This preservation path is the substrate's principal mechanism for heterogeneous admission without false understanding.

Every state-affecting envelope also carries `context`, `authority`, `causal_parents`, and `protocol_instance` explicitly. Ambient locale, clock, identity, policy, or “current Protocol” state is not semantically authoritative unless captured by a referenced context.

# 4. Explicit Object Model

An Object is an identity-bearing, event-addressable state carrier over the substrate:

```text
Object {
  root_id: EntityId,
  semantic_id: SemanticId,
  kind: TypeRef,
  state_revision: StateRevision,
  state: Map<FieldRef, FactSet>,
  relations: Set<RelationRef>,
  personas: Set<PersonaRef>,
  endpoints: Set<EndpointDescriptor>,
  authority_policy: PolicyRef,
  active_protocols: Set<ProtocolInstanceRef>,
  lineage: Set<LineageEdge>
}
```

`FactSet` is evidence-preserving rather than last-write-wins:

```text
FactSet = Set<Fact>
Fact {
  value: Value,
  claim: Claim,
  evidence: Set<EvidenceRef>,
  asserted_by: EntityRef,
  valid_context: ContextPredicate,
  causal_time: TimeRef,
  status: ACTIVE | RETRACTED | SUPERSEDED
}
```

Two active facts may conflict. Storage does not select a winner unless an explicitly named resolution Protocol is invoked. Retraction adds a signed event; it does not delete the prior assertion.

An Object exposes four surfaces:

- a read surface for substrate facts, events, relations, and lineage;
- a contextual view surface for pure interpretation;
- a proposal surface on which bound Protocols request runtime transitions;
- a semantic-successor surface for meaning-changing operations.

Endpoints declare accepted message types, authority requirements, idempotency keys, and receipt types. They do not imply that every bound Protocol may call them. Binding creates a capability intersection among Object endpoint, Protocol contract, context policy, and caller authority.

Object behavior is an event-sourced transition relation:

```text
delta_O : (ObjectState, AcceptedProposal, ContextRef)
          -> Judgment<(ObjectState', Set<EventEnvelope>)>
```

`delta_O` is owned by the Object's current semantic definition. A Protocol may propose an input and prove that it satisfies the endpoint precondition, but the Object independently checks authority, revision, type, and invariant obligations before applying it. Thus a Protocol can influence an Object without becoming the Object.

# 5. First-class variable Protocol Model

A Protocol is an immutable, meaning-bearing entity:

```text
Protocol {
  family_id: EntityId,
  semantic_id: SemanticId,
  kind: TypeRef,
  substrate_range: VersionConstraint<K>,
  parameters: List<ProtocolParameter>,
  roles: Map<RoleName, RoleContract>,
  channels: Set<ChannelContract>,
  states: TypedStateMachine,
  contract: Contract,
  proofs: ProofBundle,
  contact: ContactDescriptor,
  lineage: Set<LineageEdge>
}
```

Protocol parameters may range over substrate values, types, roles, contexts, policies, and other Protocols. In particular:

```text
ProtocolVar alpha : ProtocolKind<K_v>
Constraint(alpha) : Set<ContractPredicate>
Binding(alpha)     : UNBOUND | CANDIDATES(Set<ProtocolRef>) |
                     BOUND(ProtocolRef, BindingEvidence) |
                     CONFLICT(Set<ProtocolRef>, Evidence)
```

`alpha` is a first-class term. It may appear in an Object field, relation endpoint, contract parameter, event payload, view request, or higher-order Protocol contract. It is not required to be resolved before all useful work can occur. Operations declare whether they require a concrete binding; a read may preserve an unbound variable, while execution of a transition normally requires a single adequately proven binding or an explicit multi-dispatch rule.

A `ProtocolInstance` binds one immutable Protocol meaning to roles, parameters, context, and runtime state:

```text
ProtocolInstance {
  instance_id: EntityId,
  protocol: ProtocolRef,
  role_bindings: Map<RoleName, ObjectRef | PersonaRef | RelationRef>,
  parameter_bindings: Map<ParameterName, Value | TypeRef | ProtocolRef>,
  context: ContextRef,
  runtime_revision: StateRevision,
  runtime_state: Value,
  pending_obligations: Set<Obligation>,
  subscriptions: Set<EventPattern>,
  lifecycle: ADMITTED | ACTIVE | SUSPENDED | COMPLETED |
             FAILED | SUPERSEDED | QUARANTINED
}
```

Protocol meaning and Protocol runtime state are distinct. Advancing an `ACTIVE` instance through an already-declared state machine is a runtime mutation. Changing its roles, event meanings, guarantees, resolution rules, or state-machine interpretation is a semantic mutation and requires a successor Protocol or successor binding, as applicable.

Higher-order Protocols are permitted only when their Protocol-valued parameters have contract bounds. A higher-order host cannot invoke an unknown child Protocol merely because its bytes parsed successfully. Invocation requires a supported contact form and a proved or policy-accepted contract at the relevant boundary.

# 6. Proof-carrying Protocol contracts

A contract is an immutable typed artifact with both declarative and executable contact:

```text
Contract {
  contract_type: TypeRef,
  substrate: VersionConstraint<K>,
  scope: ContextPredicate,
  roles: Map<RoleName, RoleSurface>,
  assumptions: Set<ClaimSchema>,
  guarantees: Set<ClaimSchema>,
  accepted_events: Set<EventSchema>,
  emitted_events: Set<EventSchema>,
  relation_rules: Set<RelationRule>,
  transition_rules: TypedStateMachine,
  refusal_rules: Set<RefusalRule>,
  observables: ObservationAlgebra,
  effects: EffectSet,
  resource_claims: Set<ResourceClaim>,
  proof_obligations: Set<ProofObligation>,
  proof_bundle: ProofBundle,
  test_vectors: Set<ConformanceTrace>,
  contact: ContactDescriptor
}
```

A proof bundle identifies its logic, checker, checker version, trusted roots, axioms, imported lemmas, claimed theorem, proof bytes, and digest. Proof evidence is never detached from these dependencies.

```text
ProofReceipt {
  theorem: Claim,
  result: VERIFIED | REJECTED | CHECKER_UNAVAILABLE | RESOURCE_LIMIT |
          DEPENDENCY_MISSING | TRUST_ROOT_REJECTED,
  checker: TypeRef,
  dependency_digests: Set<Digest>,
  context: ContextRef,
  checked_at: TimeRef,
  receipt_digest: Digest
}
```

Only `VERIFIED` establishes the claimed theorem under the named checker and dependencies. `REJECTED` establishes that the submitted proof term failed that checker; it does not establish the negation of the theorem. Unavailable, incomplete, untrusted, or resource-exhausted checking yields `NOT_PROVEN` with the relevant reason.

Contracts may carry proof classes independently:

- schema well-formedness and type closure;
- state-machine preservation of declared invariants;
- refinement of another contract;
- compatibility with a named role/channel contract;
- confinement of effects to declared capabilities;
- bounded safety or liveness claims;
- fidelity of a state or trace translation;
- deterministic behavior under a declared scheduler, if claimed.

One verified class does not imply another. In particular, parsing and type-checking do not prove behavioral compatibility, and behavioral compatibility does not grant authority.

Proof transport is optional for preservation but required for any conclusion that cites the proof. A host may admit a contract as opaque or observable even when it cannot check the proof. Its honest outcome is then `NOT_PROVEN`, not `false` and not `SUPPORTED`.

# 7. Partial compatibility and refinement lattice

All lattice judgments are indexed by a comparison frame:

```text
Frame F = (substrate_version,
           context_class,
           role_polarity,
           channel_mapping,
           observation_algebra,
           proof_system,
           host_policy)
```

For contracts `P` and `Q`, write `Q <=_F P` when `Q` is a proved behavioral refinement of `P` in frame `F`. With assume/guarantee contracts, a valid witness must show at minimum:

1. the mapped types and roles are well-formed;
2. `Q` does not demand more from an environment admitted by `P`, expressed as `A_P => A_Q` under the frame mapping;
3. under `A_P`, `Q` provides guarantees at least as strong as `P`, expressed as `G_Q => G_P`;
4. externally visible `Q` traces project into permitted `P` traces under the declared observation algebra;
5. additional effects, refusals, timing assumptions, and resource demands are either absent or explicitly tolerated by `P`.

The judgment result is not boolean:

```text
refines(Q, P, F) -> Judgment<RefinementWitness>
```

`Compat_F(P,Q)` means there exists a witnessed role/channel composition whose joint assumptions are satisfiable and whose guarantees do not violate either side's declared safety conditions. Compatibility is not inferred from shared names or shared ancestry. It may be directional when role polarity or effects differ.

```text
compatible(P, Q, F) -> Judgment<CompositionWitness>
```

The lattice operations are partial:

- `meet_F(P,Q)` is the greatest representable common refinement when one is evidenced;
- `join_F(P,Q)` is the least representable common abstraction when one is evidenced;
- either may return `MULTIPLE_VALID` when several incomparable optima are evidenced;
- either may return `UNKNOWN` when search or evidence is incomplete;
- either may return `NOT_PROVEN` when a candidate exists but its obligation is unmet;
- either may return `CONFLICT` when accepted premises force incompatible obligations;
- either may return `OUT_OF_SCOPE` when no valid comparison frame or type mapping is declared.

The carrier is therefore a family of locally evidenced posets with partial meets and joins, not one completed global lattice. Results from distinct frames cannot be combined until a frame bridge is supplied. A host must preserve the frame with every cached result; dropping it invalidates the judgment.

There is no automatic lattice completion. A synthetic union that says “behave as either `P` or `Q`” is not a join unless its selection observables, refusals, effects, and obligations are themselves contracted and the leastness proof succeeds. Likewise, an uninhabitable conjunction is not a useful meet merely because a logical formula can be written.

Compatibility and refinement are revocable only by adding a new context, trust, or evidence judgment; prior receipts remain historically addressable. A changed checker, observation boundary, policy, or type bridge produces a new frame and a new result rather than rewriting the old one.

# 8. Bidirectional Object–Protocol interaction

Object–Protocol contact uses a mediated five-step cycle:

1. **Expose:** the Object exposes typed endpoints, current semantic identity, state revision, relations, and capabilities allowed in the binding context.
2. **Observe:** the Protocol instance receives matching Object or relation events and may request pure contextual views.
3. **Propose:** the Protocol emits a typed `ActionProposal`, `Question`, `Refusal`, or `NoAction` with contract and evidence references.
4. **Decide/apply:** the Object checks type, authority, expected revision, contract preconditions, and Object invariants, then accepts or refuses the proposal.
5. **Receipt:** the Object emits an outcome event; the Protocol consumes that event to advance, retain, suspend, compensate, or fail its own runtime state.

Core envelopes are:

```text
ActionProposal {
  proposal_id: EntityId,
  protocol_instance: ProtocolInstanceRef,
  target: ObjectRef | RelationRef,
  endpoint: EndpointRef,
  input: Value,
  expected_semantic_id: SemanticId,
  expected_state_revision: StateRevision,
  context: ContextRef,
  authority: AuthorityRef,
  contract_clause: ClaimRef,
  evidence: Set<EvidenceRef>,
  idempotency_key: Digest
}

ActionReceipt {
  proposal_id: EntityId,
  decision: APPLIED | REFUSED | STALE | DUPLICATE | DEFERRED,
  judgment: Judgment<Value>,
  resulting_object: ObjectRef,
  emitted_events: Set<EventRef>
}
```

This is bidirectional in the strong sense: Objects send facts, relation changes, questions, refusals, and receipts to Protocols; Protocols send interpretations, view requests, proposals, commitments, and compensations to Objects. Neither side gets an unlogged back door into the other's state.

The Object may challenge a Protocol's claim by requesting the proof receipt named in a proposal. The Protocol may challenge an Object's refusal by requesting its policy clause and evidence. These challenge paths produce additional events; they do not suspend authorization rules.

Concurrency uses optimistic revisions and idempotency. A stale proposal is not silently retried against new state because its precondition may no longer mean the same thing. The Protocol receives `STALE` and must re-observe, re-evaluate, and emit a new proposal or an evidenced no-op.

# 9. Relation and Event responsiveness

Relations are first-class typed hyperedges rather than embedded Object fields:

```text
Relation {
  relation_id: EntityId,
  semantic_id: SemanticId,
  kind: TypeRef,
  endpoints: Map<RoleName, Set<ObjectRef | PersonaRef | ProtocolRef | Value>>,
  attributes: Map<FieldRef, FactSet>,
  context: ContextRef,
  state_revision: StateRevision,
  authority_policy: PolicyRef,
  lineage: Set<LineageEdge>
}
```

An endpoint change is a relation transition and emits an event. It is not a quiet update to each participating Object. Protocol contracts may match on relation kind, endpoint role, endpoint semantic type, attribute delta, cardinality, and causal neighborhood.

Events use a canonical envelope:

```text
EventEnvelope {
  event_id: EntityId,
  event_type: TypeRef,
  producer: ObjectRef | ProtocolInstanceRef | RelationRef,
  subject: ObjectRef | ProtocolInstanceRef | RelationRef,
  relation: RelationRef?,
  payload: Value,
  context: ContextRef,
  causal_parents: Set<EventRef>,
  logical_time: TimeRef,
  authority: AuthorityRef,
  schema_digest: Digest,
  envelope_digest: Digest
}
```

A Protocol subscription is a typed predicate over the envelope and, optionally, a bounded causal window. On each delivered event the Protocol transition function returns:

```text
step_P(instance_state, event, readable_snapshot)
  -> Judgment<(new_instance_state,
               Set<ActionProposal | ViewRequest | ProtocolEvent | Refusal>)>
```

The `readable_snapshot` is pinned to semantic IDs and revisions. If required relation or Object data is unavailable, the Protocol returns `UNKNOWN` or requests it; it must not evaluate against an unrecorded ambient snapshot.

Event delivery is at-least-once unless a stronger channel contract is proved. Consumers deduplicate by `event_id` and digest. Reuse of an `event_id` with a different digest is `CONFLICT`. Missing causal parents produce a causal gap and normally `UNKNOWN`; they are not proof that no parent exists. Reordering is permitted only if the Protocol declares commutativity or supplies a reorder buffer policy.

Relation responsiveness is tested independently of direct Object events: a conforming runtime must awaken every active subscription whose predicate changes from false to true because of a relation creation, endpoint change, attribute transition, tombstone, or successor edge.

# 10. Heterogeneous and unseen Protocol admission

Admission is descriptor-driven, not registry-name-driven. A host may receive a Protocol family and schema it has never encountered. Admission proceeds through independent gates:

1. **Envelope integrity:** verify digests, canonical encoding, semantic IDs, and declared substrate range.
2. **Schema closure:** resolve namespaced type dependencies or preserve unresolved values opaquely.
3. **Contact classification:** determine whether contact is declarative only, sandbox-executable, native-executable, remotely mediated, or unavailable.
4. **Proof checking:** run only identified supported checkers under explicit resource bounds.
5. **Effect and authority intersection:** compare declared effects with host capabilities and policy.
6. **Contract placement:** attempt only requested refinement, compatibility, meet, or join judgments in a declared frame.
7. **Conformance probes:** run signed test vectors or sandbox traces when supported.
8. **Admission decision:** issue a reasoned status without promoting unknowns to success.

Admission states are:

```text
ADMITTED_EXECUTABLE  contract understood enough for authorized execution;
ADMITTED_OBSERVABLE  can preserve, inspect, route, and/or view, but not mutate;
QUARANTINED           integrity/contact/proof is pending or suspect;
UNSUPPORTED           a named local capability or policy requirement is unmet;
REJECTED              a concrete integrity, type, policy, or proof check failed.
```

These states are capability-specific. A Protocol can be executable for read/view yet unsupported for mutation. `UNSUPPORTED` is not a claim that the Protocol is incoherent or impossible elsewhere. `REJECTED` must name the failed check and preserve the submitted artifact and receipt when policy permits.

An unseen Protocol is never assigned semantic equivalence to a familiar Protocol by similarity alone. It may propose a type bridge or refinement witness. The host checks that artifact in a frame. If several known placements are equally evidenced, placement returns `MULTIPLE_VALID`; if no adequate evidence exists, it returns `UNKNOWN` or `NOT_PROVEN`.

Opaque relay is allowed only when the relay contract can enforce declared size, integrity, routing, confidentiality, and effect boundaries without interpreting the payload. Opaque execution is not admission: unknown code cannot acquire mutation authority merely by declaring that it is a Protocol.

# 11. Contextual views and honest outcomes

A view is a pure, immutable derivation:

```text
ViewRequest {
  subjects: Set<ObjectRef | RelationRef>,
  protocol: ProtocolRef | ProtocolVar,
  personas: Set<PersonaRef>,
  context: ContextRef,
  observation_algebra: ObservationAlgebraRef,
  evidence_horizon: Horizon,
  policy: PolicyRef
}

View<T> {
  value: Judgment<T>,
  subjects: Set<EntityRef>,
  source_revisions: Set<(SemanticId, StateRevision)>,
  protocol: ProtocolRef | ProtocolVar,
  personas: Set<PersonaRef>,
  context: ContextRef,
  derivation: DerivationRef,
  evidence_used: Set<EvidenceRef>,
  evidence_omitted: Set<(EvidenceRef, OmissionReason)>,
  created_at: TimeRef,
  view_digest: Digest
}
```

Views do not become Object facts unless a separate authorized runtime mutation asserts them, with the view recorded as evidence. Cache creation is an implementation detail and cannot alter the semantic result. A cache hit must reproduce the pinned request and source revisions.

The result algebra is:

```text
Judgment<T> =
    ESTABLISHED(value: T, evidence: NonEmptySet<EvidenceRef>)
  | MULTIPLE_VALID(values: NonEmptySet<T>, evidence: Set<EvidenceRef>,
                   selection_rule_missing: Claim)
  | UNKNOWN(missing: Set<Requirement>, known: Set<EvidenceRef>)
  | CONFLICT(positions: NonEmptySet<(Claim, Set<EvidenceRef>)>,
             conflict_rule: Claim)
  | NOT_PROVEN(claim: Claim, obligation: ProofObligation,
               attempted_receipts: Set<ProofReceipt>)
  | OUT_OF_SCOPE(boundary: ScopeRef, requested: Claim)
  | UNSUPPORTED(requirement: Requirement, host_profile: CapabilityRef)
```

Meanings are intentionally distinct:

- `UNKNOWN`: the system lacks required facts or evidence.
- `CONFLICT`: adequately admitted evidence supports incompatible positions under the same comparison rule.
- `NOT_PROVEN`: a formal or contractual conclusion lacks a verified proof obligation.
- `OUT_OF_SCOPE`: the requested question is outside the declared contract, context, role, or observation boundary.
- `MULTIPLE_VALID`: several answers satisfy the declared criteria and no authorized selection rule chooses among them.
- `UNSUPPORTED`: the current host lacks a named local capability or permission.

None is equivalent to `false`, `null`, an empty set, or an exception. APIs may offer an explicitly named lossy projection to a boolean, but it must require a policy for every non-boolean constructor and record that policy in the resulting derivation.

Views are contextual without being relativistically opaque: the context, Protocol, source revisions, omitted evidence, and derivation are inspectable. Two views may disagree legitimately. Comparing them requires either a common frame or a context bridge; otherwise the comparison is `OUT_OF_SCOPE` or `UNKNOWN`.

# 12. Plurality and multi-Persona semantics

A Persona is a first-class, Object-rooted semantic facet:

```text
Persona {
  object_root_id: EntityId,
  persona_semantic_id: SemanticId,
  kind: TypeRef,
  claims: Set<Claim>,
  visibility_policy: PolicyRef,
  eligible_roles: Set<RoleConstraint>,
  lineage: Set<LineageEdge>
}
```

An Object may expose zero, one, or many Personas concurrently. A Persona does not own the Object, and the Object is not reducible to the union of Personas. Personas may overlap, disagree, be nested by an explicitly modeled relation, or be mutually hidden by policy. No default “true Persona” is assumed.

A Protocol binds roles to the Object, a Persona, or a declared set of Personas. The binding is part of the Protocol instance identity and context. Evidence observed through one Persona is not automatically visible through another. Any cross-Persona transfer requires a disclosure or translation contract, appropriate authority, and an event receipt.

Plural Protocol execution is also normal:

- compatible Protocols may run in a proved composition;
- unrelated Protocols may run in isolated lanes over disjoint endpoints;
- possibly conflicting Protocols may run as parallel observers;
- mutation proposals from multiple Protocols meet at the Object's authority and invariant gate;
- conflict resolution is itself a named Protocol, never an invisible priority order.

If two Protocols produce distinct valid views, the Object stores both derivations and reports `MULTIPLE_VALID` unless a context-authorized selector exists. If their claims are logically incompatible under a shared frame, the result is `CONFLICT`. Plurality is preserved in either case; one Protocol's acceptance does not erase the other.

Persona fusion, splitting, or redefinition is a semantic mutation. It creates successor Persona semantic IDs and explicit lineage. A view that spans several Personas remains a view; it does not create a synthetic fused Persona unless that separate semantic operation is authorized and evidenced.

# 13. Protocol switching and fidelity

Switching from active instance `I_P` to `I_Q` is a negotiated migration:

```text
SwitchPlan {
  source_instance: ProtocolInstanceRef,
  target_protocol: ProtocolRef,
  target_bindings: BindingSet,
  state_map: TranslationRef,
  event_map: TranslationRef,
  obligation_map: TranslationRef,
  context_map: TranslationRef,
  fidelity_claim: EXACT | OBSERVATION_PRESERVING | LOSSY | FORKED,
  proof: ProofBundle?,
  loss_report: Set<LossItem>,
  cutover_policy: PolicyRef,
  rollback_plan: SwitchPlanRef?
}
```

The switch algorithm is:

1. pin the source Protocol semantic ID, instance revision, context, causal frontier, and pending obligations;
2. admit the target Protocol independently;
3. check or classify the state, event, context, and obligation mappings;
4. dry-run target invariants and authority intersections;
5. return `MULTIPLE_VALID` if several target states satisfy the mapping with no selector;
6. return `UNKNOWN`, `NOT_PROVEN`, `CONFLICT`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, or `UNSUPPORTED` without cutover when appropriate;
7. emit `SwitchPrepared`; then atomically activate, run in parallel, fork, or refuse according to policy;
8. emit receipts that bind predecessor, successor, mappings, losses, and causal frontier.

`EXACT` fidelity requires a verified round-trip law over the declared observation algebra:

```text
project_P(back_to_P(to_Q(s, trace))) == project_P(s, trace)
```

It must also preserve all live obligations or discharge them with accepted evidence. `OBSERVATION_PRESERVING` may discard target-internal distinctions but must preserve declared source observables. `LOSSY` requires an enumerated loss report and explicit authority. `FORKED` retains both instances from the same causal frontier.

If fidelity cannot be proved, the system may still offer a lossy or parallel switch, but it cannot label it exact. A failed cutover does not pretend time was reversed: compensating events may restore a declared equivalent state, while the attempted switch and its side effects remain in history.

Suspending `I_P` does not delete it. Resumption checks that intervening Object and relation events can be replayed or mapped. If they cannot, resumption is `CONFLICT`, `UNKNOWN`, or a request for a successor instance rather than silent state reuse.

# 14. READ / VIEW / RUNTIME_MUTATION / SEMANTIC_MUTATION boundary

Every public operation declares exactly one semantic effect class:

| Class | Meaning | Target identity effect | Required checks | Mandatory record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `READ` | Retrieve stored facts, events, relations, contracts, receipts, or lineage without interpretation beyond decoding. | None. | Visibility, type decoding, pinned revision. | Audit receipt when policy requires; no domain event. |
| `VIEW` | Purely derive a contextual projection or judgment from pinned inputs. | None. | Read authority, Protocol admission for viewing, derivation validity. | Immutable `View` with context, sources, omissions, and derivation. |
| `RUNTIME_MUTATION` | Apply a transition already authorized and semantically defined by the current Object/Protocol contract. | Same `SemanticId`; increment `StateRevision`. | Authority, expected revision, endpoint precondition, invariants, idempotency. | Causally linked proposal, receipt, and domain events. |
| `SEMANTIC_MUTATION` | Change type meaning, contract clauses, role semantics, event interpretation, Persona definition, translation rule, or authority semantics. | New successor `SemanticId`; predecessor remains addressable. | Semantic authority, schema closure, re-admission, lineage, proof obligations, migration classification. | Successor artifact, lineage edges, migration/switch receipt, loss report if any. |

Examples:

- fetching an Object's stored temperature fact is `READ`;
- interpreting that fact as a warning under Protocol `P` and context `C` is `VIEW`;
- recording an authorized acknowledgement under `P`'s declared transition is `RUNTIME_MUTATION`;
- changing what “acknowledged” means, or replacing `P`'s warning threshold semantics, is `SEMANTIC_MUTATION`.

The boundary is enforced transitively. A nominal `VIEW` that calls an effectful decoder is mutation and must be rejected or reclassified. A `READ` that consults and updates semantic defaults is not a read. A runtime mutation cannot install a new schema as a side effect. Internal caches and metrics may change operational bytes, but they must be observationally irrelevant to the declared semantic state; otherwise they fall into the appropriate mutation class.

Batch operations report the effect class of each component. A mixed batch does not inherit the weakest class. Partial application returns per-component receipts and a causal record; it is never summarized as a single success boolean.

# 15. Successor identity and lineage

N08 distinguishes a lineage root, an immutable semantic identity, and a runtime revision:

- `root_id` groups a historical family without asserting semantic equivalence;
- `semantic_id` identifies one immutable meaning-bearing definition;
- `state_revision` identifies runtime state under that meaning.

A semantic artifact's `semantic_id` is derived from, or cryptographically bound to, its canonical definition and dependency digests. Any semantic mutation creates a new semantic identity. The predecessor cannot be overwritten, even when a mutable human-facing alias now points to the successor.

Lineage edges are typed claims:

```text
LineageEdge {
  edge_id: EntityId,
  kind: REFINES | SUPERSEDES | DERIVED_FROM | FORKED_FROM | MERGED_FROM |
        MIGRATED_FROM | CORRECTS | REVOKES | EQUIVALENT_UNDER,
  predecessors: NonEmptySet<SemanticId>,
  successor: SemanticId,
  context: ContextRef,
  mapping: TranslationRef?,
  claim: Claim,
  evidence: Set<EvidenceRef>,
  judgment: Judgment<LineageWitness>,
  authority: AuthorityRef,
  event: EventRef
}
```

An edge name alone proves nothing. `EQUIVALENT_UNDER` must name the observation algebra and carry a proof receipt. `REFINES` is frame-indexed. `CORRECTS` preserves the erroneous predecessor and cites the corrected claim. `REVOKES` affects trust or availability under a context; it does not erase historical occurrence.

Merges have multiple predecessors and must preserve unresolved differences. If no coherent merge exists, the result may be `CONFLICT`, `MULTIPLE_VALID`, or `NOT_PROVEN`; the system must not fabricate a successor. Forks preserve their common causal frontier. Lineage traversal returns graphs, not a presumed single chain.

Runtime revisions are monotone per semantic identity. Two concurrent children of one revision are either reconciled through an explicit runtime merge defined by the current contract or retained as branches. If reconciliation changes meaning, it is a semantic successor rather than a runtime merge.

# 16. Executable contact and conformance trace

The minimal host contact is the following typed interface. An implementation may choose another language or transport only if it preserves these observable results.

```text
interface ProtocolHost {
  admit(candidate: ProtocolEnvelope, profile: HostProfile)
    -> Judgment<AdmissionReceipt>

  bind(protocol: ProtocolRef | ProtocolVar,
       roles: BindingSet,
       context: ContextRef)
    -> Judgment<ProtocolInstanceRef>

  ingest(event: EventEnvelope)
    -> Judgment<Set<DispatchReceipt>>

  read(request: ReadRequest)
    -> Judgment<StoredValue>

  view<T>(request: ViewRequest)
    -> View<T>

  propose(proposal: ActionProposal)
    -> Judgment<ProposalReceipt>

  apply(proposal_id: EntityId,
        expected_revision: StateRevision)
    -> ActionReceipt

  switch_protocol(plan: SwitchPlan)
    -> Judgment<SwitchReceipt>

  semantic_successor(request: SemanticMutationRequest)
    -> Judgment<SuccessorReceipt>
}
```

Reference dispatch pseudocode:

```text
ingest(e):
  require verify_envelope(e)
  append_once(e.event_id, e.digest) else
    return same_digest ? ESTABLISHED(DUPLICATE) : CONFLICT(event_id_reuse)

  if missing(e.causal_parents):
    record_causal_gap(e)

  matches := subscriptions_matching(e.event_type,
                                    e.subject,
                                    e.relation,
                                    e.context)
  receipts := {}
  for i in stable_order(matches):
    snapshot := pin_readable_snapshot(i, e)
    j := step(i.protocol.semantic_id, i.runtime_revision, e, snapshot)
    receipts += persist_step_judgment(i, e, j)
  return summarize_without_boolean_collapse(receipts)
```

Minimal conformance trace for an unseen Protocol `example.net/ThresholdGate@7`:

```text
T0  Host has never seen the Protocol family name.
T1  Candidate carries K-compatible schemas, a sandbox contact, a proof receipt for
    state-invariant preservation, and a declared `observes` relation rule.
T2  admit(candidate) -> ESTABLISHED(ADMITTED_EXECUTABLE, receipt_A).
T3  bind(candidate, {sensor: O1, subject: O2}, C1) -> instance I7.
T4  relation R1(kind=`observes`, sensor=O1, subject=O2) is created.
T5  R1 creation emits E1; I7 consumes E1 and subscribes to typed `reading` events.
T6  E2(reading=11, threshold=10, parent=E1) arrives.
T7  step(I7,E2) emits proposal P1 to append an `alert-raised` fact to O2.
T8  O2 checks authority, expected revision, and invariant; apply(P1) -> APPLIED,
    emitting E3 and receipt RCP1.
T9  I7 consumes E3 and advances to `ALERTED`.
```

Required negative variants of the same trace are:

- if the proof checker is unavailable, T2 is `NOT_PROVEN` and admission may be only `ADMITTED_OBSERVABLE` under policy;
- if the contact effect set requests an unavailable capability, T2 is `UNSUPPORTED` for execution;
- if another valid threshold interpretation is present with no selector, the view at T6 is `MULTIPLE_VALID`;
- if two admitted facts assert incompatible readings under the same frame, T6 is `CONFLICT`;
- if E2 omits a required causal parent, the dependent judgment is `UNKNOWN` rather than “no alert”;
- if P1's Object revision is stale, T8 is `STALE` and no automatic reinterpretive retry occurs.

An implementation conforms only if it produces equivalent typed outcomes, receipts, events, and lineage—not merely the same happy-path value.

# 17. Invariants, failures, falsification, and unsupported Protocols

## 17.1 Required invariants

1. **Object/Protocol separation:** no Protocol transition directly rewrites Object state; it uses a typed proposal and receives a receipt.
2. **Semantic immutability:** a meaning-bearing `SemanticId` never changes definition.
3. **Runtime locality:** runtime mutation changes only state permitted by the current semantic definition and increments its revision.
4. **Context retention:** every view, proof, compatibility, refinement, switch, and lineage judgment retains its context or frame.
5. **Evidence retention:** conflict resolution, correction, and retraction do not erase admitted evidence or prior receipts.
6. **No outcome collapse:** honest result constructors remain distinguishable through storage, transport, and APIs.
7. **Typed causality:** every state-affecting event identifies its schema, producer, subject, context, and causal parents.
8. **Relation responsiveness:** qualifying relation transitions reach subscribed Protocol instances.
9. **Authority intersection:** parse, admission, proof, binding, and authority are independent gates; none silently grants another.
10. **Proof specificity:** a proof receipt establishes only its named theorem under its named checker, dependencies, and frame.
11. **Plurality preservation:** simultaneous Personas, views, and Protocols are not fused without a contracted semantic mutation.
12. **Switch honesty:** exactness is claimed only with the applicable round-trip and obligation-preservation proof.
13. **Lineage continuity:** every semantic mutation emits an addressable successor and lineage edge.
14. **Opaque restraint:** preserved opaque data is not semantically interpreted or executed without supported contact.
15. **Partial-lattice restraint:** missing meet/join/comparison is reportable and never repaired with an invented universal element.

## 17.2 Failure behavior

- **Malformed or digest-invalid envelope:** reject, preserve a failure receipt, and do not dispatch.
- **Unknown type decoder:** preserve opaquely when allowed; return `UNSUPPORTED` for semantic operations.
- **Missing proof dependency/checker:** return `NOT_PROVEN`; do not infer the theorem's negation.
- **Authority denial:** emit a refusal receipt without mutating the target.
- **Stale runtime revision:** return `STALE`; require a new observation and proposal.
- **Duplicate ID, same digest:** return `DUPLICATE` idempotently.
- **Duplicate ID, different digest:** return `CONFLICT` and quarantine both causal branches as policy directs.
- **Missing event parents:** record the event and causal gap if policy permits; dependent results remain `UNKNOWN`.
- **Protocol step timeout/resource exhaustion:** suspend or quarantine the instance and emit `UNSUPPORTED` or `UNKNOWN` according to the named bound; never fabricate `NoAction`.
- **Partial batch failure:** preserve per-item receipts and the applied prefix/branches; compensation, if any, is a new event sequence.
- **Switch failure after external effect:** emit failure and compensation events; never delete the attempted switch.
- **Lineage proof failure:** retain the proposed edge as `NOT_PROVEN` or `REJECTED`, but do not install it as established lineage.

## 17.3 Falsification battery

N08 is falsified, rather than merely inconvenienced, by any conforming-looking implementation in which one of the following can occur:

1. changing a Protocol contract in place leaves its `SemanticId` unchanged;
2. a Protocol mutates an Object without an Object-side authority/invariant decision and receipt;
3. a previously unseen, kernel-compatible Protocol cannot even be preserved or classified because its family name was not pre-enumerated;
4. an opaque/unproved Protocol receives mutation authority merely because it parsed;
5. a required relation endpoint change fails to awaken a matching active subscription;
6. `UNKNOWN`, `CONFLICT`, `NOT_PROVEN`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, or `MULTIPLE_VALID` is serialized as false, null, empty, or success without an explicit lossy policy;
7. two simultaneous Personas are silently merged by a view;
8. an exact switch fails the declared round-trip observation test or loses a live obligation;
9. a semantic mutation lacks a queryable predecessor edge or makes the predecessor unreachable;
10. a cached view can be reused across different source revisions or contexts without invalidation;
11. refinement or compatibility is asserted without its comparison frame and witness;
12. the system always returns a meet or join, even for contracts with incompatible types, effects, or observation algebras;
13. replaying the same event and digest applies its runtime effect twice;
14. a proof checker failure is presented as proof of the theorem's negation;
15. executable contact and the contract's signed conformance traces disagree while admission remains established.

## 17.4 Explicitly unsupported Protocol classes

The following are unsupported for the indicated capability unless a future, explicitly scoped extension supplies the missing boundary:

- Protocols whose identifiers, events, contexts, or lineage cannot be represented or bridged to the stable substrate;
- self-modifying Protocols that change semantics without producing successor identities and lineage;
- Protocols that conceal effects, authority requirements, or externally visible channels;
- Protocols whose only “proof” is execution of unbounded opaque code;
- Protocols requiring a proof logic or trust root the host does not support, for conclusions dependent on that proof;
- Protocols demanding context-free global comparison or a universal compatibility/top element;
- Protocols requiring destructive history erasure, evidence deletion, or contradiction suppression;
- Protocols that cannot distinguish observation from mutation at their contact boundary;
- Protocols whose safe execution depends on an unavailable oracle, scheduler, resource guarantee, secret, or hardware capability;
- Protocols requiring lossless switching while refusing to expose state, trace, context, or obligation mappings;
- Protocols whose event semantics cannot tolerate the offered delivery contract and provide no stronger transport adapter;
- higher-order Protocols that invoke unbound or uncontracted Protocol variables with mutation authority.

Unsupported classification is local and evidence-bearing. It is not a universal condemnation: another host profile, frame, checker, adapter, or future substrate version may support the Protocol, creating a new admission receipt without rewriting the earlier one.

# 18. Global questions — OPEN

Every item in this section remains **OPEN**. N08 deliberately exposes these as parameters or unresolved governance choices and does not select a global answer elsewhere in the proposal.

1. **OPEN — Kernel type floor:** What is the smallest stable typed substrate that is sufficiently durable without encoding one domain's ontology?
2. **OPEN — Canonical encoding:** Which canonicalization and digest rules should define semantic identity across runtimes and languages?
3. **OPEN — Context identity:** Which contextual facts belong inside a `ContextRef`, and what equivalence relation, if any, should contexts use?
4. **OPEN — Proof pluralism:** Which proof systems, checkers, axioms, resource bounds, and trust roots may participate in cross-host receipts?
5. **OPEN — Observation algebra:** Who defines the observables used for refinement, switch fidelity, and semantic equivalence?
6. **OPEN — Partial-lattice computability:** Which useful fragments permit decidable refinement, compatibility, meet, or join search?
7. **OPEN — Probabilistic contracts:** How should statistical, approximate, learned, or confidence-bearing Protocol guarantees enter the judgment algebra?
8. **OPEN — Temporal semantics:** Which clock, fairness, scheduler, and failure assumptions are adequate for portable liveness claims?
9. **OPEN — Resource accounting:** How should compute, storage, latency, energy, and proof-checking budgets become contract terms across hosts?
10. **OPEN — Distributed causality:** Which causal model and partition behavior should be standard for relation/event delivery?
11. **OPEN — Authority governance:** How are delegations, revocations, appeals, emergency powers, and cross-domain policies represented and reconciled?
12. **OPEN — Privacy and disclosure:** Which proof forms can establish compatibility or safety without revealing protected Object, Persona, or Protocol state?
13. **OPEN — Persona governance:** Who may create, expose, merge, split, correct, or revoke a Persona, especially when authorities disagree?
14. **OPEN — Conflict policy:** Which resolution Protocols are legitimate in a context, and how are their own conflicts represented?
15. **OPEN — Admission economics:** How should denial-of-service risks from large schemas, proof search, or conformance probes be bounded fairly?
16. **OPEN — Remote contact:** What guarantees are required when executable contact is remote, intermittently available, or controlled by another authority?
17. **OPEN — Alias semantics:** How should mutable human-facing names point to successors without being mistaken for stable semantic identity?
18. **OPEN — Retention and erasure law:** How can lineage/evidence retention coexist with legitimate deletion, privacy, safety, and jurisdictional requirements?
19. **OPEN — Cross-substrate bridges:** What constitutes an adequate proof that a bridge preserves types, effects, contexts, and epistemic outcomes?
20. **OPEN — Validation regime:** What independent reference implementations, adversarial traces, and empirical studies are necessary before any part of N08 could become normative?

Until these questions receive answers in named scopes, implementations must surface the corresponding parameter, capability limit, or honest outcome. No default chosen for convenience is a global resolution.
