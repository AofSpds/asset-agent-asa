## 1. Candidate statement

N11 is a bounded institutional runtime in which Objects and Protocols are separately typed, first-class records joined through an append-only blackboard. A Protocol does not merely process an Object: it can classify, authorize, contest, transform, or decline it, while posted Objects can in turn activate, constrain, challenge, suspend, or require a successor to that Protocol.

The blackboard admits several Protocols at once. It does not appoint one protocol as an implicit universal judge. Coexistence is permitted only when jurisdiction, authority, effect permissions, and translation boundaries are explicit. A claim may therefore be supported under one Protocol, contested under another, and undecidable under a third without being collapsed to one supposedly neutral status.

This is a concrete proposal for a finite reference implementation. It is not a claim that institutional disagreement is computable in general, that every Protocol can interoperate, or that recorded authority is legitimate.

## 2. Scope, assumptions, and non-goals

N11 targets a finite institution that can name its participants, contexts, Protocol versions, authority grants, and effect adapters. Its minimum operating assumptions are:

1. records can be content-addressed and durably ordered within one deployment;
2. a deployment can authenticate the source of a record, even if it disputes that source's authority;
3. effectful operations pass through a host-controlled boundary guard; and
4. a Protocol can be pinned to an immutable version for replay, or else be marked non-replayable.

The proposal does not supply a theory of truth, justice, identity, legitimacy, or optimal governance. It does not infer authority from popularity, technical ownership, signature possession, or protocol adoption. It does not promise a total translation between heterogeneous vocabularies. It does not erase an earlier record when a correction, withdrawal, or successor is posted.

The finite reference profile in Section 16 limits one Context to 32 active Protocol versions, 128 active Personas, 50,000 live Objects, bridge paths of length four, and 256 staged effects per transaction. Those are demonstrator limits, not proposed social constants.

## 3. Core vocabulary and identifiers

The proposal uses the following typed identifiers:

| Identifier | Names | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `oid` | Object record | A stable, immutable content-bearing item or reference |
| `pid@version` | Protocol record | Executable/evaluable rules pinned to one immutable version |
| `cid` | Context snapshot | The bounded situation in which interpretation or action occurs |
| `persona_id` | Persona | A declared speaking/acting position, separate from legal identity |
| `grant_id` | Authority grant | A scoped permission from a named source, not proof of legitimacy |
| `bridge_id@version` | Bridge | A directional, possibly partial mapping between Protocols |
| `eid` | Effect record | A proposed, committed, failed, compensated, or declined consequence |
| `rid` | Run receipt | The inputs, versions, decisions, and outputs of one R/E run |

`Protocol` is capitalized for the first-class model entity. Lowercase “protocol” refers informally to a convention or procedure outside the registered entity. Objects, Protocols, Contexts, Bridges, Authority Grants, Personas, Effects, and Receipts are distinct record kinds even when they share the same storage substrate.

There are three independent relations that must not be conflated:

- **provenance**: “record B cites or derives from record A”;
- **authority**: “grant G permits persona X to attempt effect Y in scope S”; and
- **semantic interpretation**: “Protocol P assigns interpretation I to Object O in Context C.”

Possessing provenance does not confer authority. Possessing authority does not make a claim true. A semantic interpretation does not by itself permit an external action.

## 4. Object Model

An Object is an immutable envelope with mutable meaning represented only by additional records:

```text
Object {
  oid: Hash,
  kind: Claim | Commitment | Evidence | Dissent | Decision |
        AuthorityGrant | ResourceRef | Observation | ProtocolNotice,
  payload: Bytes | TypedValue | ExternalReference,
  issuer: persona_id,
  issued_at: LogicalTime,
  provenance: [oid],
  signatures: [Signature],
  visibility: Public | ContextMembers | NamedSet | Sealed,
  retention_class: Ephemeral | Case | Institutional,
  supersedes: oid?,
  schema_ref: URI?
}
```

The Object envelope never stores a single mutable `truth` field. Interpretive state is posted as an Assessment:

```text
Assessment {
  target: oid,
  protocol: pid@version,
  context: cid,
  assessor: persona_id | ProtocolActor,
  status: LocalStatus,
  reasons: [oid],
  confidence: Number | Interval | Unspecified,
  dissent_refs: [oid],
  run: rid
}
```

Claims, commitments, evidence, dissent, and authority are therefore not comments hidden in metadata. They are addressable Objects with provenance and may themselves be assessed. `supersedes` records correction or replacement but does not delete the superseded Object.

Object identity is content identity for the envelope, not metaphysical identity of the thing described. Two Protocols may co-refer, split, or merge described entities only through explicit Interpretation or Bridge records. A ResourceRef points outside the blackboard and carries freshness and dereference policy; its external target is not assumed immutable.

## 5. First-class variable Protocol Model

A Protocol is data that can be selected, inspected, versioned, constrained, and invoked. It is not compiled invisibly into the host. The minimum descriptor is:

```text
Protocol {
  id: pid@version,
  predecessor: pid@version?,
  maintainers: [persona_id],
  vocabulary: SchemaBundle,
  accepted_object_kinds: Set<ObjectKind>,
  admission_rules: RuleSet,
  interpretation_rules: RuleSet | ExecutableModule,
  local_status_system: StatusDescriptor,
  authority_requirements: RuleSet,
  permitted_effect_classes: Set<EffectClass>,
  context_requirements: Schema,
  resource_budget: Budget,
  determinism: Deterministic | Seeded | DeclaredNondeterministic,
  bridge_exports: [ExportDescriptor],
  switch_contract: SwitchDescriptor,
  conformance_vectors: [TestVector],
  signature: Signature,
  lifecycle: Proposed | Admitted | Active | Suspended | Retired | Quarantined
}
```

Protocol variables are resolved per run, not at build time. An R/E request may name one Protocol, a set, or a selection policy. The resulting receipt pins the versions actually used. Selection policies are themselves registered Objects with an issuer and authority scope; they cannot masquerade as neutral host behavior.

The host provides only a small safety kernel: schema isolation, resource accounting, receipt generation, boundary enforcement, and effect mediation. A hosted Protocol may define its own vocabulary, local status algebra, evidence tests, and decision rules. It cannot expand its authority grant, conceal its selected version, or directly call an external effect adapter.

## 6. Institutional blackboard and canonical records

The blackboard is an ordered log plus derived indexes. Its canonical entries are `Post`, `Assessment`, `Challenge`, `Grant`, `Run`, `Effect`, `Switch`, and `Lineage`. Indexes by Object, Protocol, Context, Persona, and status are disposable projections; the ordered entries and referenced payload hashes are the replay source.

Writes use optimistic concurrency on a Context revision. A transaction either appends its complete institutional record set or appends an explicit partial-failure receipt. Externally irreversible effects cannot be rolled back by changing the log; compensation is a new Effect whose relationship to the failed or completed Effect is explicit.

The blackboard is polycentric in a precise sense: several admitted Protocols may publish Assessments over the same Object, and no host field selects one Assessment as `the result`. A Context may designate a decision Protocol for a limited matter. That designation is an AuthorityGrant-backed fact in the log, is time-bounded, and does not remove rival assessments or dissent.

## 7. Context Model and boundaries

An interpretation or effect is invalid in N11 unless it pins a Context snapshot:

```text
Context {
  cid: Hash,
  jurisdiction: [NamedScope],
  matter: [TopicRef],
  participants: [persona_id],
  roles: Map<persona_id, [Role]>,
  resources: [ResourceRef],
  active_protocols: [pid@version],
  active_bridges: [bridge_id@version],
  authority_grants: [grant_id],
  valid_during: TimeInterval,
  confidentiality: PolicyRef,
  risk_mode: Observe | Simulate | Commit,
  parent_context: cid?,
  revision: Integer
}
```

Authority is evaluated as an intersection, not an accumulation: the requested effect must fit the Context, an unexpired grant, the acting Persona's capacity, the Protocol's permitted effect classes, and the host boundary policy. A larger claim made by any one component does not widen that intersection.

The authority scope tuple is `(jurisdiction, matter, actors, resource, time, effect_class, magnitude)`. Missing dimensions default to no power rather than unlimited power. Conflicting grants yield `authority_disputed`; operational and constitutional effects are blocked until a named resolution rule resolves the conflict. Read-only R-path execution may continue while showing the conflict.

Parent and child Contexts do not inherit authority automatically. Inheritance requires a delegation Object specifying which dimensions narrow or remain equal. A child Context cannot widen its parent's grant.

## 8. Honest status and protected plurality

N11 supplies a transport vocabulary, not a mandatory truth lattice:

`asserted`, `supported`, `contested`, `refuted`, `withdrawn`, `expired`, `undetermined`, `incomparable`, and `opaque`.

A Protocol maps its local statuses to zero or more transport statuses and declares whether the mapping is exact, approximate, or unavailable. The local status remains primary in its Assessment. If no honest mapping exists, the transport status is `opaque`; the host does not invent one.

Plurality is preserved by the key `(target, protocol, context, assessor, run)`. A view may summarize Assessments, but the view must return the aggregation rule, omitted records, and dissent count. The following are prohibited projection shortcuts:

- converting absence of support into refutation;
- converting a majority count into authority;
- selecting the newest Assessment without showing superseded dissent;
- averaging incomparable confidence measures; and
- presenting a bridge's lossy output as an exact source meaning.

A Dissent Object identifies its target, grounds, applicable Context, preferred remedy, and whether it disputes facts, inference, authority, meaning, procedure, or effect. Grounds may be sealed for safety, in which case the public record reports that sealed grounds exist and who may inspect them. Dissent is preserved but is not an automatic veto unless the active authority rule grants it that force.

## 9. Personas and worldviews

A Persona is a declared institutional standpoint through which a participant speaks or acts. One authenticated person or agent may hold several Personas, and several participants may be authorized to occupy a shared office Persona. The binding is recorded separately and may be contested.

```text
Persona {
  persona_id: Hash,
  controller_refs: [IdentityRef],
  capacities: [Role],
  worldview: WorldviewDescriptor,
  protocol_preferences: [pid@version],
  trust_rules: [RuleRef],
  disclosure_policy: PolicyRef,
  delegation_refs: [grant_id],
  valid_during: TimeInterval
}
```

A WorldviewDescriptor may declare ontology commitments, accepted evidence types, risk posture, temporal horizon, and non-negotiable constraints. It is an assertion by or about a Persona, not a complete psychological model. Competing worldview descriptions may coexist and be assessed by different Protocols.

The finite demonstrator includes four example Personas: `claimant`, `respondent`, `steward`, and `auditor`. These are examples, not exhaustive human categories. Switching Persona changes the applicable capacities and worldview declaration; it does not silently change Object history or transfer another Persona's authority.

## 10. R/E response contract

N11 realizes the R/E requirement as two explicitly tagged paths that share one envelope.

- **R-path**: represent/read/recognize. It is side-effect-free and returns protocol-relative interpretations, statuses, disagreement, provenance, and uncertainty.
- **E-path**: evaluate/enact. It first produces an effect plan; it may simulate or commit that plan only after authority and boundary checks.

```text
RERequest {
  mode: R | E,
  objects: [oid],
  context: cid,
  protocol_selector: pid@version | [pid@version] | SelectionPolicyRef,
  persona: persona_id,
  requested_effects: [EffectIntent]?,
  risk_mode: Observe | Simulate | Commit,
  nonce: Bytes
}

REResponse {
  run: rid,
  pinned_context: cid,
  pinned_protocols: [pid@version],
  interpretations: [Interpretation],
  assessments: [Assessment],
  dissent: [oid],
  authority_findings: [Finding],
  uncertainties: [Gap],
  effect_plan: [eid],
  outcome: Answered | Plural | Undetermined | Incomparable |
           Declined | Quarantined | Failed,
  provenance_roots: [oid],
  replay_receipt: Receipt
}
```

`Answered` does not mean true; it means the selected Protocols produced a response within their declared domain. `Plural` is a successful response when incompatible outputs are faithfully retained. `Declined`, `Undetermined`, `Incomparable`, and `Quarantined` are honest terminal statuses, not exceptions to be coerced into an answer.

An E-path `Commit` response is issued only after effect adapters return durable acknowledgements or explicit failures. A timeout returns `Failed` plus an uncertain-effect marker; retry requires the same idempotency key and cannot be reported as a clean non-event.

## 11. Bidirectional Object–Protocol interaction

The interaction is intentionally two-way:

| Direction | Mechanism | Permitted result |
|---|---|---|
| Object → Protocol | admission and interpretation | accept, reject, quarantine, classify, or decline the Object |
| Protocol → Object | Assessment or derived Object | attach a local status, derivation, obligation proposal, or transformation |
| Object → Protocol | Challenge or ProtocolNotice | request review, expose failed test, propose suspension, or propose a successor |
| Protocol → Context | effect plan | request a scoped epistemic, deontic, operational, or constitutional change |
| Context → Protocol | activation and boundary guard | activate, constrain, suspend, budget, or deny effect authority |
| Effect → Protocol | outcome receipt | supply real-world success, failure, or uncertainty as a new input |

One evaluation cycle is finite:

1. pin the Object set, Context revision, Persona, and Protocol versions;
2. validate schemas and visibility rights;
3. run each Protocol in its declared resource budget;
4. append its Assessments and declared uncertainty without merging them;
5. resolve authority for each proposed Effect through the boundary guard;
6. in `Observe` or `Simulate`, stop after the plan; in `Commit`, dispatch allowed effects;
7. append adapter receipts, compensation needs, dissent, and the run receipt; and
8. expose outcome Effects as Objects eligible for later Protocol runs.

Feedback cannot rewrite the Protocol that generated it during the same run. A meaning-changing update follows successor lineage in Section 15 and becomes eligible only in a later run. This prevents a Protocol from retrospectively changing the rules that explain its own receipt.

## 12. Unseen and heterogeneous Protocols

An unseen Protocol enters through a signed manifest and one of three adapters: declarative rules, deterministic bytecode, or a remote evaluator with a recorded transport contract. Admission runs a finite suite:

1. descriptor and schema validation;
2. resource-limit enforcement;
3. unknown-Object behavior;
4. local-to-transport status disclosure;
5. authority-boundary denial test;
6. effect-plan serialization;
7. replay or declared-nondeterminism test; and
8. switch export and lineage test.

Passing admits only the tested descriptor version; it does not certify correctness, fairness, or safety. Failure yields `quarantined` with test receipts. A quarantined Protocol may be stored and inspected but cannot generate committed effects.

Heterogeneity is represented rather than hidden. Bridges are directional functions with domain, codomain, preconditions, loss markers, author, version, and authority scope. `A→B` does not imply `B→A`. A Bridge may map only a subset of Object kinds or local statuses. Unmapped material remains attached as an explicit gap.

If a Protocol lacks a compatible adapter, N11 can transport its manifest and outputs as `opaque` Objects. It cannot interpret or enact them. This is a supported refusal mode, not evidence that the unseen Protocol is defective.

## 13. Coexistence and Protocol switching

Coexistence uses a Context-local topology: active Protocol nodes, directional Bridge edges, and an authority overlay. Each node keeps its own Assessments. Cross-protocol queries traverse only admitted Bridges and report path, versions, and loss at each hop. Bridge paths longer than the configured limit are declined rather than approximated silently.

A switch from `P@a` to `Q@b` is a recorded handoff, not mutation:

1. create `SwitchIntent(P@a, Q@b, cid, persona, reason)`;
2. verify authority to change the active Protocol set for the named matter;
3. freeze the source input frontier and export through a named Bridge, if one exists;
4. run `Q@b` in `Simulate` and produce a semantic/effect diff;
5. classify unmapped Objects, statuses, commitments, and authority grants;
6. solicit endorsements or dissent required by the Context's switch rule;
7. append `Activated`, `Forked`, or `Declined`; and
8. retain `P@a`, its receipts, and unresolved obligations for audit.

`Activated` is allowed only when blocking gaps are absent under the declared switch rule. `Forked` keeps both Protocols active for different scopes or constituencies. Existing commitments transfer only through an explicit assumption Object accepted by the destination authority rule. No switch converts descriptive semantic similarity into legal or institutional succession by itself.

Rapid per-request switching is allowed for R-path comparison. E-path switching is locked per transaction, so an actor cannot choose one Protocol for authorization and another for accountability after seeing the outcome.

## 14. Four effect classes

Every effect has exactly one primary class and may cite secondary implications. Mixed effects are decomposed before authorization.

| Class | Changes | Examples | Minimum gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EPISTEMIC` | Blackboard interpretations | add Assessment, flag uncertainty, register dissent | write permission and provenance |
| `DEONTIC` | Institutional positions | create/accept/waive a commitment, permission, prohibition, or duty | scoped role plus applicable rule |
| `OPERATIONAL` | External state or resources | send instruction, transfer allocation, actuate service, publish notice | adapter capability, grant, idempotency, risk check |
| `CONSTITUTIONAL` | Rules of rule-making | admit/suspend Protocol, change authority topology, approve Bridge, alter switch rule | named constitutional grant and threshold |

An Effect record contains `eid`, intent hash, primary class, actor Persona, generating Protocol, Context, authority findings, preconditions, idempotency key, adapter, status, receipt, and compensation link. Its lifecycle is `proposed → authorized → staged → committed | failed | uncertain | declined`, with `compensated` recorded as a linked later effect rather than a rewrite.

The host rejects undeclared secondary implications discovered before commit and records the rejection. Implications discovered after commit become Observation Objects and may trigger compensation, review, or a constitutional challenge; they are not retroactively erased.

## 15. Semantic change and successor lineage

Protocol records are immutable. A change to vocabulary meaning, admissibility, inference, status semantics, authority requirements, permitted effects, switch behavior, or bridge exports creates a new `pid@version` with a `predecessor` link. The successor declaration contains:

- change set and rationale;
- authoring and approval Personas;
- affected Object and Context classes;
- expected status and effect differences;
- migration Bridges and their loss claims;
- regression and counterexample vectors; and
- effective interval plus coexistence or retirement plan.

Formatting, documentation, or runtime packaging changes may produce a new build digest under the same semantic version only if the conformance vectors and executable semantics are byte-identical after canonicalization. Otherwise a successor version is required.

Lineage can branch. Two successors may cite the same predecessor, and neither becomes canonical without a Context-scoped constitutional decision. Merging branches creates another successor with two lineage parents and explicit conflict mappings. Historical Assessments remain pinned to their original versions. Re-evaluation under a successor creates new Assessments and a diff receipt; it does not relabel the old run.

An Object whose meaning depends on a retired Protocol remains readable through that Protocol's archived descriptor or is reported `non-replayable`. Retirement removes activation eligibility, not history.

## 16. Reference implementation

The finite implementation has seven components:

1. **Append log** — immutable records, hashes, signatures, and Context revisions.
2. **Projection store** — replaceable indexes and plurality-preserving query views.
3. **Protocol registry** — descriptors, versions, lifecycle, conformance receipts, and lineage.
4. **Sandbox runner** — deterministic or seeded execution with CPU, memory, time, and output caps.
5. **Boundary guard** — authority-scope intersection and effect-class enforcement outside Protocol code.
6. **Effect coordinator** — simulation, idempotent dispatch, receipt collection, and compensation links.
7. **Bridge/switch service** — partial mappings, diffs, gaps, activation locks, and lineage records.

The demonstrator uses a single ordered append log, content-addressed blob store, relational projection tables, and a capability-isolated worker process. Canonical JSON with schema hashes is sufficient for the wire format. A production deployment could substitute storage and sandbox technologies while retaining the record contract.

Reference caps are: 32 active Protocol versions per Context; 128 Personas; 50,000 live Objects; 1,000,000 evaluator steps; 64 MiB evaluator memory; 5 seconds wall time for a local evaluator; 4 Bridge hops; 256 Effects per transaction; and 16 MiB total response payload. A cap breach returns `Declined(resource_limit)` with a receipt.

Minimum APIs are `post_object`, `register_protocol`, `admit_protocol`, `run_re`, `challenge`, `propose_switch`, `commit_switch`, `query_plural`, `stage_effects`, `commit_effects`, and `trace_lineage`. Mutating APIs require idempotency keys and expected Context revisions.

## 17. Invariants, failures, falsification, and unsupported claims

### 17.1 Required invariants

For the bounded runtime to conform to N11:

1. **Pinned interpretation:** each Assessment cites one Object, Context snapshot, Protocol version, assessor, and run.
2. **No silent overwrite:** correction, withdrawal, compensation, switch, and semantic change append records.
3. **Plurality preservation:** incompatible Assessments remain retrievable; summaries disclose their selection rule and omissions.
4. **Power intersection:** a committed effect has a matching Context, grant, Persona capacity, Protocol permission, and host-boundary approval.
5. **Effect mediation:** Protocol code cannot directly invoke an external adapter.
6. **Dissent traceability:** a cited Dissent Object remains reachable from the target or its successor view.
7. **Bridge honesty:** partial, approximate, lossy, and failed mappings are labeled in the response.
8. **Lineage on meaning change:** a semantic change yields a successor and never changes the bytes or interpretation receipt of its predecessor.
9. **Persona separation:** switching Persona does not transfer grants, commitments, or authorship without an explicit delegation or assumption record.
10. **Replay honesty:** a run either reproduces from pinned inputs or declares why it is non-replayable.

### 17.2 Anticipated failures and bounded responses

| Failure | Detection | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown or malformed Protocol | admission suite | quarantine; permit opaque inspection only |
| Missing/stale Context | hash or revision mismatch | decline and name required snapshot |
| Authority conflict | incompatible scoped grants | show `authority_disputed`; block operational/constitutional commit |
| Bridge gap | unmapped item or status | retain source; emit gap; decline blocking switch |
| Protocol timeout/exhaustion | sandbox meter | terminate run; append resource-limit receipt |
| Nondeterministic divergence | replay test | mark declared/observed divergence; prohibit deterministic claim |
| Persona capture or impersonation | binding challenge/revocation | suspend affected capacity; preserve earlier receipts as contested |
| Blackboard flooding | quota and rate policy | throttle by disclosed rule; record excluded range in summaries |
| Partial external effect | missing/ambiguous adapter receipt | mark `uncertain`; lock blind retry; open reconciliation |
| Circular assessment/challenge | dependency-cycle check | preserve cycle; stop derivation at configured depth |
| Host compromise | signature/log verification failure | stop commits; export verifiable records; no claim of internal recovery |

### 17.3 Falsification tests

The proposal is falsified as implemented if any of these tests succeeds:

- changing a Protocol version retroactively changes an old run receipt;
- two contrary Assessments are queried but the API returns only one without disclosing the filter;
- an operational adapter is reached without the full authority-scope intersection;
- a lossy Bridge is presented as exact or its source path cannot be reconstructed;
- a Persona switch inherits an unmentioned authority grant;
- a semantic rule change occurs without a successor lineage record;
- a quarantined Protocol commits an Effect;
- an E-path retry duplicates an idempotent external effect while reporting one commit; or
- an `undetermined`, `incomparable`, or `opaque` result is coerced to `supported` or `refuted` by the host.

A pilot should additionally measure replay rate, undisclosed omission rate, blocked unauthorized effect attempts, unresolved Bridge gaps, switch rollback/compensation incidence, and time to surface dissent. Thresholds require a deployment-specific validation plan and are not supplied by this frozen proposal.

### 17.4 Explicitly unsupported

N11 does not currently support or establish: universal Protocol translation; globally unique real-world identity; legitimacy of recorded authority; fairness of Protocol outputs; correctness of evidence; privacy guarantees against a compromised host; deletion-compatible provenance; Byzantine consensus across independent deployments; automatic resolution of genuine worldview conflict; total termination for arbitrary evaluator code; or semantic equivalence inferred only from matching outputs.

Claims about safety, scalability beyond the finite profile, political acceptability, and human usability remain unvalidated. The state header is controlling: this document is a frozen, non-normative proposal, not a deployed standard.

## 18. Global OPEN questions preserved

N11 deliberately leaves project-level uncertainty open. A mechanism below is not treated as an answer to the normative question around it.

1. **OPEN — Protocol boundary:** What distinguishes a first-class Protocol from an Object, policy, workflow, institution, or host safety rule without privileging one ontology?
2. **OPEN — Meta-power:** Who may admit, suspend, or replace Protocols and Bridges, and by what authority when the authority model itself is disputed?
3. **OPEN — Cross-worldview comparison:** Can outcomes from incompatible status systems be compared without installing a hidden super-Protocol?
4. **OPEN — Semantic equivalence:** What evidence is sufficient to call a switch meaning-preserving, especially when downstream effects have not yet occurred?
5. **OPEN — Bridge accountability:** Who bears responsibility for omissions and losses in a directional Bridge, including harms visible only to a minority Persona?
6. **OPEN — Dissent and liveness:** How long may unresolved dissent block action, and which emergency exceptions are legitimate rather than convenient?
7. **OPEN — Provenance and erasure:** How should durable auditability coexist with privacy, safety, sealed evidence, and lawful deletion duties?
8. **OPEN — Unseen Protocol minimum:** What is the least interface an unseen Protocol must expose before coexistence is meaningful rather than opaque colocation?
9. **OPEN — External truth:** How should the runtime reconcile contested or unavailable observations of real-world effect completion?
10. **OPEN — Validation:** Which institutions, adversarial cases, affected Personas, and longitudinal measures are adequate to validate or reject this candidate?

These questions remain OPEN after N11. Future answers that alter meaning, authority, or effect semantics require a successor document and explicit lineage rather than silent revision of this frozen proposal.
