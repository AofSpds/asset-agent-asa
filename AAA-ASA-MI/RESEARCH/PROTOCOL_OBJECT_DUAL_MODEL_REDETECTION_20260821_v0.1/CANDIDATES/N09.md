## 1. Candidate thesis

N09 models the world as a **typed multiset of terms inside nested membranes**, changed by provenance-bearing rewrite rules. Objects and Protocols are both first-class terms, but they are not collapsed into one type: an Object supplies identity, state, relations, events, and lineage; a Protocol supplies variable rules, observations, permissions, status predicates, and conflict policy. A membrane names the locality in which a rule may inspect or rewrite terms.

The central configuration is:

\[
\mathcal C = \langle B, M, O, P, R, E, A, V, X, L \rangle
\]

where `B` is the remaining execution budget, `M` membranes, `O` object versions, `P` Protocol versions, `R` relations, `E` events, `A` authorities/personas, `V` materialized views, `X` evidence (including conflicts), and `L` the append-only transition ledger. A run is a finite rewrite sequence `C0 => … => Cn` ending because no enabled rule remains, the budget is exhausted, or an explicit failure/quiescence term is produced.

This is a bounded candidate, not a universal ontology or a claim that every Protocol can be interpreted.

## 2. Commitments, scope, and non-claims

N09 commits to the following:

1. **Locality:** every observation or mutation occurs through a membrane and declared ports.
2. **Protocol variability:** a Protocol is data with rules; it is never merely a fixed evaluator built into the Object.
3. **Bidirectionality:** Protocol rules may respond to Objects, relations, and events, while Objects may expose, reject, negotiate, or react to Protocol contact.
4. **Evidence before resolution:** overlapping enabled rules create explicit critical-pair evidence; resolution is itself a visible rule application.
5. **Finite claims:** execution and conformance claims are parameterized by explicit bounds.
6. **No silent coercion:** unknown syntax, missing authority, projection loss, and unsupported effects become terms in the configuration.

N09 does **not** claim semantic completeness, universal Protocol translation, global confluence, termination beyond a stated bound, a unique true view, automatic trust in provenance, or equivalence between absence of a found counterexample and proof.

## 3. Core vocabulary and typed configuration

The minimal sorts are:

```text
ObjectKey, VersionID, ProtocolKey, ProtocolVersionID, MembraneID
PersonaID, RelationID, EventID, RuleID, EvidenceID, TraceID
Term, Predicate, Effect, Capability, Budget, Clock, Provenance
```

Principal terms are:

```text
obj(ok, vid, kind, payload, ports, claims, clock)
proto(pk, pvid, language, rules, views, statusRules, policy, caps)
rel(rid, relKind, endpoints, payload, clock)
event(eid, eventKind, source, payload, clock, consumedBy)
persona(pid, principal, grants, preferences, disclosure, clock)
mem(mid, parent, imports, exports, contents)
view(viewID, subjectVID, pvid, context, persona, result, support, loss)
evidence(evidenceID, kind, subject, body, provenance)
failure(failureID, kind, subject, body)
```

Terms form multisets; order has no meaning unless represented explicitly by a clock, sequence, or causal edge. All keys and version identifiers are opaque atoms. Payload equality never establishes identity. Each rule has the shape:

```text
[ruleID @ provenance]
membrane-pattern + guard  =>  replacement + emitted-evidence
requires capabilities; costs k; observes footprint; writes footprint
```

The ledger records the exact pre-state digest, instantiated rule identifier, substitutions, consumed and produced term identifiers, authority, budget cost, post-state digest, and parent transition(s).

## 4. Explicit Object Model

An Object version is:

\[
O_v = (ok, vid, kind, payload, ports, claims, relations, eventCursor, clock).
\]

- `ok : ObjectKey` names the enduring lineage anchor; it is not reused across unrelated lineages.
- `vid : VersionID` names one exact immutable version.
- `kind` is an asserted classifier, not privileged truth.
- `payload` is typed state addressable by paths.
- `ports` declare readable surfaces, accepted event kinds, proposed mutations, and response rules.
- `claims` is a multiset of attributed assertions, denials, unknowns, and disputes.
- relations are first-class `rel` terms, not embedded pointers.
- `eventCursor` prevents accidental re-consumption while permitting declared replay.
- `clock` is logical/causal metadata; wall-clock order is not assumed sufficient.

An Object is never mutated in place. A successful state-changing rule consumes `obj(ok, vid, …)` and produces `obj(ok, vid2, …)` plus `succeeds(vid2, vid, transitionID)`. Old versions remain addressable through the ledger or an explicitly declared archival store. Object ports may themselves contain rewrite rules, so contact can produce an Object-originated response such as `accept`, `reject`, `counteroffer`, `emit`, or `requestEvidence`.

## 5. First-class variable Protocol Model

A Protocol version is:

\[
P_q = (pk, pvid, language, signature, rules, views, statusRules,
       conflictPolicy, capabilities, imports, tests, provenance).
\]

`rules` are inspectable terms, not hidden callbacks. A Protocol can be passed through a port, stored, related to an Object, viewed by another Protocol, forked, versioned, accepted, refused, or rewritten subject to semantic-mutation authority. Protocol variables range over Protocol terms:

```text
P : Protocol where supports(P.language, membrane.interpreters)
```

A rule may quantify over `P` without knowing its key in advance, but only declared syntax and effects become executable. Protocol state, if any, is explicit and versioned. A Protocol never gains ambient access merely by being present in a membrane.

Protocol meaning is operationally represented by its declared rules plus interpreter/version, imports, and capability contract. Two Protocol terms with textually identical rules but different interpreter semantics or imports are not assumed equivalent.

## 6. Bidirectional Protocol–Object interaction

Interaction is a rendezvous mediated by compatible ports:

```text
offer(contactID, pvid, objectPort, requestedMode, context, persona)
response(contactID, vid, accept | reject | counteroffer | needEvidence, terms)
session(contactID, vid, pvid, negotiatedCaps, context, persona, budget)
```

A generic two-sided contact requires both a Protocol-side rule and an Object-port response to be enabled. Creation of `session` consumes neither party; later rewrites use exact `vid` and `pvid`. This makes stale contact detectable.

Protocol-to-Object flow includes reading declared paths, requesting views, proposing effects, asserting status under the Protocol, and subscribing to relation/event patterns. Object-to-Protocol flow includes emitting accepted events, restricting disclosure, demanding evidence, challenging a status derivation, and proposing an alternate Protocol or capability set. A refusal is an observable outcome, not an engine error.

Feedback cannot occur invisibly: any Protocol term produced in response to an Object must be a new Protocol version or declared runtime state, and any Object change must be a successor version. Contact itself is recorded even when it is rejected or times out.

## 7. Relation and Event responsiveness

Relations are n-ary, version-addressed terms:

```text
rel(rid, kind, [(role1, vid1), …, (roleN, vidN)], payload, clock)
```

Rules declare a relation footprint, including endpoint roles and whether exact versions or lineage heads are acceptable. When an endpoint advances, N09 does not silently retarget the relation. A separate `rebaseRelation` rule must produce a successor relation and evidence for its justification.

Events are immutable occurrences. Event-responsive rules match on event kind, source identity/version, causal clock, membrane, subscription, and replay policy. A rule that consumes an event adds its `(ruleID, pvid)` to `consumedBy`; a non-consuming observer records an observation edge. Events may create relations, invalidate views, enable Object responses, or advance Protocol runtime state.

Concurrent relation/event matches are evaluated as candidate rewrites. If their read/write footprints overlap, the executor constructs a critical-pair record before choosing, branching, deferring, or rejecting them. Arrival order alone is never presented as semantic necessity unless a Protocol explicitly declares it.

## 8. Heterogeneous and previously unseen Protocols

An unseen Protocol enters through an intake membrane as opaque bytes plus a manifest. Intake proceeds in explicit stages:

1. parse under a named language/interpreter version;
2. validate signature, sorts, imports, declared effects, and capability needs;
3. lower to the N09 rule intermediate representation where possible;
4. generate a support report per rule and view;
5. admit fully, admit a declared subset, keep opaque for READ-only inspection, or quarantine.

Heterogeneous Protocols coexist in distinct membranes and interact through bridge rules. A bridge is itself a versioned Protocol with provenance, loss declarations, tests, and an explicit direction. `bridge(Pa, Pb)` does not imply `bridge(Pb, Pa)` or semantic equivalence.

Previously unseen Protocols are never executed by analogy to familiar ones. Unknown predicates, nondeterministic external calls, undeclared state, unbounded recursion, incompatible identity models, or effects without a capability mapping remain unsupported terms. Partial support reports exactly which rules were lowered; it must not label the whole Protocol supported.

## 9. Contextual views

A view is a derived artifact identified by all semantic inputs:

\[
ViewKey = H(vid, pvid, context, personaVersion, projectionVersion,
            relevantRelations, relevantEvents, interpreterVersion).
\]

`context` is a typed finite term containing locale, purpose, membrane, temporal cut, disclosed relation radius, selected alternatives, and any Protocol-specific parameters. A projection rule produces `view(...)` with:

- the exact Object and Protocol versions;
- result terms;
- supporting rule applications and source paths;
- omitted/redacted fields;
- a loss ledger;
- unresolved conflicts and unknowns;
- the budget and stop reason.

Views are cached only under `ViewKey`. Events or successor terms invalidate dependent cache entries through recorded dependencies. Contextual difference is expected: distinct views may disagree without either being overwritten. No view mutates its source merely by being materialized.

## 10. Honest status and plurality

Status is an attributed judgment:

```text
status(subjectVID, predicate, value, pvid, context, support, confidenceKind)
```

`value` is one of `affirmed`, `denied`, `unknown`, `inapplicable`, or `contested{alternatives}`. `confidenceKind` distinguishes proof under a finite rule set, bounded-search evidence, imported assertion, heuristic result, and user/persona assertion. These categories cannot be silently promoted.

Conflicting statuses coexist as a plurality. A Protocol may define a visible aggregation rule, but the aggregated status retains links to all inputs and the applied policy. Lack of a derivation yields `unknown`, not `denied`. Budget exhaustion yields `unknown(budget-exhausted)` unless the applicable logic justifies a stronger result independently.

The engine reports operational facts—such as parse success, enabled rules, explored traces, and critical pairs—separately from domain claims. Provenance establishes origin and transformation history, not truth.

## 11. Multi-Persona authority and participation

A Persona is a scoped, versioned participation term, not an Object identity:

```text
persona(pid, personaVersion, principalRef, grants, preferences,
        disclosureRules, commitments, clock)
```

Multiple Personas may act in one session. Every rule application identifies the active Persona set and the grant that authorized it. A joint action requires an explicit quorum/combination rule; grants are not unioned by default. Persona preferences may select among already-permitted alternatives but cannot create capabilities.

Statuses, views, and events preserve the Persona under which they were produced. Persona disagreement creates parallel proposals or `contested` evidence rather than last-writer-wins. Changing Persona never changes Object identity, Protocol identity, or historical authorship.

## 12. Switching fidelity

Switching may change Persona, Protocol, context, interpreter, membrane, or any combination. A switch is a rewrite producing:

```text
switch(oldSession, newSession, mapping, preserved, dropped,
       reinterpreted, obligations, evidence)
```

Before switching, the executor freezes an exact source checkpoint: `vid`, `pvid`, Protocol runtime version, event cursor, active alternatives, pending offers, capabilities, causal clock, and remaining budget. The target session starts only after a mapping report is materialized.

Fidelity is stated component-wise:

- `exact`: identifier and term preserved byte-for-byte;
- `observational-within(T, B)`: no difference found for test set `T` within bound `B`;
- `translated(loss)`: a declared bridge was used and loss is enumerated;
- `reinterpreted`: source terms retained but target meaning differs;
- `dropped(reason)`;
- `unknown`.

Round-trip checks (`A -> B -> A`) compare exact checkpoints and declared observations. Failure produces evidence and does not roll forward silently. N09 never calls bounded observational agreement full semantic equivalence.

## 13. Access and mutation modes

Every session grants a subset of four non-interchangeable modes:

| Mode | Permitted effect | Required output | Forbidden shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| `READ` | Inspect declared raw terms/metadata | read evidence with exact versions and redactions | deriving a contextual presentation and calling it raw |
| `VIEW` | Materialize derived contextual terms | view support, context, Protocol, Persona, loss, stop reason | mutating source or hiding unresolved alternatives |
| `RUNTIME_MUTATION` | Advance runtime state under unchanged Protocol semantics | successor runtime/Object version, events, rule provenance | editing rules, signatures, status meaning, or conflict policy |
| `SEMANTIC_MUTATION` | Change Protocol rules, schemas, projections, interpreters, bridges, or meaning-bearing Object port rules | new Protocol/Object semantic version, migration/compatibility report, lineage edges | treating the change as ordinary runtime state |

Mode checks occur both when a rule is admitted and when it fires. A rule declaring `READ` but possessing a nonempty write footprint is rejected. Mixed operations require every implicated grant; splitting one semantic change into several runtime rewrites does not downgrade it.

## 14. Successor exact identity and lineage

`VersionID` is exact identity for an immutable version. `ObjectKey` is a lineage anchor, not an interchangeable identifier for every state. The only ordinary successor constructor is:

```text
successor(newVID, priorVID, objectKey, transitionID,
          ruleID, pvid, personaVersions, causes, preDigest, postDigest)
```

Required conditions are:

1. `newVID != priorVID` and `newVID` has never appeared;
2. both versions carry the same `ObjectKey` for continuation;
3. pre/post digests match the exact consumed/produced terms;
4. the transition and causal event set exist in the ledger;
5. branching is retained—two children do not overwrite each other;
6. merging creates a fresh version with all parents and a merge rule;
7. rollback creates another successor pointing to the restored content source; it does not erase history.

If an operation changes the identity criteria declared by the applicable Object/Protocol semantics, it must create a new `ObjectKey` and an explicit `derivedFrom`, `replaces`, `splitsFrom`, or `mergesFrom` relation. Content equality, naming similarity, or a bridge translation alone never proves successor identity. Protocol versions obey the analogous `protocolSuccessor` discipline.

## 15. Executable contact and bounded operational semantics

The following small rewrite fragment is mechanically interpretable after syntax lowering:

```text
rl [open-contact @ engine:v1] :
  B(n) mem(m, { obj(ok,vid,k,s,ports,c,t)
                proto(pk,pvid,L,rs,vs,ss,pol,caps)
                offer(cid,pvid,port,VIEW,ctx,per) REST })
 =>
  B(n-1) mem(m, { obj(ok,vid,k,s,ports,c,t)
                  proto(pk,pvid,L,rs,vs,ss,pol,caps)
                  session(cid,vid,pvid,meet(ports[port],caps),ctx,per,n-1)
                  evidence(fresh,contact,cid,(vid,pvid,VIEW),engine:v1)
                  REST })
 if n > 0 /\ accepts(ports[port],pvid,VIEW,per).

crl [apply-protocol-rule @ P.ruleProv] :
  B(n) mem(m, { session(cid,vid,pvid,C,ctx,per,b)
                obj(ok,vid,k,s,ports,claims,t) protoRule(pvid,r)
                MATCH REST })
 =>
  B(n-cost(r)) mem(m, { session(cid,vid2,pvid,C,ctx,per,b-cost(r))
                        obj(ok,vid2,k,s2,ports,claims2,t2)
                        successor(vid2,vid,ok,tr,r,pvid,per,causes,d0,d1)
                        emitted(r) REST })
 if enabled(r,MATCH,ctx,per,C)
    /\ mode(r)=RUNTIME_MUTATION
    /\ n >= cost(r)
    /\ vid2=freshVersion(ok)
    /\ (s2,claims2,t2)=effect(r,s,claims,t).
```

The bounded executor is:

```text
execute(C0, stepBound, branchBound, termBound):
  frontier := [(C0, [])]
  while frontier nonempty and budgets remain:
    pop (C, trace)
    E := enabledRuleInstances(C)
    CP := criticalPairs(E)              // overlap of matches/read-write sets
    record CP with witnesses and provenance
    successors := policy(C, E, CP)      // branch, prioritize, join, defer, reject
    validate every successor invariant
    enqueue at most branchBound successors of size <= termBound
  return explored traces, terminal states, conflicts, failures, and stop reasons
```

A reproducible contact test fixture consists of an initial configuration, interpreter versions, rule manifests, all three bounds, selection policy, and expected trace/evidence predicates. External effects are replaced by declared effect tokens unless a capability-bound adapter is part of the fixture.

## 16. Invariants and critical-pair conflict evidence

Every committed rewrite must preserve:

1. **Exact-version addressability:** sessions, views, relations, and statuses name exact versions.
2. **Freshness:** successor, evidence, event, relation, and transition identifiers are never reused.
3. **Lineage completeness:** each new version has its declared parent edge(s) and ledger transition.
4. **Locality:** a rule reads/writes only its membrane, declared imports, and negotiated ports.
5. **Capability safety:** actual effects are a subset of granted and declared capabilities.
6. **Mode separation:** semantic writes cannot commit under runtime-only authority.
7. **Provenance closure:** every derived term links to rule/interpreter, inputs, Persona, and transition.
8. **Plurality preservation:** unresolved alternatives are not discarded by an unrecorded choice.
9. **Budget monotonicity:** each non-quiescent rule has positive declared cost; budget never increases except via a separately authorized budget-allocation event.
10. **View purity:** creating a view does not change source terms.

For enabled instances `a` and `b`, a critical-pair candidate exists when their matched terms overlap or `write(a)` intersects `read(b) ∪ write(b)` (symmetrically). Evidence stores the smallest known witness subconfiguration, both instantiated rules and provenance, alternative one-step results, attempted joins within join bound `J`, and one of `joinable-within(J)`, `divergent-within(J)`, `policy-resolved`, or `unknown-after(J)`. Only the first is positive bounded join evidence; none is a global confluence proof.

## 17. Failure modes and falsification plan

Failures are first-class terminal or recoverable terms, including:

- `ParseUnsupported`, `InterpreterMissing`, `ImportUnresolved`;
- `CapabilityDenied`, `ModeViolation`, `MembraneEscape`;
- `StaleObjectVersion`, `StaleProtocolVersion`, `IdentityCollision`;
- `UndeclaredEffect`, `NonPositiveRuleCost`, `BudgetExhausted`;
- `CriticalConflict`, `PolicyInapplicable`, `JoinUnknown`;
- `ProjectionLossUndeclared`, `ViewDependencyStale`;
- `PersonaAuthorityAmbiguous`, `SwitchLoss`, `ReplayViolation`;
- `InvariantViolation`, which prevents commit and preserves the pre-state plus witness.

N09 is falsified, for the tested fragment, by any reproducible fixture showing one of the following:

1. a semantic rule change commits with only `RUNTIME_MUTATION`;
2. a generated view changes its source Object;
3. a successor lacks a fresh exact identifier or complete parent/transition evidence;
4. two conflicting writes commit without critical-pair or declared serialization evidence;
5. an unseen unsupported rule executes through guessed semantics;
6. Persona switching changes historical attribution;
7. budget exhaustion is reported as denial/proof rather than bounded unknown;
8. a bridge drops or reinterprets a term without a loss entry;
9. replay consumes a single-consumption event twice under the same Protocol rule;
10. a rule accesses terms outside its membrane/ports without an explicit bridge/import.

Validation should include property-based generation of configurations, adversarial rule overlaps, lineage branch/merge cases, heterogeneous interpreter pairs, round-trip switches, event reorderings, and mutation-mode confusion. Outcomes are reported with seeds, bounds, versions, and minimal counterexample traces. Passing a finite suite leaves N09 `NOT_VALIDATED` unless an external validation process explicitly changes that state.

## 18. Unsupported Protocols and preserved global OPEN questions

N09 explicitly leaves unsupported any Protocol whose required semantics cannot be finitely represented or safely adapted in the available interpreter set, including Protocols requiring unbounded liveness guarantees, hidden mutable state, undeclared ambient authority, non-replayable external effects without effect tokens, self-modification without semantic versioning, identity by mutable description alone, or conflict resolution that cannot expose its choice/provenance. Such a Protocol may remain opaque and inspectable under `READ`; opacity is not executable support.

The following **global OPEN questions are preserved as OPEN**. N09 offers representations and bounded experiments but does not close them:

1. What constitutes Object identity across transformation, splitting, merging, copying, and replacement beyond a locally declared identity policy?
2. When does Protocol-driven change become a change of the Object, a change of the Protocol, or creation of a new coupled system?
3. Which authority may declare a status canonical, and can canonicality coexist with irreducible plurality?
4. What observation set is sufficient to claim switching fidelity across heterogeneous Protocols?
5. When can two Protocols be called semantically equivalent rather than merely bridged or observationally aligned within a bound?
6. How should incompatible causal, temporal, and identity models be composed without privileging one silently?
7. Which conflict policies are legitimate for concurrent Persons/Personas and Protocols, and who authorizes them?
8. How can negative evidence from bounded execution be communicated without being mistaken for impossibility or proof?
9. What minimum provenance is trustworthy when interpreters, bridges, or authorities themselves are disputed?
10. Which unsupported effects should remain permanently outside executable contact rather than be approximated?

These questions, failure records, alternatives, and unsupported cases remain visible in the configuration or its evidence bundle. Freezing this proposal fixes the text of N09; it does not make N09 normative, complete, preferred, or validated.
