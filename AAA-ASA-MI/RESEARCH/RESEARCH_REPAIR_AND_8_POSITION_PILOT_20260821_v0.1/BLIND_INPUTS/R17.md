## 1. Problem interpretation

R17 rejects the assumption that continuity must be carried by memory, lineage, a persisting substrate, or a reconstructed inner narrative. For a bounded purpose, it models only counterfactual dispositions under a human-declared family of probes: what the system would permit, refuse, answer, defer, or do under interventions.

Its question is:

> Are two resumptions operationally equivalent on a preregistered probe algebra, under explicit schema translations and error bounds, and which new probe would separate them?

Equivalence is version-, protocol-, and tolerance-relative. It is not identity. Probe enrichment may split an old equivalence without making the earlier statement historically false. Hidden constitution is outside scope until it produces an accessible difference; this is the proposal's strongest economy and largest risk.

## 2. Explicit assumptions

R17-A1. A finite, preregistered probe family can capture the distinctions relevant to a bounded resumption decision. Probe selection is a material and challengeable commitment.

R17-A2. Probe and response spaces admit finite-dimensional vector or stochastic-kernel representations. Refusal, unknown, undefined, and disputed outcomes remain explicit response sectors rather than being encoded as zeros.

R17-A3. An instantiation's counterfactual disposition can be represented by a family of response operators, not merely a stored table of visible training answers. Operators must generate held-out compositions and continuations.

R17-R17. Renaming or changing coordinates without changing operational content acts by invertible basis maps. Scientific conclusions should be invariant under that conjugation.

R17-A5. Schema evolution is represented by a partial intertwiner between old and new probe/response spaces. Translation may be exact, one-to-many, or unavailable.

R17-A6. Probe order matters only when the corresponding operators do not commute. Presentation order alone is irrelevant; a nonzero commutator is an exposed causal/interaction assumption.

R17-A7. Historical logs and provenance receipts are retained for audit but are not constitutive of operational equivalence. Lineage and authority remain separate evidence channels outside the equivalence kernel.

R17-A8. An untested probe yields `NOT_TESTED`; an out-of-space probe yields `UNDEFINED`; finite-budget failure yields an interval or `BUDGET_UNKNOWN`.

## 3. Model specification

### 3.1 Protocol version

A protocol version `v` freezes:

- a finite probe space `Q_v`;
- a response space `R_v`, including explicit refusal and uncertainty sectors;
- a probe algebra `A_v` generated by sequential and, where declared, parallel composition;
- a held-out generator subset and composition suite;
- tolerances by probe, never one compensatory global score;
- authorization metadata identifying who may change the protocol.

### 3.2 Response operator family

An instantiation `s` is represented by a family

`T_s^v(a) : Q_v -> R_v` for `a in A_v`.

In a finite pilot these are matrices or stochastic kernels. The family must answer composed held-out probes from the same frozen generators. A dossier that merely stores each expected answer is not a R17 model.

Sequential order sensitivity is witnessed by the commutator

`[T(a), T(b)] = T(a)T(b) - T(b)T(a)`

where the spaces permit composition. Declared-independent probes must commute within tolerance.

### 3.3 Protocol-relative equivalence

For a preregistered probe subalgebra `P subseteq A_v`, two instantiations are `P`-equivalent when:

1. their response and refusal/uncertainty sectors correspond under registered basis maps;
2. every generator and held-out composition in `P` agrees within its own tolerance;
3. the spectra/singular values of declared invariant compositions agree within error;
4. no result depends on runtime identifiers or display labels.

The receipt is per probe and invariant; no total equivalence score is formed. If some probes pass and others fail, the result is a typed partial-equivalence profile.

### 3.4 Coordinate and schema intertwiners

An old-to-new translation is a partial pair `(U_Q, U_R)` satisfying

`T_new(a_new) U_Q = U_R T_old(a_old)`

on its declared domain. Exact equality licenses exact translation; set-valued or subspace-valued images license alternatives; absence returns `UNDEFINED_TRANSLATION`. The historical operator remains archived under its old protocol.

### 3.5 Probe enrichment and historical integrity

If `P1 subset P2`, two instantiations can be equivalent under `P1` and distinguishable under `P2`. R17 records both statements with protocol versions:

`s ~[P1,v1] t` and `s !~[P2,v2] t`.

The second does not overwrite the first. It reports that the older protocol lacked a separating operation.

### 3.6 Translation-cycle holonomy

For scoped accounts A, B, and C with pairwise intertwiners, compose the cycle

`H_A = U_CA U_BC U_AB`.

A justified common coordinate account requires `H_A` to act as identity on the shared tested subspace within tolerance. Non-identity holonomy is a native obstruction: pairwise translations exist, yet one global identification is not licensed.

### 3.7 Output receipt

The receipt contains:

- agreement/failure/untested state for each probe and composition;
- separating probes and minimal distinguishing sequence where found;
- spectra, singular-value intervals, commutators, and holonomy witnesses used;
- protocol, basis, tolerance, and intertwiner versions;
- translation gaps and computation bounds;
- `EQUIVALENCE_SCOPE = protocol/subalgebra only`;
- `IDENTITY = NOT_INFERRED`, `LINEAGE = EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE`, and `AUTHORITY = EXTERNAL_DECISION`.

## 5. Native consequences

N-R17-1. **Basis invariance.** Consistent alpha-renaming or invertible coordinate change conjugates the operators but preserves spectra, commutators, separating probes, and equivalence classification.

N-R17-2. **Prospective distinguishability.** Adding a new probe can split two formerly equivalent resumptions while preserving the truth of their old protocol-relative equivalence.

N-R17-3. **Order diagnosis.** Reordering declared-independent probes changes nothing; a material order effect must be witnessed by a nonzero commutator rather than inferred from presentation order.

N-R17-4. **Pairwise-without-global obstruction.** Pairwise schema translations can all exist while their cycle has non-identity holonomy, mechanically rejecting a single common account on the affected subspace.

N-R17-5. **Memory-content non-sufficiency.** Two copies with the same stored memories can be operationally distinct if a held-out probe separates their response operators; two different internal realizations can remain equivalent if no declared probe separates them.

N-R17-6. **No lineage or authority from equivalence.** Even exact operational equivalence does not produce descent, identity, ownership, or authorization because those predicates are absent from the kernel and explicitly external in the receipt.

## 6. Failure and falsification conditions

R17 must be weakened, merged, or abandoned if:

1. Preregistered held-out compositions cannot be generated without adding their answers as new operator entries.
2. Benign basis changes or alpha-renaming alter spectra, commutators, or equivalence conclusions beyond numerical tolerance.
3. Repeated late cases show that non-interactive historical or constitutive facts control future behavior while remaining invisible to every feasible probe family.
4. Adversarial mimics pass the frozen suite but systematically diverge on natural continuations, and probe enrichment cannot bound this risk.
5. Small plausible changes to the probe algebra reverse central conclusions without an independent human-purpose or information-gain justification.
6. Schema intertwiners cannot distinguish unavailable translation from a zero response or silently reinterpret old operators.
7. The operator representation requires a hidden global state or fixed time step that the stated view denies or fails to expose.
8. Strict-budget execution cannot produce per-probe bounds and instead substitutes falsehood, closure, or a fabricated equivalence.

## 7. Limitations

- R17 is deliberately operationalist and may erase inaccessible but morally or historically important constitution.
- Probe design can encode evaluator preference, cultural expectations, or a desired continuity answer.
- Finite behavioral equivalence is vulnerable to mimicry outside the tested algebra.
- Linear or stochastic-kernel approximations may poorly represent open-ended language and social interaction.
- A response operator describes dispositions, not subjective experience, life, or consciousness.
- Audit logs remain necessary for lineage and accountability even though they are not constitutive of equivalence.
- Human authorization is external, so the model cannot decide who should control a resumption.

## 8. Self-critique

The contrarian economy may be too severe: if a successor acts similarly only because it copied a surface policy while losing relationships, promises, or historical responsibility, R17 may call it equivalent within an impoverished protocol. The model can always propose a richer future probe, which risks making failure indefinitely deferrable. Its finite operator language may also be a testing framework rather than a complete world model. R17 is retained only if preregistered probe enrichment, mimic adversaries, and holonomy tests generate useful distinctions that simpler behavior checklists do not.

## 9. Alternative explanations considered

- **Historical or causal lineage:** better for descent and accountability, but it can preserve a line whose present dispositions have radically changed.
- **Memory-continuity account:** humanly salient, but copied or fabricated memory is not sufficient.
- **Lived-evidence similarity/transport:** preserves historical texture, but geometry and feature choice may encode the answer.
- **Persistent entity:** would assume the continuity question rather than test it.
- **Constraint or rewrite kernel:** can generate admissible histories or structural change, but R17 intentionally tests whether a smaller operational quotient suffices.
- **Pure narrative recognition:** useful for review but vulnerable to post-hoc persuasive coherence.

## 10. Theory contribution

Even if rejected as a whole model, R17 contributes:

1. a prospective, protocol-relative notion of equivalence that can later split without historical overwrite;
2. an alpha-renaming/basis-invariance test stronger than vocabulary matching;
3. a commutator witness separating causal order from presentation order;
4. an intertwiner receipt for exact, one-to-many, and unavailable schema translation;
5. a holonomy obstruction for pairwise-compatible but globally inconsistent accounts;
6. an explicit proof that operational equivalence alone cannot transfer identity, lineage, or authority.

## 11. Testable and implementable contact

### Minimal implementation

Use small rational matrices or finite stochastic kernels for probe generators; enumerate bounded compositions; compute per-probe residuals, singular spectra, commutators, and translator-cycle products. Exact rational arithmetic is preferred for the pilot. Larger cases may return certified norm bounds.

### C1 — Meaning revision without historical overwrite

- Archive the t1 R1 operator and its inputs. It returns `READY@R1` from A and B.
- R2 introduces a C probe. Because historical C is absent, the R2 operator returns `NOT_TESTED/UNKNOWN` for the historical artifact, not `NOT_READY`.
- The R1 result remains valid under protocol R1. A partial R1-to-R2 intertwiner maps A/B and has no domain for C.
- The old operator, protocol, and result may not be overwritten.

### C2 — Interrupted branches and partial merge

- Ignore regenerated runtime IDs. Build partial response operators from the complete X and recoverable Y traces, preserving `NOT_TESTED` entries for corrupt Y material.
- The decision probe separates X (`accept d`) from Y (`reject d`); note probes expose x and the recoverable part of y.
- A proposed consolidation is admissible only relative to a declared merge protocol. It may preserve an unresolved response sector for d rather than force one branch's answer.
- R17 can report behavioral compatibility, conflict, and untested continuations. Causal descent from K is supported only by external checkpoint receipts; persona identity and authority remain not inferred.

### C3 — Continuous change under different sampling

- Represent preference dispositions as a continuous operator path `T(t)` or bounded approximation.
- Endpoint operator difference, convergent path-length bounds, and invariant subspaces are primary claims.
- One versus three threshold crossings is not intrinsic unless threshold, hysteresis, dwell time, and sampling envelope are part of the protocol.
- Under H4 refinement, operator-path invariants must converge; otherwise R17 reports sampling dependence and withdraws the event claim.

### C4 — Dependency and schema change

- Retain the old `owner` probe/response space and historical operator.
- Exact translations use an intertwiner. One-to-many owner-to-principals mapping uses a subspace- or set-valued image and returns alternatives rather than selecting one principal.
- The third class has no intertwiner and yields `UNDEFINED_TRANSLATION`.
- A downstream result transports only where the intertwining equation commutes; otherwise the affected consequence is unsupported under the new schema.

### C5 — Local agreement without a justified global account

- Keep three scoped probe algebras and their pairwise intertwiners.
- Pairwise reconciliations remain supported on their tested subspaces.
- Compute the translator-cycle holonomy. If it is non-identity, no single common coordinate account is justified on the affected subspace, despite pairwise success.
- If several global intertwiners satisfy all tested equations, preserve them as alternatives. Any result reversal after translator mutation must cite the changed cycle product.

### C6 — Copy, divergence, and later consolidation

- Immediately after restoration, the two successors may be equivalent under the checkpoint protocol even with stable IDs removed.
- Divergence is recorded when a separating probe or composition appears. Exchanged memories affect equivalence only if they change response operators.
- Consolidation is evaluated as a new operator family against the frozen protocol; it does not retroactively fuse earlier histories.
- Strongest claims are exact or partial operational equivalence at named protocol versions. Numerical sameness, causal descent, sole continuation, and authority inheritance are outside the kernel and remain not established unless separate evidence supports them.

## 12. What would change the conclusion

R17 should be strengthened if held-out compositions, basis invariance, probe enrichment, and holonomy tests work prospectively and discriminate resumptions with fewer arbitrary commitments than history-heavy alternatives.

It should be retained only as an evaluation/interface layer if it usefully measures operational equivalence but cannot constitute a broader world or history.

It should be merged with a lineage or historical-evidence model if operational profiles are useful but repeatedly omit responsibility-relevant facts.

It should be abandoned if adversarial mimicry dominates, feasible probe sets cannot capture continuity-relevant distinctions, coordinate invariance fails, or its operators reduce to a post-result lookup table.
