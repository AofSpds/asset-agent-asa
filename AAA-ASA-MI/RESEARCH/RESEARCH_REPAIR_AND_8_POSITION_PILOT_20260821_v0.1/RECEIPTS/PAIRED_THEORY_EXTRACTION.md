# Paired Theory Extraction: Track A and Track B

## 1. Scope and reading discipline

This review extracts theory-bearing material from the eight original candidate files, the research maps `02`–`04`, and the frozen held-out set. It does not use independent evaluation receipts or an alias key. It does not select a winner, aggregate a score, infer validation from repetition, or turn any research proposition into Owner intent.

“Pre-reveal” below means only that a Track B claim appears in its frozen block before exposure to the research maps. It does not mean the claim was uncued: the neutral contract and C1–C6 already supplied shared problem pressures. Track A positions were written with the research basis visible, so their overlap with `02`–`04` is not evidence of independent rediscovery.

The eight positions do not reduce to one common kernel. Their primary objects differ:

| Position | Primary theoretical object | What it deliberately does not make constitutive |
|---|---|---|
| A1 VCHC | Versioned family of admissible possible histories | Persistent object, scalar identity, automatic authority |
| A2 TRAC | Test-indexed behavior trees and simulations | Provenance or hidden constitution by default |
| A3 LOT | Transport of versioned lived-orientation evidence | Numerical identity or governance authority |
| A4 CIOM | Counterfactual response operators under a probe algebra | Lineage, authorship, or authority |
| B1 ALC | Scoped, provenance-bearing claim and lineage warrants | A single enduring persona fact |
| B2 RRE | Robust future-facing resumption envelope | Genealogical sameness as the practical certificate |
| B3 BTV | Uncertain trajectory families inside a viability envelope | Snapshot sameness or authorization |
| B4 PCSC | Proof-carrying succession constitution | Unversioned or default `same_persona` inference |

These are incompatible commitments, not interchangeable implementations. A1 and B1 ask what history or evidence warrants; A2 and A4 ask what interventions can distinguish; A3 asks what lived evidence is carried; B3 asks whether a viable route exists; B2 asks whether a role and its protected commitments can be resumed; B4 asks what a constitution licenses.

## 2. Candidate-by-candidate extraction

### A1 — VCHC: Versioned Constraint-History Calculus

- **Useful concept:** A world query ranges over a nonempty family of typed, version-scoped possible histories. `THEN` evaluates frozen historical semantics; `NOW` evaluates only licensed partial translations. Merge is a constrained product over a shared prefix, not state union.
- **Key distinction:** Semantic status (`FORCED`, `IMPOSSIBLE`, `ALTERNATIVE`, `UNDEFINED`, `DISPUTED`, `NOT_PROVEN`) is separate from a purpose-relative continuity profile, and every continuity profile is separate from identity and authority.
- **Negative result:** With rules fixed, evidence removal can only preserve or enlarge the admissible-history family; it cannot create a new forced conclusion. A translation hole cannot create a target fact, and a merge cannot invent an unsupported branch fact.
- **Counterexample retained:** A refinement-invariant continuous phenomenon that cannot be represented by the declared finite or symbolic history language defeats the proposed foundation. Equally plausible type or factorization choices that reverse results expose constraint-language capture.
- **Candidate experiment:** Corrupt or remove evidence while freezing rules and verify monotone weakening; mutate a partial translator into a default-total translator and identify the first fabricated conclusion; run an isomorphic payload swap; test merge non-invention over a corrupt branch.
- **Failure condition:** Monotonicity violation, conclusion-specific constraints after payload swaps, repeated factorization reversals without a selection rule, inability to reproduce a continuous invariant without a post-hoc threshold, or systematic intractability that changes status under compression.
- **Possible reformulation:** Narrow VCHC to a semantic reconstruction and audit layer if it cannot generate continuous or interactive consequences; couple it to a separately justified dynamics model rather than claiming it is the whole substrate.
- **Main hidden commitment:** The vocabulary, factorization, constraint set, translators, and continuity profile can be chosen independently of the desired answer and are expressive enough to bound the relevant possibilities.
- **Likely applicability boundary:** Strongest for bounded, typed, versioned evidence, schema change, branch/merge, and explicit uncertainty. Weak for open-ended embodied dynamics, lived experience, legitimacy, and governance unless those are imported as constraints.

### A2 — TRAC: Test-Relative Affordance Coalgebra

- **Useful concept:** A state is a behavior position characterized by its possible intervention/response tree. Test-relative bisimulation gives scoped sameness; directional simulation gives scoped behavioral continuance; a shortest distinguishing trace supplies an operational witness.
- **Key distinction:** Descent evidence, behavioral continuance, and test-relative sameness are three different claims. Protocol adapters bound what can be compared across versions.
- **Negative result:** Identical histories do not force behavioral equivalence, and different histories do not force behavioral distinction. Enlarging a test set may split an equivalence class but cannot merge states already distinguished by the smaller test set.
- **Counterexample retained:** Two systems may remain indistinguishable under every feasible test yet differ in promise origin, consent, authorship, or authorization. If that difference is constitutive for the task, operational equivalence is insufficient.
- **Candidate experiment:** Add one preregistered held-out test and verify partition refinement plus a shortest separator. Compare same-history/different-refusal and different-history/same-behavior pairs. Then add provenance-inspection actions and attribute any split to test-vocabulary expansion.
- **Failure condition:** Normatively important hidden-state distinctions recur, partitions fail to stabilize with depth, test selection encodes the conclusion, continuous path properties matter but are interaction-invisible, or human reviewers require history that behavioral witnesses cannot supply.
- **Possible reformulation:** Retain TRAC as an operational qualification or interface layer alongside a history, normative, or governance account.
- **Main hidden commitment:** A finite, governed action/observation vocabulary and depth expose every distinction relevant to the scoped resumption decision; what remains inaccessible may be quotiented away for that purpose.
- **Likely applicability boundary:** Strongest for interactive competence, refusal, capability, and counterfactual response under bounded protocols. Weak for inaccessible historical participation, moral responsibility, subjective continuity, and continuous internal dynamics.

### A3 — LOT: Lived-Orientation Transport

- **Useful concept:** Versioned evidence atoms are encoded into provisional channels—memory, disposition, commitment, relationship-position, and anticipation—and compared by unbalanced transport. The output is a non-scalar certificate of matched, lost, novel, untranslatable, disputed, and unsupported evidence.
- **Key distinction:** Content similarity is separated from supported inheritance; lived-orientation evidence is separated from numerical identity and authority; channel-specific conflicts cannot be compensated away by one aggregate score.
- **Negative result:** Common checkpoint evidence copied into two branches cannot become two independent units of ancestry after merge. A coherent memory without a valid source receipt remains novel or unsupported. Opposed commitments cannot disappear through cost-free averaging.
- **Counterexample retained:** Plausible encoders, channel choices, or transport costs may reverse the central certificate. A forged or culturally biased evidence geometry can make technical distance masquerade as lived continuity.
- **Candidate experiment:** Inject an accurate but unsupported autobiographical episode; copy one checkpoint unit into two branches and merge; perturb independently defensible costs; test opposed commitments; refine sampling of a continuous orientation path and require convergence of endpoint/action bounds.
- **Failure condition:** Encoder or cost sensitivity dominates, a simple memory/behavior baseline matches every discriminator, source-capacity accounting double-counts or suppresses legitimate independent evidence, false memories are marked inherited, channels proliferate after each case, or humans cannot trace the certificate.
- **Possible reformulation:** Treat LOT as a human-facing continuity-evidence layer, adding independent dynamics and governance. Abandon it if the geometry supplies narrative decoration but no discriminator beyond provenance bookkeeping.
- **Main hidden commitment:** A finite channel decomposition and defensible cost geometry approximate lived orientation without encoding a preferred cultural or evaluator answer; provenance receipts are sufficiently trustworthy to control inheritance claims.
- **Likely applicability boundary:** Strongest for reviewable evidence about memories, commitments, relationships, dispositions, and semantic revision. Weak for unrecorded phenomenology, open-ended world dynamics, source-authentication, and legitimate authority.

### A4 — CIOM: Counterfactual Invariant Operator Model

- **Useful concept:** A preregistered probe algebra acts through response operators. Equivalence is protocol-relative; partial intertwiners handle schema change; commutators distinguish causal order from presentation order; translation-cycle holonomy obstructs an unjustified global account.
- **Key distinction:** Old equivalence under a smaller probe algebra can remain historically true after a new probe separates the systems. Operational equivalence remains separate from lineage and authority.
- **Negative result:** Memory equality is neither necessary nor sufficient for operational equivalence. Pairwise translations do not imply a global coordinate account. Exact operational equivalence cannot itself produce descent, authorship, identity, or authorization.
- **Counterexample retained:** An adversarial mimic may pass the finite algebra and diverge on natural continuations. A historically or morally constitutive fact may remain invisible to every feasible probe.
- **Candidate experiment:** Apply invertible basis changes; require held-out compositions to be generated rather than stored; enrich the probe algebra with a separator; run a nonidentity translator cycle; compare memory twins with different held-out dispositions.
- **Failure condition:** Basis changes alter conclusions, held-out compositions require lookup insertion, mimicry remains unbounded, small plausible probe changes reverse results without justification, inaccessible facts repeatedly control the decision, or the implementation smuggles in a hidden global state/time step.
- **Possible reformulation:** Use CIOM as an interface/evaluation layer paired with lineage and governance; retain operator paths only where continuous behavior is genuinely captured.
- **Main hidden commitment:** The chosen finite operator representation has real compositional structure and the probe algebra covers the relevant counterfactual dispositions rather than merely rehearsing expected answers.
- **Likely applicability boundary:** Strongest for bounded operational protocols, compositional tests, order effects, and schema-coordinate invariance. Weak for historical responsibility, tacit social meaning, open-ended interaction, phenomenology, and authorization.

### B1 — ALC: Accountable Lineage Calculus

- **Useful concept:** A typed evidence graph supports claims with explicit supporting and opposing proof paths. Continuity is a five-component profile—descent, memory transfer, commitment transfer, behavioral resemblance, and authorization—rather than one Boolean.
- **Key distinction:** Historical relationship warrant is different from what a resumed process may legitimately claim or do. Raw record growth is monotone, while conclusions can vary by explicit interpretive context. Timeout is separate from every evidential status.
- **Negative result:** Snapshot identity does not establish descent; missing evidence does not become negative without a closed-world rule; merge does not settle contradiction or fuse histories; pairwise reconciliation does not license a global account.
- **Counterexample retained:** Two current snapshots are indistinguishable, but only one has a verified derivation from checkpoint K. Conversely, a future non-decomposable continuity variable could predict justified resumption after every profile component is controlled.
- **Candidate experiment:** B1-T pairs snapshot-matched candidates with different derivation evidence and separately supplies an inconsistent translator cycle. Relabeling, record-order permutation, and regenerated identifiers must leave the claim profile unchanged.
- **Failure condition:** Evidence loss strengthens an undeclared claim, contradiction/absence/corruption/timeout collapse, a translator cycle is globalized, merge silently discards conflict, or a reproducible non-decomposable continuity fact outpredicts the five components.
- **Possible reformulation:** Narrow the graph to an epistemic lineage/audit system if provenance ceases to matter once commitments and conduct are controlled, or if the graph cannot yield consequences beyond bookkeeping.
- **Main hidden commitment:** Decision-relevant continuity is decomposable into finite scoped claims whose sources, rebuttals, and proof paths can be authenticated and meaningfully reviewed.
- **Likely applicability boundary:** Strongest for historical warrant, lineage, source-sensitive claims, contradiction, and sourced authority. Weak for continuous dynamics, subjective experience, urgent action under unresolved evidence, and facts that resist finite claim representation.

### B2 — RRE: Robust Resumption Envelope

- **Useful concept:** A review capsule combines versioned evidence, feasible histories, protected commitments, counterfactual probes, tolerances, and amendment procedures. It yields lower/upper bounds for strong, possible, failed, or unresolved operational resumption.
- **Key distinction:** Operational role continuation is separate from genealogical descent and external authority. Accountable amendment differs from silent drift or a protected-commitment breach.
- **Negative result:** An exact descendant can fail operational continuation, while an unrelated implementation can qualify operationally. Several successors may qualify, and continuity can be non-transitive across domains or tolerances.
- **Counterexample retained:** A verified descendant deliberately violates a protected commitment; a non-descendant preserves every commitment and passes held-out interactions. The reverse counterexample is probe gaming: a system passes the review envelope but fails natural interaction.
- **Candidate experiment:** Match those two candidates and compare the operational certificate, the separate descent claim, and prospectively registered human resumption/trust judgments against ID/snapshot baselines. Add pairwise-feasible but jointly infeasible translators and require a minimal conflict core.
- **Failure condition:** Protected commitments cannot be elicited stably, probes do not generalize, reviewer power determines the answer without an accountable rule, adversarial systems game the envelope, or disclosed human judgments reliably prefer the breached descendant and the certificate adds no predictive value.
- **Possible reformulation:** The post-reveal audit already narrows B2 from a possible complete practical account to an operational-continuance module that must not erase history, lineage, or governance.
- **Main hidden commitment:** A legitimate group can declare stable non-tradeable commitments, tolerances, probes, and amendment rules that predict future human trust rather than simply encode it after the fact.
- **Likely applicability boundary:** Strongest for future-facing role resumption, commitments, competence, and accountable change in a bounded domain. Weak for origin, lived participation, authorship, metaphysical sameness, and authority selection.

### B3 — BTV: Belief-Tube Viability

- **Useful concept:** Partial noisy observations define a set of compatible trajectories under a versioned differential inclusion. Continuity can be existential (`possibly continuous`) or robust, and human compatibility is indexed by an explicit viability envelope.
- **Key distinction:** A compatible route, a route that remains viable, shared descent, numerical sameness, and authorization are five different questions. Snapshot proximity is neither necessary nor sufficient for a viable trajectory.
- **Negative result:** Under noncontracting uncertainty and no new evidence, a longer interruption cannot strengthen robust continuity. New consistent evidence shrinks the compatible path set and cannot create a path already proved impossible under the same model. Information consolidation does not fuse lived intervals.
- **Counterexample retained:** Abrupt symbolic or institutional change may not fit a continuous approximation. Two candidates can be identical in every declared coordinate and counterfactual probe yet receive stable human distinctions that the state representation cannot express.
- **Candidate experiment:** Hold endpoints fixed while varying gap duration, observation density, and admissible dynamics; test coordinate-change invariance; inject one exogenous jump; swap viability policies; compare possible versus robust reachability with sound inner/outer bounds.
- **Failure condition:** Coordinate transformations or benign refinement reverse invariants, ordinary accepted change routinely violates the dynamics, unacceptable resumptions lie robustly inside the envelope, accepted ones remain unreachable, state variables expand into a case list, or computation remains indeterminate.
- **Possible reformulation:** Retain B3 as a continuous diagnostic if discrete institutional or subpropositional factors dominate; yield the primary continuity judgment to another account when no stable envelope can be justified.
- **Main hidden commitment:** A finite state, set-valued dynamics, metric, and policy envelope can represent human-relevant change without converting contested values into arbitrary geometry; path existence is meaningful continuity evidence.
- **Likely applicability boundary:** Strongest for continuous or uncertain change, interruption, sampling, disturbance, and viability. Weak for abrupt normative acts, origin-sensitive obligation, tacit meaning, and authority unless explicitly imported.

### B4 — PCSC: Proof-Carrying Succession Constitution

- **Useful concept:** A paraconsistent, versioned succession constitution gives separate proof obligations for descent, content carriage, policy-relative continuation, authorization, and sameness. `same_persona` has no default introduction rule; translations themselves require warrants.
- **Key distinction:** Supported-only, opposed-only, both, and neither are distinct. Descriptive evidence is separated from licensed action, and procedural legitimacy is separated from lived or behavioral authenticity.
- **Negative result:** No proof is not proof of negation. Adding valid opposition does not erase positive support. Copied memory proves content carriage, not authorship, lived participation, sameness, or authority. Pairwise translator warrants do not imply global coherence.
- **Counterexample retained:** A procedurally valid but behaviorally hollow successor may satisfy the constitution; an authentic but poorly documented continuation may fail it. Tacit, affective, or embodied reasons may resist reusable proposition-level policy.
- **Candidate experiment:** Add contradictory evidence and require `supported-only` to become `both`; rename vocabulary and require alpha-equivalent certificates; mutate one translator and trace the exact delta; run held-out human adjudications against reusable policies; vary governance policy while freezing substrate facts.
- **Failure condition:** The engine derives beyond displayed premises, contradiction causes deletion or explosion, incoherent translators still globalize, budget exhaustion becomes rejection, policies routinely license hollow successors, or stable reviewer reasons require a growing list of case-specific clauses.
- **Possible reformulation:** Keep B4 as a governance and audit layer, letting a behavioral, lived-orientation, or dynamical account supply the primary continuity evidence when reasons are continuous or subpropositional.
- **Main hidden commitment:** Decision-relevant human reasons can be expressed as stable, reusable, contestable policies, and the authority that promulgates or changes those policies is itself legitimate and inspectable.
- **Likely applicability boundary:** Strongest for obligation, authorization, institutional adjudication, contestable claims, and policy revision. Weak for phenomenology, continuous dynamics, tacit relationship meaning, and behavioral authenticity not captured in premises.

### 4.4 Overlap that does not imply truth

The most repeated results are also the most fixture- and basis-exposed: non-overwrite follows C1; branch plurality follows C2/C6; sampling caution follows C3; partial translation follows C4; local-without-global follows C5; relabeling invariance follows the metamorphic contract. Repetition therefore shows responsiveness to the same problem, not independent empirical confirmation.

Even genuine pre-reveal overlap can result from shared task structure or familiar formal priors. It does not establish that histories, tests, transport geometry, viability dynamics, or constitutions are what a Persona is. Nor does the common output `IDENTITY = NOT_INFERRED` make the accounts compatible: one withholds identity because histories remain alternative, another because identity is absent from the behavior kernel, another because transport is only evidence, and another because no constitutional rule introduces it.

### 5.1 Minimum cross-account readout

Every experiment should return separate fields rather than a common score:

1. historical descent and source provenance;
2. content accuracy and acquisition path;
3. observed and counterfactual behavior;
4. continuous/path viability, including local versus whole-path status;
5. lived-orientation evidence by channel, with conflicts noncompensatory;
6. commitment or obligation status;
7. authorization and policy source;
8. identity/sameness only if the candidate has an explicit rule for it;
9. unknown, disputed, untranslatable, out-of-scope, and budget states;
10. the exact constraint, probe, cost, coordinate, translator, or policy on which the conclusion depends.

This readout does not synthesize the theories. It keeps their outputs comparable while preserving the possibility that they answer different questions.

## 6. Extraction headlines

- The deepest live conflict is constitutive: warranted history, accessible behavior, viable trajectory, transported lived orientation, operational role, and legitimate authority are not the same target.
- Track B shows four clear pre-reveal families—lineage, operational commitments, continuous viability, and governance—plus partial human-lived concerns; A3 supplies the only fully explicit lived-orientation transport account. The neutral contract itself cued many shared distinctions.
- Track A contributes more architecture-specific machinery; its conceptual alignment with the maps is exposure-dependent and cannot count as independent confirmation.
- The strongest negative results concern non-transfer: memory does not transfer descent, behavior does not transfer obligation, descent does not transfer authority, local translation does not transfer global coherence, and sampling does not manufacture an intrinsic event.
- U1, U2, U3, U5, and U6, supplemented by gap-duration, path-versus-endpoint, and cross-channel tradeoff tests, can separate the five account families without selecting a canonical theory.
