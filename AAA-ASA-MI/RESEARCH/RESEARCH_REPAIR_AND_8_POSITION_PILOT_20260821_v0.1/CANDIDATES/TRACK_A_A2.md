## 1. Problem interpretation

The prevailing research basis may overestimate what can be learned from records, lineage, and ontological reconstruction. A human-compatible Persona is encountered through what it can perceive, refuse, promise, revise, explain, and do under interaction. A perfect archive can preserve a dead or incapable system; conversely, two differently implemented systems may sustain the same relevant commitments and affordances.

TRAC therefore models a world as an **open field of possible interventions, responses, and successor capabilities**. A state is characterized by its behavior tree under a declared test vocabulary. Continuity and sameness are test-relative simulation/bisimulation results, not history or identifier facts. Provenance constrains which state may have occurred but does not constitute the state by default.

Contrarian claim: relation/history/identity vocabulary may be useful human or audit projections, while counterfactual interactive capability is the more discriminating low-level substrate for some Persona questions.

## 2. Explicit assumptions

`A2.1 OPERATIONAL_DISCERNIBILITY` — Within declared scope, two states that no permitted test can distinguish are equivalent for that scope. This does not assert metaphysical identity.

`A2.2 OPEN_TRANSITION_SEMANTICS` — A state exposes possible action/response successors, including explicit refusal and unknown/unexplored outcomes.

`A2.3 BOUNDED_TEST_SCOPE` — Computation uses a frozen test set `T` and depth/budget `k`; equivalence is always indexed by them.

`A2.4 TEST_VOCABULARY_IS_A_COMMITMENT` — Action/observation alphabets and what counts as a response can encode ontology or values; they must be exposed and mutated.

`A2.5 HISTORY_IS_EVIDENCE_NOT_ESSENCE` — Logs/checkpoints narrow the possible current behavior positions but do not prove identity or continuity without a behavioral or separately justified normative criterion.

`A2.6 PARTIAL_PROTOCOL_MIGRATION` — Semantic/schema revision uses partial adapters between old and new actions/observations; old traces remain under their old protocol.

`A2.7 COMPOSITION_REQUIRES_A_CONTRACT` — Branch consolidation is a new behavior position constructed under an explicit conflict/refusal/choice contract, never raw memory union.

## 3. Model specification

For semantic/protocol version `v`, define an open coalgebra

`C_v = (S_v, A_v, O_v, δ_v, obs_v)` where:

- `S_v` is a set of behavior positions, not persistent identities.
- `A_v` is the permitted intervention/action alphabet.
- `O_v` contains ordinary observations plus `REFUSED`, `UNKNOWN`, `CONFLICT`, and `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`.
- `δ_v(s,a) ⊆ O_v × S_v` gives all currently admitted response/successor pairs.
- `obs_v(s)` gives passive observations if the protocol permits them.

`Beh^k_T(s)` is the finite observation tree generated by all tests in frozen set `T` to depth/budget `k`.

- `s ≡_{T,k} t` iff no test in `T` through depth `k` distinguishes their observation trees.
- `s ⪯_{T,k} t` iff `t` simulates all obligations/capabilities declared continuity-relevant for `s`; this is directional continuance, not identity.
- `Distinguish(s,t)` returns a shortest test trace when one exists.

Protocol revision:

- `α_vw : (A_v,O_v) ⇀ P(A_w,O_w)` is a partial, possibly one-to-many adapter.
- Old trace queries use `C_v`; current comparison uses only the translated subprotocol. Unmapped behavior remains outside the comparison, not false.

Evidence and lineage:

- A trace/checkpoint log defines a set `Consistent(E) ⊆ S_v`.
- Missing/corrupt logs enlarge this set.
- A claimed transition between branches requires a trace witness; behavioral equivalence alone does not establish descent, and descent alone does not establish behavioral continuity.

Merge/consolidation:

- `M_G(x,y)` constructs a new state under a frozen composition contract `G` specifying precedence, nondeterminism, refusal, conflict exposure, and obligations.
- If `G` cannot resolve incompatible behaviors, both branches remain possible or the merged action returns `CONFLICT`; no hidden winner is selected.

## 5. Native consequences and preregistration

### Derived consequences

`NC-A2-1 TEST-REFINEMENT MONOTONICITY` — If `T ⊆ T'` and depth is unchanged, adding tests can split an equivalence class but cannot merge two states already distinguished by `T`.

`NC-A2-2 HISTORY/BEHAVIOR SEPARATION` — Identical histories do not force equivalence when a test distinguishes current behavior; different histories do not force distinction when all frozen tests agree.

`NC-A2-3 LABEL INVARIANCE` — Bijections over state/action/observation labels preserve equivalence, simulation, and shortest-witness structure.

`NC-A2-4 PARTIAL-ADAPTER BOUND` — A protocol migration can establish equivalence only on the adapter's mapped subprotocol. It cannot license claims over unmapped actions.

### Prospective native-test registration

`PRIMARY_NAT-A2` — Add one held-out discriminating test to an existing suite. The partition induced by `≡_{T,k}` must refine or remain equal; it must never coarsen. A shortest distinguishing trace must explain every split.

`COUNTERINTUITIVE_NAT-A2` — Two successors with different memories/provenance but identical frozen behavior trees will be equivalent for `(T,k)`. Two successors with identical checkpoint/history but a different refusal or obligation response will be distinct. This result must survive H1/H3.

`ASSUMPTION_MUTATION` — Expand `T` with provenance-inspection actions. Previously equivalent states may split; the change must be attributed to A2.4, not reported as an intrinsic identity discovery.

`UNIQUE_WEAK_REGIME` — If two behaviorally indistinguishable states must remain different for human governance because of hidden provenance, consent, or normative commitment, TRAC deliberately collapses a target-relevant distinction. That is a predeclared failure, not a context to add after the fact.

## 6. Failure/falsification conditions

TRAC must be materially redesigned, merged, or abandoned as a general Persona substrate if:

1. **Normative hidden-state failure:** repeated held-out cases require two states with identical behavior under every implementable test to remain distinct because provenance, consent, promise origin, or authority is constitutive. TRAC then needs a provenance/normative kernel and loses its foundational sufficiency claim.
2. **Test answer-encoding:** advertised equivalence or distinction disappears under alpha-renaming/payload swap or requires fixture-specific tests. Then its native consequence is evaluator construction.
3. **Depth instability:** conclusions oscillate without stabilization as `k` increases, so bounded equivalence supplies no useful continuity surface. It may remain a local testing tool only.
4. **Continuous-opacity failure:** C3-like path properties matter but no permitted interaction can recover refinement-invariant behavior without importing a continuous dynamics model. TRAC is then insufficient for that domain.
5. **Open-world intractability:** unknown successors dominate and every meaningful query remains unresolved under realistic budget. Budget exhaustion stays explicit but defeats practical use.
6. **Human-purpose failure:** human reviewers consistently require lineage/history explanations that behavioral witnesses cannot supply or trust. A formally correct partition would still fail the stated purpose.

## 7. Limitations

- Test vocabulary can be an anthropocentric or evaluator-authored hidden ontology.
- Finite bisimulation approximations can miss delayed divergence.
- Behavioral equivalence can erase morally, legally, or historically material distinctions.
- It is weak at explaining continuous internal dynamics unless those dynamics affect observations.
- Provenance is treated as evidence by default, which directly conflicts with views where history constitutes the Persona.
- Human familiarity and subjective continuity are not reducible to test success without further evidence.

## 8. Self-critique

TRAC may be a rigorous qualification/continuity test layer rather than the world substrate sought by ASA-MI. Its contrarian strength is also its greatest risk: it can declare two systems equivalent while a human considers them importantly different. The phrase “all permitted tests” hides a governance decision over who may test what. If the test suite is broad enough to include every history/provenance query, the model may merely re-import the structures it claims to avoid.

## 9. Alternative explanations considered

- **Versioned history/constraint model:** stronger historical reconstruction and explicit non-closure; may encode answers in constraints and treat interaction as secondary.
- **Lineage/succession model:** directly represents descent and fork/merge; descent may not preserve capability or commitments.
- **Relational constitution:** may explain participant roles and co-constitution; risks unfalsifiable relation expansion or graph/representation leakage.
- **Continuous viability field:** captures smooth change and self-maintenance; observation and semantic revision remain separate problems.
- **Persistent object:** human-familiar and cheap, but assumes what copy/divergence cases should test.
- **Model ecology:** TRAC may be one operational lens alongside history, normative, and continuous kernels rather than a universal foundation.

## 10. Theory contribution

TRAC contributes a sharp three-way separation:

1. **descent evidence** from logs/checkpoints,
2. **behavioral continuance** as directional simulation,
3. **test-relative sameness** as bisimulation.

It supplies a negative test for current research: if a proposed Persona model cannot say which intervention would distinguish claimed successors, its continuity language may be purely narrative. Conversely, TRAC exposes where operational equivalence is too weak because human governance requires provenance- or norm-sensitive distinction.

## 11. Testable/implementable contact

Finite pilot implementation:

1. Encode a versioned labelled transition system with explicit `REFUSED/UNKNOWN/CONFLICT` outcomes.
2. Build depth-`k` behavior trees under a seeded, frozen test generator.
3. Use partition refinement to compute `≡_{T,k}` and simulation checking for `⪯_{T,k}`.
4. Emit shortest distinguishing traces.
5. Validate partial protocol adapters and merge constructor `M_G`.

```text
partition = all_states
for test in frozen_tests:
  partition = split(partition, outcome_tree(test, depth=k))
assert partition_only_refines(previous_partition)
```

Prospective artifacts: exact LTS, test generator/seed, protocol adapter, merge contract, depth/budget, result partition, witnesses, and hashes. Run C1–C6 plus H1–H7 and at least two unseen isomorphic cases.

## 12. What would change the conclusion

- Stable partitions across increasing `k`, held-out tests, and label/payload mutations would strengthen TRAC as an operational substrate candidate.
- Frequent provenance/normative counterexamples would narrow it to a test layer and favor merger with a history or governance model.
- A compact non-operational model that predicts every shortest distinguishing witness more cheaply would weaken TRAC's necessity.
- Evidence that human-recognized continuity tracks behavior more reliably than IDs/memory/history would strengthen its contrarian thesis.
- Evidence that history or consent origin matters even under complete behavioral equivalence would reject the thesis as a general Persona account.

### C1

### C2

The X and partial-Y logs constrain sets of behavior positions. Missing Y observations enlarge its set. A merge under `G` preserves decision conflict as nondeterministic/conflict behavior unless a declared rule resolves it. Regenerated IDs prove nothing; lineage needs a transition witness, and authority is outside behavioral inference unless tested explicitly.

### C3

One-versus-three crossings are observer/test-resolution results. TRAC licenses only behavior exposed by the frozen sampling protocol. It does not invent a path-level event invariant; without a continuous observation contract, the invariant is `NOT_PROVEN/OUT_OF_SCOPE`. This is a declared weak regime.

### C4

The old single-owner behavior remains replayable. Exact and one-to-many adapters establish comparison only over mapped actions; the third class is outside the adapter. Any downstream action requiring that class returns unknown/refusal rather than a fabricated principals set.

### C5

Each pairwise adapter may support a local bisimulation. Non-composable adapters prevent a global quotient/behavior position; local equivalences remain valid, but no global account is licensed. Changing one adapter must explain any partition reversal.

### C6

The common checkpoint constrains an initial behavior prefix but does not establish sameness. Divergence is reported by shortest distinguishing tests. Later memory consolidation affects equivalence only if it changes observable obligations/capabilities; otherwise the successors remain test-equivalent but not proven identical or authority-equivalent.
