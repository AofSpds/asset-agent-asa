## Interim findings

1. TOP-1 remains regime-sensitive: AHCK wins E1/E5; TRCC wins E2/E4/E6; CCRA wins E3. No universal winner has emerged.
2. E2 and E4 are strongly aligned: both reward witnessed/generative structural change, with TRCC and WLRF in the top two. This is expected conceptual correlation, not independent replication of the same criterion.
3. E5 is the strongest adversary to the rewrite family: TRCC and WLRF fall to fifth/sixth because their causal and lineage results are event/refinement sensitive under continuous change.
4. E6 does not merely reproduce E2: CCRA and CCP rise because vocabulary removal, partial translation, and historical currentization are first-class, while TRCC retains first place through exact trace replay plus substantive dynamics.
5. AHCK is currently the most regime-stable generalist by score dispersion; TRCC/CCRA/WLRF/CCP/LPCW show stronger specialist response profiles.
6. All six candidates remain Pareto non-dominated across E1–E6. No candidate is weakly worse than another on all six regimes.
7. Current ecological phenotypes are provisional:
   - AHCK: regime-stable admissible-history generalist.
   - TRCC: high-power generative/ontogenesis + history-revision specialist, vulnerable to continuity/refinement.
   - WLRF: compact rewrite specialist, stronger implementation economy than TRCC but weaker context/continuity.
   - CCRA: rich contextual/non-global + semantic-evolution specialist with large commitment surface.
   - CCP: conservative contextual/non-closure/revision specialist, weaker native dynamics.
   - LPCW: lean anti-global/local-time specialist, strongly cover-dependent.
8. Common-core scores move between evaluators, indicating lens halo in addition to the intended 30% regime bias. Do not modify E7–E9 mid-experiment. After E1–E9 complete, perform fixed-common-core normalization as a secondary analysis.
